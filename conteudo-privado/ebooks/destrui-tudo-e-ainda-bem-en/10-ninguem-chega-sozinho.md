# Nobody gets there alone

> Learning alone is possible. It just isn't the cheapest route in time, or in mistakes.

A lot of people think they have to work everything out on their own. It's an understandable pride, and it's almost always expensive. Learning in a group, with clear rules and people to talk to, saves time and mistakes. That's why MoreThanMoney exists.

This chapter describes the ecosystem we've built. I'll describe it as it is, without promises. There are no guaranteed results. There's work, discipline, and a team alongside you.

## What exists, in plain language

The ecosystem has several parts. You don't need all of them. Some are for people just starting out, others for people who already have written rules and want tools.

| Part | Who it's for | What it is, without promises |
|---|---|---|
| Member | Anyone starting out who wants a method | The entry level: content, community and support to learn the basics |
| Premium | Anyone who wants more guidance | A level with a trial period, with trading ideas, strategies and team analysis. It is not a guarantee of profit |

| MTM Auto | Anyone who already understands what they are copying | The automatic part of the ecosystem, connected to accounts that are yours. Copying someone else's trades doesn't eliminate risk |
| MTM Funded | Anyone who wants to train on simulated accounts | Simulated accounts, with Performance Sponsorship (Patrocínio de Desempenho) as recognition. It is not a prop firm |

Look at the second column. It isn't a ladder of success where you climb rung by rung until you get rich. It's a set of tools, and each one serves a different phase.

## What MTM Funded is, and what it isn't

This is the point where people get most confused, so I'll be direct.

MTM Funded accounts are simulated. They don't use real money from MTM, or from anyone else. They're accounts for training rules, measuring discipline and learning to follow a plan without the pressure of real money.

Performance Sponsorship is a form of recognition: if a student shows discipline in their training, they can receive support to open their own account with a regulated broker, PU Prime. The account belongs to the student. MTM doesn't hold client money and doesn't place real trades for anyone.

:::alerta
It isn't a prop firm. Nobody gives you capital to trade with their money, and there's no paid "challenge" that promises you a real account at the end. If anyone tells you otherwise, walk away.

## How to choose by your phase

I suggest three phases. In each one, the next step is different:

| Phase | How you recognise yourself | Which part makes sense |
|---|---|---|
| I'm learning | I haven't written my risk rules or my journal yet | Member, the classes and the exercises in this book |
| I already have rules on paper | I've been using a checklist, a journal and a daily limit for at least a month | Premium, scanners and, if you want, MTM Funded to train |
| I already trade with a real account | I have a regulated broker, a calculated position size and a weekly review | PU Prime broker route, with the same discipline as always |

The table has a hidden warning: the earlier you're in the right phase, the less money and time you lose. But if you don't recognise yourself in any phase, the next step is to keep learning, not to buy more tools.

## A warning sign worth keeping

There's a pattern worth knowing, because it turns up in lots of places that look friendly. When someone asks you to recruit friends or family so you can earn, or promises you growing income through a network you build, you're looking at the structure of a pyramid scheme. The name doesn't matter, nor does who presents it. What matters is where the money comes from: if it comes from new people joining, and not from a product or service someone actually uses, there's a problem.

Be wary of any plan where the money comes from bringing in other people, and always ask exactly where the money they promise you will come from.

## I'm on the other side

I'm on the other side of it. I know that, from this side, the questions look like a lot and the answers look like few, and that asking for help can feel like a weakness.

It isn't. Asking for help is a risk management decision, because it reduces the number of mistakes you make on your own. Whoever does it isn't handing their future over to anyone. They're gathering information, method and company to decide better.
