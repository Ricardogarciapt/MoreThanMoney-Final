# The 30-day plan

> A plan that fits into fifteen minutes is a plan that gets done. A plan that needs a whole weekend gets put off.

This is the practical appendix to the book. There's no new theory here. There's an order for using what you've read in the earlier chapters, one day at a time, and the templates you'll print or copy into a notebook.

The plan doesn't ask you to open an account or to deposit money. The market exercises are done on paper or on a demo account, and only once you have your rules written down. If you feel ready for more, chapter 10 explains how to choose the next step, without rushing.

:::destaque
The aim of these 30 days isn't to win anything. It's to stop deciding on gut feeling and start deciding with written rules, sums done and a record you can review.
:::

## How to use this plan

- One day, one task. If you miss a day, pick up the next one, without trying to make up for it.
- Each task has a time shown. If it runs over, stop and carry on tomorrow.
- In the weeks, there's a 15-minute review at the end of every seven days (days 7, 14, 21 and 28; day 30 is the final review).
- Use a paper notebook. Writing by hand makes you think more slowly, and that's what we want.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Leverage | The ability to control a position bigger than your own capital, lent by the broker. It multiplies gains and losses |
| Asset | Whatever is traded: a currency pair, gold, an index |
| Bid/Ask | The sell price (bid) and the buy price (ask) of an asset. The difference is the spread |
| Capital | The total money available in your account, used as the base for calculating risk |
| CFD | Contract for difference. An instrument that tracks the price movement without you buying the asset |
| Regulated broker | An intermediary subject to supervision by a recognised authority |
| Standard deviation | A measure of how far values sit from the average. It's the basis for the regression bands |
| Trading journal | A written record of each decision, with the why written before the result |
| Drawdown | The fall in capital from the highest point to the next lowest point |
| Forex | The currency exchange market |
| Lot | The size of the position. A standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency |
| Margin | Money the broker sets aside to keep a position open |
| Pip | The smallest usual price move of a pair. On EUR/USD, 0.0001 |
| Position | An open trade, long or short |
| Risk-reward ratio | The relationship between what you risk and what you hope to win on a trade |
| Trend regression | A middle line calculated on recent prices, around which the price oscillates |
| Risk per trade | The percentage or amount of capital you accept losing on a single trade |
| Exit | The moment you close the position, with a gain or a loss |
| Spread | The difference between bid and ask, charged on every trade |
| Stop-loss | An order that closes the position when the price reaches a loss level set in advance |
| Take-profit | An order that closes the position when the price reaches a gain level set in advance |
| Hit rate | The percentage of winning trades out of the total |
| Volatility | How strongly the price moves over a given period |
| XAU/USD | The symbol for gold quoted in dollars |

## Books from my desk

I didn't learn on my own, and you won't learn from this book alone either. These are underlined, dog-eared and with coffee spilled on them:

- **"Trading in the Zone", by Mark Douglas.** The psychology of trading. It teaches you to think in probabilities and to accept risk before you enter.
- **"How to Day Trade for a Living", by Andrew Aziz.** Practical and direct about routine, risk management and preparing for the day. Read it for the discipline, not for the promise in the title.
- **"Market Wizards", by Jack D. Schwager.** Interviews with professional traders. Notice how almost all of them talk more about risk than about entries.
- **"Atomic Habits", by James Clear.** It isn't about trading, but it's about what makes a trader last: small rules repeated every day.

:::nota
One book a month.
One underline a day.
One new rule a week, tested before you trust it.
:::

## Final educational warning

This book is educational content. It isn't financial advice, it isn't a recommendation to buy or sell any asset, and it doesn't guarantee results. The examples are hypothetical and are there to show how mechanisms work, not to predict anything.

Trading involves real risk, and you can lose some or all of the capital you put into your account. Before making any financial decision, look for independent information and, where appropriate, professional advice.

:::destaque
Nobody can guarantee you a result. You can guarantee yourself the process: written rules, sums done and an honest record. That's what this book tries to leave with you.
:::

## In summary

- Thirty days, one task a day, with short times and a clear aim: deciding with written rules.
- The plan doesn't require a real account. The market exercises are on paper or on a demo account.
- The three weekly reviews and the final review are what turn tasks into a habit.
- The checklist, the journal and the glossary are tools to use, not to file away.
- The educational warning always applies, and applies most when the numbers look simple.
