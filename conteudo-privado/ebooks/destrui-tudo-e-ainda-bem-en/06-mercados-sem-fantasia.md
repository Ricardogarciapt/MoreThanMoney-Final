# Markets without the fantasy

> A market isn't a money machine. It's a place where lots of people, on the other side of the screen, are also deciding.

Before I could start building again, I had to understand the ground. And the ground of financial markets is full of pretty promises and badly done sums. In this chapter I want to strip away the fantasy and leave only the part you can check.

I'm not going to tell you that markets are easy, or that they're a shortcut. Nor am I going to say they're a roulette wheel. They're something else: a price system with rules, costs and risks that are known and can be studied.

:::destaque
Trading isn't guessing the future. It's deciding, with written rules, what you'll do when the price moves one way or the other, and how much you're prepared to lose if you're wrong.
:::

## So what is trading?

When you buy a product at the supermarket, you pay a price and take the product home. That's the end of the story. Trading a financial instrument is different: you don't take anything home. You buy or sell a position on a price that's going to change, and later you close that position. The difference between the entry price and the exit price is the result, positive or negative.

Think about a flat rent in Lisboa. If you rent a T2 (a two-bedroom flat) for €900 and the next year the landlord asks for €950, the contract has changed, but you haven't gained or lost anything until you decide to sign another one. In a market, the price changes every second and your position is worth more or less every second. That's what makes the subject emotionally hard.

### Investing or trading?

They're different things, and mixing them up is one of the most common sources of trouble:

| | Investing (long term) | Trading (short term) |
|---|---|---|
| Typical time frame | Years | Minutes, hours or days |
| How many decisions | Few | Many |
| What matters most | Time and low costs | Discipline and risk management |
| Day-to-day noise | Can be ignored | Right at the centre of everything |

In this book I talk about trading, and what it demands of you. If your goal is only to save and invest over the long term, chapter 5 will be more useful to you than this one.

:::curso Lesson 104 · Who's on the other side
When you press buy, someone is selling. And it's almost never someone like you.

- **The big banks** trade currencies with each other in huge volumes. They're the ones who make the price move in the short term.
- **The central banks** move interest rates and sometimes step in directly on the currency.
- **The multinationals and the funds** trade currencies because they pay suppliers abroad or because they have their own strategies.
- **Retail traders**, like you and me, are the smallest slice.

Knowing this takes away a useful illusion of loss: the market doesn't know you exist, and it isn't against you. It simply doesn't care.

## Forex, in two sentences

The Forex market (short for "foreign exchange", the currency exchange) is where currencies are swapped for one another. When you trade EUR/USD you're betting, in simple terms, on the euro rising or falling against the dollar. You don't buy euros or dollars on paper: you buy or sell a contract on how the price moves.

Forex is the most liquid market in the world, which means there are lots of buyers and sellers at the same time. That doesn't make it safe. It makes it fast, and speed is exactly what can throw you off balance if you don't have rules.

## Gold: XAU/USD

Gold is traded against the dollar under the symbol XAU/USD. Lots of people see gold as a safe haven, and sometimes it does behave that way. But the price of gold also rises and falls with interest rates, economic news and market mood. Think of it as an asset with its own volatility, often bigger than that of a major currency pair.

So each instrument has its own rhythm, and treating gold as if it were just another currency pair is a common mistake. The rules of one market don't automatically apply to another.

## The words you'll hear

When you join a trading group, you'll immediately hear half a dozen terms. Here they are, with the definition I use in my own life:

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| Pip | The smallest usual price move of a currency pair. On EUR/USD it's 0.0001 |
| Lot | The size of the position. A standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency |
| Spread | The difference between the buy price and the sell price, charged on every trade |
| Leverage | How much money the broker lends you so you can open a position bigger than your account |
| Stop-loss | The order that closes the position when the price reaches a level you set in advance |
| Take-profit | The order that closes the position when the price reaches your target |

### Pip

A pip is the unit used to measure a price move. On EUR/USD, one pip is 0.0001. If the pair goes from 1.1000 to 1.1025, it has risen 25 pips. That sounds like very little, but the value in money depends on the size of the position, and that's where many people get it wrong.

### Lot

For EUR/USD, with your account in dollars, one pip on a standard lot is worth about $10. On a micro lot (0.01 of a lot) it's worth about $0.10. The lot sets how much weight each move carries in your account. Later, in chapter 8, I show you how to choose it sensibly.

### Spread

The spread is the hidden cost of every entry. If the buy price is 2 pips above the sell price, the position starts out losing 2 pips the moment you open it. So trading often with high spreads is a slow way of leaving money on the table.

### Leverage

Leverage is perhaps the most dangerous word in Forex. It lets you control a large position with little of your own capital. That doesn't make the position any less risky. It makes your account more sensitive to every move.

:::alerta
Leverage multiplies gains and losses in exactly the same way. High leverage isn't a bonus. Anyone who uses it without a written limit usually finds out, at their own expense, that a small move is enough to close the account.
:::

In Europe, regulators (ESMA, for example) limit the leverage available to retail clients. On the main currency pairs, the cap is 30:1. That limit exists because the regulator concluded that many retail clients don't cope well with high leverage.

## Why most people lose

I don't have statistics to give you, and I won't make them up. But I can describe the pattern, which is what matters, because it repeats in almost every story I hear.

:::historia
Illustrative example. A man from Braga opens an account with €1,500, with no plan. In the first few weeks he makes a little and feels invincible. He increases the lot size. Then in one week he loses what he'd made in three. To win it back, he increases the lot size even more. After two months he closes the account with less than half his capital, still with the feeling that he was "nearly there".
:::

The pattern is almost always the same: there are no written rules before entering, the size of the position changes with mood, a loss is treated as a personal insult rather than a cost of doing business, and the rush to recover leads to worse decisions.

There's also a reason almost nobody wants to hear. The broker earns from the spread and the commissions, whether you win or lose. Call it what it is: the business model, not a personal insult. What matters is knowing who you're dealing with.

:::destaque
European regulators require brokers to publish the percentage of retail accounts that lose money on CFDs. Go and read that information before you open any account, with whichever broker you choose.
:::

## The maths nobody shows you: recovery

Before talking about technique, I want you to understand something that changes the way you think about losses. A loss isn't recovered by the same percentage it was lost by. The deeper you fall, the harder it is to get back to where you started.

[[grafico:recuperacao-perdas]]

Have a look at the table below. The right-hand column tells you what percentage you need to gain to get back to your starting capital.

| You lost | You need to gain to get back to zero |
|---|---|
| 10% | 11.1% |
| 20% | 25% |
| 30% | 42.9% |
| 50% | 100% |
| 70% | 233% |

Illustrative example, with round numbers: an account of €1,000. It loses 50% and is left with €500. To get back to €1,000 you need to double the €500, that is, to gain 100%. The account isn't symmetrical. Once you understand this, you stop treating every loss as something to fix "on the next trade".

:::alerta
The recovery sum is the most expensive trap in trading. The bigger the loss, the greater the effort needed to recover it, and the more tempting it becomes to take bigger risks. That's exactly the spiral that the risk management in chapter 8 exists to prevent.
:::

:::cartao Before opening an account with a broker
- Is it regulated? By whom? Did I check the regulator's website?
- Is client money kept separate?
- Do I know the costs: spread, commissions, swaps?
- Can I withdraw without strange conditions?
- Did I open a demo account first?
:::

:::curso Lesson 302 · What is a pip
The pip is the unit used to measure movement. On most pairs it's the fourth decimal place: if EUR/USD goes from 1.1005 to 1.1006, it has moved one pip.

- On a standard lot of EUR/USD, one pip is worth about 10 dollars.
- On a micro lot (0.01), it's worth about 10 cents.
- Gold is measured differently. It's one of the first questions asked in class, and rightly so.

If you don't know what a pip is worth on the pair you're trading, you don't know how much you're risking. It's as simple as that.

## Choosing a broker: what to check

The broker is the front door. It isn't a detail. Here, MTM works with PU Prime, a regulated broker. I don't want you to choose it on my recommendation: I want you to choose with the same checklist you'd use to buy any financial service.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is it regulated by a recognised authority? | It defines who protects you if something goes wrong |
| Is the money held in segregated accounts? | Client money should be kept apart from the company's money |
| Are the spreads and commissions published? | Without this you don't know what each trade costs |
| What's the maximum leverage for your country? | It varies depending on regulation |
| Can you see and read the terms before you deposit? | Anyone who hides the terms has a problem |

## In summary

- Trading means deciding with rules, not guessing the future. Investing and trading are different things.
- Forex and gold (XAU/USD) are fast markets. Speed demands more discipline, not less.
- Pip, lot, spread and leverage are the four measures and costs you must master before anything else.
- Most people lose because they have no written rules, increase risk to recover, and treat a loss as a personal insult.
- A big loss needs a much bigger gain to get back to zero. This sum changes the way you think about risk.
