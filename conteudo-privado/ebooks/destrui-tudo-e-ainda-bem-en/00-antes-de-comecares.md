# Before we start

> Grab a pen. This book was made to be written on.

Before you turn the page, go and find a pen. I mean it. Use the one from the bank, the one forgotten in the glovebox, the one you walked off with by accident from a counter at the tax office. Any pen will do.

I say this because I know how it goes. You read a book like this on a Sunday, underline half a dozen sentences, feel motivated until Monday at nine, and by Wednesday you can't remember the title. I did that for years. I have a whole shelf of books that left me wanting to change and with no plan at all.

This one is different for a practical reason: it has tasks. Small ones, sometimes boring, but tasks. If you do them, ten weeks from now you'll know where your time goes, where your money goes, how long you can last if your salary stops, and how to move in a market without betting the house. If you don't do them, you've read a book. That's no crime, but it isn't what I wrote it for.

## What you'll find

There are ten chapters, one per week, plus a map of the method and a 30-day plan at the end. The order matters. We start with your week and your bills, because there's no point talking about markets to someone who doesn't know how much they spend a month. Only then do we get into charts, risk and routine.

Throughout the book you'll find some blocks with their own look:

- **Key ideas**, on a dark background. These are the sentences I'd like to stay in your head.
- **Exercises**, with lines for you to write on. Write for real, on the page or in a notebook.
- **Warnings**, for the most common mistakes. Many of them were mine.
- **Cut-out cards**, with scissors at the corner. Print them, cut them out and stick them where you see them every day. The fridge works well.
- **Post-its**, which are what I'd write by hand if I were sitting next to you.

:::cartao How to read this book
- Read the chapter in one go.
- Read it again with the pen in your hand.
- Do the week's task before moving on to the next one.
- If a week goes badly, don't start again. Keep going.
:::

## What this book is not

It isn't a manual for getting rich. It has no signals to copy, no secrets, and none of those screenshots with balances nobody can verify. When numbers appear, they're marked as an example, because that's what they are.

It isn't financial, tax or legal advice either. It's what I've learned, told as honestly as I can manage. When I talk about trading, crypto or markets, the same rule always sits underneath: you can lose money, including everything you put in. The decision is always yours.

## A Sunday ritual

Pick a fixed day. I use Sunday evening, after dinner, when the house goes quiet. You open the book at the chapter for the week, reread the task and decide when you're going to do it. Put it in your phone calendar, with an alarm. It sounds like overkill. It isn't: whatever doesn't have a set time always ends up pushed to "when I have time", and that day never comes.
