# The maths of your hours

> Your time has a ceiling. Your money doesn't need to.

Rui is 36 and a maintenance technician in Aveiro. He takes home 1,250 € a month, does overtime when it comes up, and last summer took a weekend side job to pay for his holiday. At the end of the year he worked out the numbers on the back of a receipt and realised something simple: to earn more, he only knows one thing to do. Work more hours.

Rui is an example, but I bet you know someone just like him. Maybe it's you.

## The problem nobody shows you

Swapping hours for a salary is the most common way of earning money, and there's nothing wrong with it. It pays the bills, gives you stability and teaches you a trade. The problem is the ceiling. A day has 24 hours, a week has 168, and part of them goes on sleeping, eating, being with the people you love and not getting ill. When your income depends only on your hours, it grows until the day you can't give any more hours.

[[grafico:horas-semana]]

Do the maths for your week. Between sleeping, working and getting to and from work, there isn't much left. And that little bit has to fit everything else, including learning how to put your money to work. That's why method matters more than willpower: it has to fit into fifteen minutes a day.

## Why school didn't teach you this

School prepared you to be a good professional. It taught you to keep to schedules, sit exams and follow instructions. It didn't teach you how to read a bank statement, what inflation is or how to tell an investment from a bet. That isn't your fault. But from the moment you know, it becomes your responsibility.

## Financial freedom, no filters

People talk a lot about financial freedom, nearly always with photos of beaches and cars. I prefer a duller and more useful definition: financial freedom means having options. Being able to say no to a job that makes you miserable. Getting through three months without a salary without panicking. Choosing to be at a birthday party instead of picking up an extra shift.

To get there, there are three pieces, in this order:

1. **Knowledge**: understanding how money works before you risk a single euro.
2. **Income**: having some source of income that doesn't depend only on your hours.
3. **System**: rules and tools that work for you, so you don't have to rely on willpower every single day.

:::destaque
Skipping the first piece is the fastest way to lose money. Most people I know who got burned in the markets started with the second.
:::
