# Why saving isn't enough

> Money left sitting still doesn't stay the same. It gets smaller.

My grandmother kept her banknotes in an envelope at the back of a drawer. To her, that was security. And it was, back when a coffee cost a few cents and bread barely changed price from one year to the next. Today, that same envelope loses value every month, without anyone touching it.

## Inflation, explained without the jargon

Inflation is prices going up. When prices rise 3% in a year, your 1,000 € buys what about 970 € used to buy the year before. Nobody took money out of your account. But your money buys less.

In Portugal, in 2022, inflation came close to 8%. People who kept their savings in a current account lost purchasing power without noticing. Figures from INE, Portugal's national statistics office, rounded.

## The other side: time on your side

If time eats away at money sitting still, it can also work for money that's invested. This is called compound interest: today's returns start earning returns of their own tomorrow. It doesn't happen quickly. It happens slowly, and then faster.

[[grafico:juros-compostos]]

| Year | Illustrative example: 100 € a month earning 4% a year |
|---|---|
| 1 | about 1,220 € |
| 5 | about 6,630 € |
| 10 | about 14,700 € |

Illustrative example, not a forecast or a promise of returns. Investments can be worth less than what you put in. What the table shows is the power of time and consistency, not a guaranteed result.

:::destaque
The question isn't "how much can I earn this month?". It's "what habit can I keep up for ten years?".
:::

## Returns aren't luck

There are two ways of looking at the markets. The gambler wants excitement, wants to be right now, wants to win back yesterday's losses today. The investor wants consistency, accepts small losses as part of the path and measures progress in years. Both use the same screens. The results are almost always the opposite.
