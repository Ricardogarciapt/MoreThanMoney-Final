# The paths that exist

> There's no investment without risk. There's risk you understand and risk you ignore.

When someone asks me "where should I put my money?", the honest answer is always the same: it depends on your time horizon, your emergency fund and how much risk you can sleep with. Here are the most common paths, without marketing and with the risk in plain view. This isn't a recommendation. It's a map.

## The four paths, side by side

| Path | For whom | Risk | What you need to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deposits and savings certificates** | Emergency funds and short-term goals | Low | They protect you from a scare, but barely protect you from inflation |
| **ETF with monthly investing** | People thinking 10 years or more ahead | Medium | A basket of many companies; it goes up and down, and time helps |
| **Trading (Forex, gold, indices)** | People who want to learn a skill | High | Requires method, risk management and practice on a demo account |
| **Crypto** | A small part, for people who accept volatility | Very high | You can lose most of the value in a short time |

## Monthly investing (DCA)

DCA (dollar-cost averaging) means investing the same amount every month, whatever happens. You don't try to guess the best day. You buy more when it's cheap and less when it's expensive, without having to think about it.

[[grafico:dca]]

It's boring, and that's exactly why it works for many people: it takes the emotion out of the decision. At MoreThanMoney there's a room dedicated to this, the DCA room, with the day's plan for crypto and ETFs.

## Trading, without the movie

Trading means buying and selling assets (currencies, gold, indices) over a shorter timeframe, trying to profit from price moves. It's a skill, like driving or cooking. It can be learned. But it's also where most people lose money, because they jump in without rules, with leverage and in a hurry.

:::alerta Serious warning
Trading Forex and CFDs with leverage carries a high risk of loss, including the entire amount invested. Most retail CFD accounts lose money. Always start on a demo account, and only move to a live account with money you can afford to lose.
:::

What separates those who last from those who disappear isn't intelligence. It's three things: risking little on each trade (1% of the account is a common rule), having a written plan before you click, and logging every trade in a journal. That's what we teach at MoreThanMoney, with tools that help you stick to it: scanners that mark zones and signals on the chart, the house WebTrader and live classes.

## Copying strategies: what it is and what it isn't

Some people don't have time to analyse charts and prefer to link their account to a strategy that already has a track record, so that its trades are copied. At MoreThanMoney this is called MTM Auto. It can be a good way to start, as long as you understand three things: the risk is still yours, a strategy's track record doesn't guarantee the future, and you should be able to see that track record independently audited before you copy it.

:::destaque
Don't trust screenshots. Trust public audits that you can open yourself, with every trade, the good ones and the bad.
:::
