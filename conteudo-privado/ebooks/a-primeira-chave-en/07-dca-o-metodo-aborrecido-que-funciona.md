# DCA: the boring method that protects you from yourself

> The best method is the one you can keep repeating for years, even when it is boring.

DCA stands for dollar-cost averaging. In plain English: buying a fixed amount at regular intervals, for example €50 a month, whatever happens to the price. It sounds unexciting. There are no charts exploding and no adrenaline at two in the morning. And that is exactly why so many people end up liking it.

The core idea is simple. When the price is high, you buy fewer units. When it is low, you buy more. Over time, the average price you pay tends to smooth out, instead of depending on a single day.

## DCA versus a lump sum

The alternative is a lump sum: you put in one amount and buy everything at once. Neither method always wins. With a lump sum, if the price rises straight after, you end up with more. With DCA, if the price falls after your first purchase, you are less exposed to the worst moment.

DCA does not remove risk. It spreads the decision over time, and that takes the weight off something nobody can do consistently: hitting one exact moment.

:::destaque
DCA is not a promise of profit. It is a way of taking away the pressure to guess the right moment.
:::

## Illustrative example: three purchases of €100

**Illustrative example**, with round, made-up prices. Imagine you buy €100 a month for three months, at €2,000, €1,600 and €2,400 per unit of a coin.

- Month 1: 100 ÷ 2,000 = 0.05 units.
- Month 2: 100 ÷ 1,600 = 0.0625 units.
- Month 3: 100 ÷ 2,400 = 0.0417 units.

In total you invested €300 and hold about 0.1542 units. Your average price is 300 ÷ 0.1542, about €1,946 per unit. If the price is at €2,400 at the end, the position is worth about €370.

Now the lump sum: €300 at €2,000 gives 0.15 units. With the same final price of €2,400, they are worth €360. In this scenario, DCA won because the price dipped along the way.

But if the price had kept rising, €2,000, €2,200 and €2,400, DCA would give about €329 and the lump sum €360. The same strategy can win or lose depending on the path the price takes. There is no magic method.

## Why DCA helps with discipline

The biggest enemy of anyone investing is not the market. It is their own head. DCA creates a habit, with a fixed date and a fixed amount. That routine protects you from buying everything at the peak of enthusiasm and selling everything at the peak of fear.

There is another practical advantage: it works well with regular income. If you get a monthly salary, set aside a fixed slice on the day it arrives and treat that money as an account you do not touch. When it stops fitting your expenses, adjust it.

## MTM's smart DCA

There is a version of DCA that I use and teach, and that does not spoil what is good about DCA. You keep buying every time, on the same day. The only thing that changes is the size of each purchase, depending on whether the market is more stretched upwards or downwards than usual.

In the MTM System app, under Portfolios, there is a panel called DCA Analysis. Every day the system looks at the assets in the portfolios, crypto and ETFs, and gives each one one of four labels:

| Label | When it appears (crypto) | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Strong Buy | Daily drop of 8% or more | Up to double your base amount |
| Buy | Daily drop of 4% or more | One and a half times the base |
| Wait | Calm day | Half the base |
| Do Not Add | Sharp rise in a single day | Zero for that asset, do not chase the price |

For ETFs the thresholds are lower, because they move less. And read the third column carefully: these are suggestions from a system, to help you think. The decision is yours.

:::destaque
Smart DCA never stops buying because of an opinion. It only changes the size. And your monthly ceiling does not change, whether there is a label or not.
:::

### The reserve pot

This is where it becomes safe. If your plan is €100 a month, it stays €100 a month. In weeks marked Wait or Do Not Add, the part you do not spend goes into a reserve pot. In weeks marked Strong Buy, the extra top-up comes out of that pot. If the pot is empty, you buy the base and that is it. You never take money from outside the plan.

### A second opinion on the bigger chart

Anyone who wants to go a little further can confirm the day for a top-up on a daily or weekly chart, using one of the MTM scanners. Not to decide whether to buy, but to decide whether to use the pot.

- **Momentum:** adds up five confirmations (the cloud, the position of the cloud, the exponential moving average, volume and the RSI). A green daily with four or five in favour: you make the suggested top-up. A strong red: only the base, the pot waits. Lilac boxes, sideways market: only the base.
- **DEFY:** draws exhaustion bands around a moving average. When the price stretches to the outer band and turns with a strong candle, with confirmation stars, it is a sign it is far from normal.

:::cartao My Friday rule
- Two readings in favour (label and chart): base + pot.
- One reading in favour: base + at most half the pot.
- None: only the base.
- The monthly ceiling matters more than any tool.
:::

## Exercise: draw up your plan

:::exercicio My DCA plan
1. Write down a monthly amount you can keep up for at least twelve months, without sacrifices that would make you give up.
2. Choose a fixed day of the month for the purchase and write it down.
3. Write the rule for when the price falls 30% below your average: keep going, pause or review the plan. Decide now, not in the moment of panic.
4. Write the rule for when the price rises sharply: keep the plan, without speeding up purchases.

## In short

- DCA means buying a fixed amount at regular intervals.
- It spreads the decision over time, instead of betting on a single moment.
- It does not guarantee a profit: the result depends on the path the price takes.
- The biggest value of the method lies in discipline and routine.
- The plan should fit your budget without forcing you to sell.

Educational content, not financial advice. Any investment can lose value, and you can lose everything you put into it.
