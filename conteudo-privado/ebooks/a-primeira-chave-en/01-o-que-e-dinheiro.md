# What money is

> Money is a promise that everyone accepts. The rest is detail.

Think about the euro you have in your wallet. It is a piece of paper, or a metal coin, and it is no use for eating or wearing. So why does the baker give you bread in exchange? The answer is simple and a little surprising: because everyone believes the next seller will accept it tomorrow too.

This collective trust is the real foundation of money. When it exists, money works. When it disappears, money stops being useful, however nice the paper looks. It is worth keeping this in mind before you look at any digital coin, because the underlying question is always the same: who do you trust, and why?

## From barter to the euro

Before there were coins, people swapped things. Imagine a village where the fisherman needs shoes and the cobbler needs fish. If the two meet and each wants exactly what the other has, the swap happens. But what if the cobbler does not like fish? Or if the fisherman only has fish today and needs shoes in three months?

This difficulty has a name: the double coincidence of wants. To solve it, societies began using an intermediate good, something everyone accepted and that kept its value. They used salt, shells, livestock and precious metals. Later came coins and notes, and today we have the euro, issued by the European Central Bank and the national central banks.

The euro has no intrinsic value the way salt did when it preserved fish. Its value comes from the law, from trust in institutions and from the fact that everyone uses it. That does not make it any less useful. It simply means it depends on a collective agreement.

## Three jobs money has to do

Economists say money serves three purposes. They are worth memorising, because you will use them to assess any currency, including crypto ones.

:::cartao The three functions of money
- Medium of exchange: you can pay for your coffee and your rent with it.
- Unit of account: you can compare prices (a coffee costs €1.20, a transport pass costs a different amount).
- Store of value: you can keep it today and use it tomorrow without it falling apart.
:::

:::destaque
Good money is easy to use, easy to measure and hard to spoil. When one of those three things fails, trust starts to drain away.
:::

## Inflation, the silent thief

Imagine you have €100 in a drawer and a basket of shopping costs €100. If inflation is 5% a year, in a year the same basket will cost €105. Your €100 is still €100, but it no longer buys the same. Money has lost purchasing power without anyone taking anything from your wallet.

**Illustrative example**, with round, made-up figures: Inês, 29, a nurse in Coimbra, keeps €3,000 in an account that pays no interest. If average annual inflation is 4%, the basket that costs €3,000 today will cost about €3,120 in a year. The numbers are there only to show the logic. They are not a forecast.

## Why Bitcoin was born

On 31 October 2008, someone using the name Satoshi Nakamoto published a document called a whitepaper. It proposed a system of electronic money that did not need a central bank or an intermediary to validate transactions. In January 2009, the network's first block, known as the genesis block, was created on 3 January that year. Nobody knows for certain who this person is, and that is part of the story too.

The context helps you understand the idea. In 2008 there was a serious financial crisis, with banks collapsing and governments rescuing institutions. Many people felt the financial system had ended up in the hands of a few. Bitcoin was a technical answer to that distrust: a money whose rules are written in public code and cannot be changed by the decision of a single entity.

This does not mean Bitcoin is perfect or that it solves every problem. It means it works on a different logic from the euro, and that this logic brings its own advantages and risks.

## In short

- Money is worth something because people trust it and accept it.
- Good money works as a medium of exchange, a unit of account and a store of value.
- Inflation erodes purchasing power even when you spend nothing.
- Bitcoin was born in 2008 as a response to distrust in the financial system.
- Bitcoin's logic differs from the euro's, with its own advantages and risks.
