# Reading the market without guessing

> Anyone who says they know what the market is going to do is selling a certainty that does not exist.

There is a scene that plays out in many homes. It is past eleven at night, your phone is on the sofa beside you and your thumb keeps scrolling. One chart goes up, another goes down, and someone writes "this is about to explode". Nobody made you look, but you stayed. This chapter is about training a different way of looking: reading the market to understand what is going on, without pretending you can guess what comes next.

## Cycles: waves that are not a clock

Crypto markets go through phases of strong rises, euphoria, falls and quiet accumulation. Analysts call these cycles. They are worth studying, because they help you understand the context. But a past cycle is not a timetable. You do not know when one starts or when it ends, and each cycle has different causes.

Think of the sea. You know there are tides, and you know the water rises and falls every few hours. You do not know where the water will be tomorrow at three in the afternoon, because the wind and the pressure change the sum. Cycles are similar: there is a general pattern, but the detail escapes you.

## Volatility: the price that dances

Volatility measures how much a price swings. In crypto it is usually very high, compared with a fixed-term deposit or a bond fund. A 10% move in a single day is not rare, and that changes how you should size any position.

:::destaque
Volatility is not a flaw in the market. It is the reason to invest only what you can watch swing without losing sleep.
:::

## Bitcoin dominance

You will see a lot about Bitcoin dominance: the percentage of the total crypto market value that belongs to Bitcoin. When it rises, altcoins often struggle more. When it falls, there can be more appetite for alternative coins. It is not a fixed rule. It is a useful indicator for seeing where the money is heading, and that is all it is.

## News versus noise

Most crypto news is noise. A tweet from a public figure, a rumour of a partnership, a prediction from an influencer: all of this moves the price for minutes or hours, and almost never changes what you need to know about a project.

Separate two kinds of information. There is news with verifiable facts, such as a decision published on an official website, a documented technical change or an audit report. And there is noise, which is emotion wrapped in punchy sentences, with no source and no date. When information leaves you rushed and afraid, it is almost always noise.

:::conversa WhatsApp · Ana

:::

:::alerta
If a piece of news makes you want to buy or sell right now, it is already noise. Wait twenty-four hours and read it again calmly. If you still want to act, the decision is yours, but now you will have more information.
:::

## How to read a chart without fooling yourself

A chart shows the past. It helps you understand the context, but it guarantees nothing about the future. When you look at one, ask yourself three things: what period am I looking at? Which events mark the rises and the falls? What changed between the start and the end?

Choosing the period can also mislead you. A seven-day chart can look like a dramatic trend, and a five-year chart can tell a completely different story. Always change the scale, and be wary of anyone who only shows you the period that suits them.

:::ecra Example · same asset, two periods (illustrative)
Period: 7 days · Reading: upward trend
Period: 5 years · Reading: a fall phase and a recovery
Conclusion: it depends on the period you chose
:::

:::cartao The three-question filter
- Does this information come from a verifiable source?
- If the price were 20% below today's, would I still want to buy?
- Am I acting because of today's news, or because of a plan I wrote down earlier?

## A short story

:::historia
Illustrative case: Beatriz, 31, a shop manager in Lisbon, spent her nights watching charts. She thought that if she studied enough, she would eventually see the pattern. One day she realised that it was anxiety by another name. She cut down her screen time, started checking the market once a week and felt calmer and more attentive to what really mattered.

## In short

- Cycles exist, but they are not timetables. Reading the context is not the same as predicting.
- High volatility means sizing your positions with care.
- Bitcoin dominance is a useful indicator, not an oracle.
- News with a source is worth more than noise that stirs emotion.
- A chart shows the past, and the period you choose changes the reading.
