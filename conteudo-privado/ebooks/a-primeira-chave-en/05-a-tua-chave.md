# Your key

> In crypto, whoever holds the key holds the money. Everything else is a promise.

If there is one idea you should take from this book into daily life, it is this one. When you buy crypto on a platform, what you have, in practice, is a record that says you have a balance. The coins are held on a network, and whoever controls access to them is whoever holds the key. If the key is yours, the money is yours. If the key is in someone else's hands, the money is theirs, even if the screen shows your name.

English speakers put it bluntly: not your keys, not your coins. It is the most important sentence in this chapter, and perhaps in the whole book.

## What a wallet is

A crypto wallet does not store coins the way a safe stores banknotes. It stores keys. The public key works like an account number, which you can share in order to receive money. The private key is what proves that you are the person allowed to spend. Whoever holds the private key can move the money, without asking anyone's permission.

[[grafico:tipos-carteira]]

That is where the term seed phrase, or recovery phrase, comes from: a list of 12 or 24 words, generated by the wallet, that lets you recover all of your keys. It is the master key. If someone gets hold of it, they have full access to the money, in any compatible wallet.

## Hot wallets and cold wallets

There are two main types of wallet.

**Hot wallets** are connected to the internet. These are phone apps, browser extensions and accounts on platforms. They are convenient for everyday use and for small amounts, but they are more exposed: viruses, fake websites, stolen phones.

**Cold wallets** are not permanently connected to the internet. The best known examples are physical devices, such as the Ledger, which keep your keys offline and only use them when you sign a transaction. They are safer against remote attacks, but they require care with the device itself and with recovery.

There is no perfect wallet. There is a wallet that suits the amount and your habits. For larger sums that you rarely touch, a cold wallet usually makes more sense. For small amounts you use often, a hot wallet may be enough.

:::destaque
The more money you have stored and the less you move it, the stronger your reasons for using a cold wallet.

## Custody, or who keeps it for you

When the money stays on the platform, the platform is the custodian. You have a claim on the balance, but you do not hold the key. This is practical, but it carries risks: if the platform fails, is attacked or freezes accounts, your access can stay blocked for a long time.

When the money moves into a wallet you control, you become the custodian. You gain control, but you also take on total responsibility. If you lose the seed phrase, no customer service team can recover it for you. There is no "forgot my password" button for the master key.

## The seed phrase, on paper and by hand

Keep your seed phrase on paper, written by hand, and never digitally. Do not photograph it, do not email it, do not keep it in a notes app on your phone and do not put it in the cloud. A photograph is a copy that can be stolen.

To protect against fire, flooding or simply the paper wearing out, some people use an engraved metal plate. In our ecosystem there is the Cryptotag Zeus, available in the MTM shop, which is a metal holder for a backup of your seed. It is not mandatory, and there are other options. What matters is that the copy is physical, durable and hidden from anyone who should not see it.

:::cartao Rules for the physical copy
- Written by hand, on paper or metal.
- Kept somewhere only you know about.
- Never photographed, never scanned.
- Never shown to anyone, not even to someone who says they are from support.
:::

:::alerta
Nobody at your platform, no technical support team, no "account manager" and no giveaway app needs your seed phrase. Anyone who asks for it is trying to steal from you.
:::

## A short story

:::historia
Illustrative case: Rafael, 38, a designer in Porto, took a photograph of his seed phrase to "have a backup". Months later, his phone was stolen and the photo was sitting in the gallery, with no protection at all. He did not lose everything, because he did not have much stored, but he learned that a digital copy is the same as leaving the key under the doormat.
:::

## In short

- Whoever holds the private keys controls the money.
- Hot wallets are convenient and exposed; cold wallets are safer and less practical.
- With platform custody, access depends on a third party.
- The seed phrase should be copied by hand, on paper or metal, and never stored digitally.
- No legitimate party will ever ask you for your seed phrase.

## To do today

1. Write your storage plan on paper, using the four questions from the exercise.
2. If you already have a seed phrase, check whether there is any digital copy, such as a photo or a note.
3. Only delete the digital copy once you have made the physical copy by hand and kept it somewhere safe.
4. If anyone contacts you asking for the phrase, do not reply, and confirm the contact through the official website.
