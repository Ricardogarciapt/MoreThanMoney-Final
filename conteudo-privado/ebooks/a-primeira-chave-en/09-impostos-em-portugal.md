# Taxes in Portugal

> Tax is the only partner that loses nothing when you lose. So it is worth getting to know it early.

I will start with a warning that I will repeat through this chapter: the rules change, and this is a general explanation, not a tax opinion. Always confirm with a chartered accountant (contabilista certificado, the Portuguese professional title) before filing or making any decision with a tax impact. That said, there are basic concepts you should know from the start, so that you are not caught out in April.

## What counts as a capital gain

A capital gain is the profit you make when you sell something for more than you paid. In crypto, it appears when you sell a coin for euros, when you swap one coin for another at a value above cost, or when you use it to pay for something at a gain. Each of these operations can have tax consequences.

Pay attention to swapping between coins. Many people think tax only applies when you convert to euros. That is not always the case. In certain situations, swapping one crypto for another can be treated as a sale. Do not assume anything on your own: ask the accountant. That is why you should record everything, even the swaps that seem to be "just from one coin to another".

## The rules in Portugal, in broad terms

Since 2023, capital gains on crypto-assets held for less than 365 days are, as a rule, taxed at 28% in Portugal. There is an option to aggregate them with your other income, which can change the result depending on your income. Capital gains on crypto-assets held for 365 days or more are, as a rule, exempt.

:::nota
Write in the margin of your notebook: the rules change, confirm with a chartered accountant.
:::

:::destaque
How long you hold the coin before selling can completely change the tax. But never decide only because of tax: the decision to sell should have solid reasons behind it.
:::

## Illustrative example

**Illustrative example**, with round, made-up figures: Pedro, 33, bought a coin for €1,000 and sold it three months later for €1,400. The capital gain is €400. If it is taxed at 28%, the tax would be €112. If the aggregation option makes sense in your case, the figure may be different. These numbers are there to show the logic, not to calculate your tax.

In practice, the question that matters most is not "how much do I pay?", but "when did I buy and when did I sell?". With those two dates and the value of each operation, you can already grasp the order of magnitude and go to the accountant far better prepared.

If Pedro had waited more than 365 days, the general rule would point to an exemption. This does not mean he should wait. It means time is a variable you need to know about.

Purchase date: 3 January
Purchase value: €1,000
Sale date: 3 April
Sale value: €1,400
Capital gain: €400
:::

## Losses: what to do with them

When you sell at a loss, the tax calculation may take that loss into account, within the rules that apply. The detail depends on your situation, and this is where an accountant saves you time and headaches. Do not count on losses to offset anything until you have confirmed it.

## Record everything: the operations sheet

The biggest source of problems I see is not the tax itself, it is the lack of records. When the time comes to file, nobody remembers when they bought, at what price, how much they paid in fees or whether they swapped between coins. Rebuilding that afterwards costs far more.

Create a simple sheet, on paper or in a file, with these columns: date, type of operation (purchase, sale, swap, transfer), coin, quantity, value in euros, fees paid and platform used. Update it the same day.

:::ecra Example · one line of the sheet (illustrative)
Date: 12/03
Type: swap
Coin: example coin A for example coin B
Value in euros: €250
Fees: €2
Platform: your platform
:::

:::alerta
Do not wait for tax-return season to rebuild your history. Keep the platform statements, export the movements and check that your sheet matches what the platform shows.
:::

## In short

- In Portugal, capital gains on crypto held for less than 365 days are, as a rule, taxed at 28%, with a possible aggregation option.
- Held for 365 days or more, they are, as a rule, exempt.
- Swaps between coins can, in certain cases, be taxable, so record all of them.
- Your operations sheet is your best ally.
- The rules change. Always confirm with a chartered accountant.
