# Step 3 · Automate

> Automating isn't leaving things to chance. It's following rules without emotion.

After a few weeks on step 2, you'll notice something: the hard part isn't finding trades. It's managing the ones that are open. Moving the stop when price gets close, closing too early out of fear, forgetting to protect a trade that was already in profit. The third step exists to take those decisions out of your hands, in the moments when your hands are the problem.

## What gets automated

| What | How | What it saves you from |
|---|---|---|
| **Protection** | Auto Break-Even: the stop moves to entry when price reaches the first target | Watching a winning trade turn into a loss |
| **Following** | Trailing Stop: the stop follows price at a fixed distance | Closing early out of fear |
| **Exits** | Partial exits at each target | Having to decide everything at once |
| **Record** | History and journal together | Forgetting what you did and why |
| **Proof** | A shareable audit | Relying on screenshots |

On the MoreThanMoney WebTrader, Auto Break-Even, Trailing Stop and partial exits run on the server, even with the tab closed. They work with demo accounts, MTM Funded accounts (simulated) and accounts with your own broker, on MetaTrader or TradeLocker.

## Rules written down, not remembered

Automation starts on paper. Before you switch on any automation, write down the rules it's going to follow.

:::exercicio My management rules
- When it reaches the first target, I do: ☐ Break-Even ☐ partial exit ☐ both
- Percentage I close at the first target: ________ %
- Trailing Stop: ☐ yes, at ________ pips/points ☐ no
- I stop trading for the day when I lose: ________ R
- I stop trading for the week when I lose: ________ R
:::

:::destaque
The stop-trading rule is the most important automation of all. A bad day mustn't turn into a bad week.
:::

## The audit: your proof, for yourself

At the end of a month, open your history and your account's audit. Not to show anyone. To see, with numbers, whether you're sticking to the plan. The house accounts have public audits with every trade, and that's the standard I ask you to hold yourself to.
