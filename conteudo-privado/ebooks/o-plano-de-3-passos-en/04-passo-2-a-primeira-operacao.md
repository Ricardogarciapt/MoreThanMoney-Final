# Step 2 · Your first trade, with a cool head

> A good trade is one that follows the plan. Win or lose.

Joana, 33, a designer in Lisbon, placed her first demo trade on a Thursday. It went wrong: the price hit the stop in twenty minutes. She was pleased. Not because she'd lost, but because she lost exactly what she'd decided to lose, not a cent more. For the first time, she felt she was in control of something.

## The life of a trade

1. **Before:** your routine tells you whether there's a trade. The checklist confirms it.
2. **Size:** you calculate the volume from the stop, so you only risk your R.
3. **Entry:** you open the order with the stop and target already set.
4. **Management:** you let the rules work. You don't move the stop further away.
5. **After:** you log it in the journal, with the result in R and your answer to "did I follow the plan?".

:::cartao The checklist before clicking
- Is the signal confirmed (candle closed)?
- Is there room to the first target?
- Do I know where the stop is?
- Does the volume risk only 1%?
- Is there no major news in the next 30 minutes?
:::

## The right size, without complicated formulas

The most common mistake beginners make is always using the same lot size. The distance to the stop changes from trade to trade, so the same lot size risks different amounts. On the MoreThanMoney WebTrader you enter the risk as a percentage and the platform calculates the volume from the stop. In MetaTrader, do the maths with a calculator or with the table in "The Cold Method".

## Losing streaks will happen

[[grafico:sequencia-perdas]]

Even an approach that wins more often than it loses has losing streaks. It's not a sign that the method has stopped working. It's statistics. What protects you is small risk and the journal, which tells you after 30 trades whether the problem is the method or the execution.

:::historia Joana, trade 12
Three losses in a row. On the fourth, the urge was to double the volume "to win it back". She opened her journal, saw the three losses had all followed the plan, and kept at 1%. The fourth hit the target. Doubling would have worked out too, but she'd have learned the wrong rule.
:::
