# Step 1 · The mental foundation

> The market doesn't know you exist. So it isn't against you. You just have to stop working against yourself.

Everyone thinks the hard part of trading is the analysis. It isn't. The hard part is doing what you decided when the price is moving against you and your gut is telling you something else. That's why this first step ends here, in your head, not on the chart.

## Gambler or investor

| The gambler | The investor |
|---|---|
| Wants excitement | Wants consistency |
| Measures themselves by the last trade | Measures themselves by the month and the year |
| Increases the lot size to win it back | Keeps the risk the same, win or lose |
| Decides during the trade | Decides beforehand, in writing |
| Hides the losses | Logs everything in the journal |

Both use the same screens. Choose the right-hand column every day, especially on the days you feel like the left.

## Your reason

Bad days will come. A run of losses, a week with no time, a friend telling you it's all a scam. On those days, the only thing holding you in place is knowing why you started.

## The routine that fits into fifteen minutes

You don't need to spend all day staring at charts. In fact, people who spend all day looking tend to trade more and worse. A short routine, always the same, is enough.

[[grafico:rotina-15min]]

1. **Context and calendar (3 min):** is there any big news today? If so, take extra care.
2. **Reading with the scanner (5 min):** what the tool says about your asset and your chart timeframe.
3. **Checklist and plan (4 min):** have you met the rules? If not, there's no trade.
4. **Journal (3 min):** record what you did and how you felt.

:::nota
A day with no trade, with the routine completed, is a good day.
:::
