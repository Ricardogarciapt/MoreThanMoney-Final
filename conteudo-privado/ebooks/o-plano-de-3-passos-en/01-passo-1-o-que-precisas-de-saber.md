# Step 1 · What you really need to know

> You don't have to know everything. You have to know enough not to hurt yourself.

Miguel, 41, a sales rep in Leiria, opened his first broker account on a Saturday night. By Sunday he'd watched six videos. On Monday he opened three gold trades, at the maximum leverage the broker allowed. By Wednesday the account was half its value. He knew many things. He didn't know the four that matter.

## The four ideas that separate those who last from those who disappear

:::cartao The four ideas
- **Risk per trade:** how much of your account you accept losing if the idea goes wrong. Common rule: 1%.
- **Stop:** the price where you exit if you're wrong. Set it before entering, never after.
- **Leverage:** the maximum the broker lets you use, not a recommendation.
- **Demo account:** pretend money, real market. It's where you make mistakes for free.
:::

## Why 1% is so much

It sounds small. Risking 1% of a 1,000 € account means risking 10 € per trade. But look at what happens when you lose a lot in one go.

[[grafico:recuperacao-perdas]]

Someone who loses half their account needs to double what's left just to get back to the start. Someone who risks 1% can get it wrong ten times in a row and still have 90% of the account and a clear head. Getting it wrong ten times in a row happens to everyone, sooner or later.

## The R, a measure that doesn't lie

Instead of measuring results in euros, measure in R. One R is what you risk on a trade. If you risk 10 € and make 20 €, you've made 2R. If you lose the 10 €, you've lost 1R. R lets you compare trades of different sizes, and it takes the emotion out of the number.

| Trade | I risked | Result | In R |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 € | +15 € | +1.5R |
| 2 | 10 € | −10 € | −1R |
| 3 | 12 € | +24 € | +2R |
