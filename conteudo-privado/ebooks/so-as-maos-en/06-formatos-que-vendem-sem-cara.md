# Formats that sell without a face
> The hand enters the screen, and the person decides in three seconds whether to stay or scroll past.

Think of Inês, 29, who makes paper planners at home in a small flat in Setúbal. On her first attempt she filmed herself facing the camera, explaining the product, with her phone propped on a tin of beans. Hardly anyone stopped. The following week she filmed only her hands folding a page and writing the date at the top, without saying a word. That video started more conversations than the first one. The product was the same. The format changed.

When someone asks me "what kind of video should I make?", the honest answer is that it depends on what you want the person to do next. Watch to the end, save, understand, click or write to you. Each of these actions calls for a different format.

## Six formats for hands

**1. Hand B-roll.** Close-up shots: hands writing in a notebook, folding paper, assembling a box. It's supporting material that shows the process without needing any speech.

**2. Voiceover.** Your narration over footage of hands. It's useful for teaching and for explaining the reasoning behind a gesture. Record somewhere without echo, with the phone about twenty centimetres from your mouth.

**3. On-screen text.** Big phrases, little text per screen, which can be read without sound. Use it for the hook and for the call to action. Plenty of people watch videos with the sound off, so the text has to work on its own.

**4. Carousels.** A sequence of images, with one step per slide. Good for saving and sharing. A well-made carousel is a small lesson the person keeps for later.

**5. POV.** The camera in the position of your eyes. The viewer feels they're doing it with you. Good for short tutorials and for showing the order of a gesture.

**6. Process ASMR.** Natural sounds from the work: pencil scratching, paper turning, water running. It creates atmosphere and focus. Overdoing it sounds like an advert, so use it sparingly.

## Film once, publish in several formats

A one-hour session in which you put a product together with your hands can give you a short Reel, a six-step carousel and five stories with different details. That's seven pieces from a single moment of work. The numbers are an illustrative example, but the reasoning is real: the filming session is the expensive resource, and everything else is reuse.

:::destaque
Film once and publish in several formats. The same product appears from different angles without you having to start from scratch every week.
:::

## Filming with a phone

You don't need expensive kit. You need light, a plain background and steadiness. A window with natural light will do, as long as the sun isn't hitting your hands directly. Always use the same surface, because it's going to be your visual signature. Film landscape for presentations and portrait for Reels and stories.

Sound is what people notice most. In a room with echo, a cloth or a cushion nearby helps a lot.

:::cartao Filming checklist
- Window light, with no direct sun on your hands.
- The same surface in every video.
- Phone held steady, in portrait for Reels.
- A cloth nearby if there's echo.
- Music only from the libraries licensed for commercial use.
:::

:::alerta
Don't use copyrighted music outside the libraries the platform provides for commercial use. If anyone appears in the shot, even just a familiar hand or a distinctive bracelet, ask for permission before you publish. A person's image belongs to them, and the GDPR (the EU data protection law) protects the data that makes them identifiable.
:::

## Script for a hands Reel (illustrative example)

This script is for a video of about thirty seconds on organising a to-do list.

| Seconds | Shot | On-screen text | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Hands opening a notebook | "Too many tasks in your head?" | "When the list only exists in your head, it wears you out." |
| 3 to 10 | B-roll: hands writing three tasks | "Step 1: write everything down" | "Write it all down, without filtering." |
| 10 to 20 | POV: hands crossing out what isn't urgent | "Step 2: separate the urgent" | "Does this really have to be done today?" |
| 20 to 27 | Hands marking three priorities | "Step 3: pick three" | "Three, and no more. That way they fit into the day." |
| 27 to 30 | Fixed shot of the notebook with the final list | "I've saved this for tomorrow" | "If you want the template, tell me in the comments." |

The last shot invites conversation without pushing. It's the kind of ending that makes people reply.

## In summary

Each format serves a different goal: reach, process, teaching, closeness or action. Hands work well when the setting and the light repeat. Film once and reuse the session. And always ask for permission before anyone recognisable appears.

## To do today

1. Pick up your notebook or a sheet of paper and write, in one sentence, the goal of your next piece of content.
2. Choose a format from the matrix that serves that goal.
3. Film three shots of hands, with your phone propped against a box or a book, each lasting five seconds.
4. Write the text for each shot, with no more than seven words per line.
5. Watch the three shots without sound. If you can't tell what's happening, reshoot before you publish.
