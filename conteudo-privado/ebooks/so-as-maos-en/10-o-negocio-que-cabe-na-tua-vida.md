# The business that fits into your life
> Head, body and heart. A business that doesn't fit into your life ends up eating it.

There's a version of online entrepreneurship that sells the idea that you can always be working, sleep very little and call it passion. That isn't what I'm proposing. I want to talk to you about a business that fits into your life: one that respects your time, your health and the things that give you meaning.

It's often called a lifestyle business. It's a business designed around the pace of life you want to have, not one that grows at any cost. Be honest with yourself: it isn't a business without work. It's work with limits.

## What a lifestyle business is

A lifestyle business has three characteristics. It grows at a pace the person can sustain. It puts sustainability ahead of speed. And it has clear limits, set before the pressure arrives.

These limits might look like a way of holding back growth. In practice, they stop the business from becoming a second load you never switch off from. Someone who doesn't define the end of the day ends up working every hour there is.

## Head, body and heart

I think of a sustainable business as a balance between three dimensions. If one of them dominates the others, the rest start to give way.

| Dimension | What it asks for | Questions to ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Head | Planning, strategy and learning | What am I learning this week? What have I decided with clarity? |
| Body | Sleep, breaks, movement and a calm space | Am I sleeping enough? Do I have a place where I work and where I stop working? |
| Heart | Meaning, enjoyment and saying no to what drains you | What gives me energy in this work? What am I starting to hate? |

:::destaque
A healthy business isn't the one that grows fastest. It's the one you can keep going for years without losing your health or your desire to do it.
:::

## A typical week

A simple way to make sure the business fits into your life is to sketch out a typical week. It isn't a rigid timetable. It's a way of seeing where the work blocks sit and where the breaks are.

[[grafico:semana-tipo]]

This is an illustrative example for someone who works a full-time job and gives the project about seven hours a week (the table adds up to 6h 45min, rounded):

| Day | Block | Time (example) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Plan the week's content | 1 hour |
| Tuesday | Film hands, one session for several formats | 1 hour 30min |
| Wednesday | Write scripts and stories | 1 hour |
| Thursday | Work on the product | 2 hours |
| Friday | Reply to messages and DM conversations | 45 minutes |
| Saturday | Short review and scheduled publishing | 30 minutes |
| Sunday | No work | 0 |

Sunday is left blank on purpose. If the business needs Sunday to function, then the business is designing your life, and that's a signal to adjust what you do.

## Limits that protect the business

Limits are the most practical part of this chapter. Define them in writing and communicate them to your buyers when it's relevant.

:::cartao My limits
- Reply hours: weekdays, nine to six. Anything else waits for the next block.
- One day a week with no content and no stories. The feed won't die because of it.
- A fixed end to the day. You close the laptop and don't open it again that night, unless there's a real emergency.
- A short list of requests you turn down, such as work outside your topic or impossible deadlines.
:::

:::conversa Instagram · Carla
Carla: Good evening! Can you reply to my question about the template now?
eu: Good evening, Carla. I'll reply tomorrow from nine, in my messages block. If it's urgent for you, tell me and I'll see what I can do.
Carla: It's not urgent. Tomorrow's fine, thanks.
:::

:::alerta
If you're always available, the client learns to expect that you are. Limits aren't a punishment for the buyer. They're an honest way of keeping up the quality of what you deliver.
:::

:::historia
**Illustrative case: Tiago.** Tiago, a fictional person, worked as an employee and had about an hour and a half a night for his project. At first he replied to every message straight away, even at night, and within a few weeks he felt exhausted. When he set a reply window and a day without work, the project slowed down but gained in quality. The problem wasn't the time. It was the lack of boundaries.
:::

## In summary

A lifestyle business grows at a pace you can sustain, and it has both work and limits. Balance head, body and heart, because when one dimension dominates, the others give way. Sketch out a typical week and set aside one day without work, even if it seems impossible.

## To do today

1. Open the calendar on your phone and write down the hours you actually have each week for the project, after your job, your home and your rest.
2. Sketch your typical week in a phone note, with one block per task and one empty day.
3. Choose the time you close off work each day and create an alarm with that name.
4. Write your reply hours in one line and paste it into your profile description.
5. Choose your day without work and, on that day, don't open your phone for the project.
