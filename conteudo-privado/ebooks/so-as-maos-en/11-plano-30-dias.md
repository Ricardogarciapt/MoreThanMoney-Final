# The 30-day plan
> Thirty days won't cover everything. They're enough to start well and to see the path ahead of you.

We've reached the end of the book. If you've read each chapter with your notebook open, you already have plenty to work with: a specific person in mind, a brand with colour, lettering and voice, a first product you've validated, a sequence of stories, DM scripts and a week that fits into your life.

This last chapter brings it all together into a thirty-day plan, with a launch checklist and a glossary so you don't get lost in the words of digital marketing. It isn't a plan to follow to the letter. It's a map. If one day doesn't work out, adjust and carry on.

## How to use this plan

The plan is split into four parts, each with its own focus. Each day has one main task, which should fit into thirty to sixty minutes. If you have a day with less time, do the minimum version and log it in your notebook. A small day done is worth more than a big day that never happened.

Within each part the order isn't a big deal, but the order between parts matters. Don't jump to the next part before you've finished the previous one.

## Part 1 · Client and rest (days 1 to 7)

- [ ] Day 1: write your starting point on one page, without editing it.
- [ ] Day 2: choose the provisional name and the three words for your voice.
- [ ] Day 3: fill in the empathy map for one specific person.
- [ ] Day 4: collect ten real phrases about the pain (comments, questions, messages).
- [ ] Day 5: describe the offer in terms of that pain, in one sentence.
- [ ] Day 6: review what you've learnt and write down two product hypotheses.
- [ ] Day 7: rest. Read through your notes and don't work on the project.

:::nota
Day 1. There's no right or wrong starting point. Write how you are, with your fears and doubts, and keep the page.
:::

## Part 2 · Brand and first product (days 8 to 18)

- [ ] Day 8: choose your three colours and two fonts.
- [ ] Day 9: define the table, the light and the object of your signature.
- [ ] Day 10: take the test photo of your hands and review it.
- [ ] Day 11: write the three adjectives for your voice, with examples.
- [ ] Day 12: complete the brand checklist.
- [ ] Day 13: ask ten people the validation questions.
- [ ] Day 14: analyse the answers and choose a format.
- [ ] Day 15: write the product outline in five points.
- [ ] Day 16: write the first part of the product.
- [ ] Day 17: sketch the ladder of value in three rungs.
- [ ] Day 18: prepare the free lead magnet, short and useful.

## Part 3 · Content, stories and DMs (days 19 to 27)

- [ ] Day 19: gather twenty real doubts and group them by theme.
- [ ] Day 20: write three hooks and rewrite them in your own style.
- [ ] Day 21: film a session of hands for several formats.
- [ ] Day 22: publish a thirty-second Reel with on-screen text.
- [ ] Day 23: publish a six-step carousel.
- [ ] Day 24: write the five-story sequence.
- [ ] Day 25: publish the sequence, with a question sticker.
- [ ] Day 26: write your three scripts (reply, discovery and exit).
- [ ] Day 27: reply to all the interactions from the last few days.

## Launch checklist

Before you announce the product, confirm each point. If one isn't done, it's not a failure. It's a sign to put the launch back by a week.

- [ ] The product is finished and reviewed by someone you trust.
- [ ] The price is set and written clearly, with no small print.
- [ ] You know how the person receives the product (file, link, access) and you've tested that route.
- [ ] The description says who the product is for, what it is and what it isn't.
- [ ] There's a clear sentence saying that results depend on each person's effort.
- [ ] If you've collected contacts, consent is recorded and there's a way to leave the list.
- [ ] You have template replies for the most common questions.
- [ ] You have a set day and time to launch, and a reply window.
- [ ] You know what you'll do if the launch goes worse than you hoped.

## My 30-day review

Reread your notebook from day 1. Write down three things you did that you're going to keep. Write down three things that didn't go as you expected, and why, honestly. Write down what you've learnt about your client, in the words you heard. Finally, choose one single next step for the next thirty days.

## Glossary

The terms below appear throughout the book. If any of them seems unfamiliar, come back here.

- **Visual signature.** The set of recurring elements (table, light, object, angle) that identify the brand.
- **B-roll.** Supporting shots, usually close-ups, that illustrate a process without dialogue.
- **Call to action (CTA).** The specific request you make at the end of a piece of content.
- **Consent.** A person's clear and voluntary agreement to receive messages or to have their data kept.
- **DM.** A direct, private message between two people on a social network.
- **Ladder of value.** The sequence of products and touchpoints, from the free one to the most complete.
- **Faceless.** Literally "without a face". A brand where the content shows hands, process and product, not the face of the person making it.
- **Follow-up.** A message sent after a conversation, to resume contact respectfully.
- **Hook.** The first line or first shot of a piece of content, which has to hold attention in a few seconds.
- **Lead magnet.** Free content, such as a guide or a checklist, that lets the person try your way of teaching.
- **Lifestyle business.** A business designed around the pace of life the person wants to have, with defined limits.
- **POV.** Point of view: footage shot from the angle of the eyes of the person doing the action.
- **GDPR.** The General Data Protection Regulation, which governs the collection and use of personal data in the European Union.
- **UGC.** Content created by creators for brands, which the brands use on their own channels.
- **Validation.** Confirming, before you build, that there is real demand for what you plan to offer.
- **Voiceover.** Recorded narration that accompanies images, without the person appearing on screen.
