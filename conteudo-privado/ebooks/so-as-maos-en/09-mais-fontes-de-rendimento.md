# More sources of income
> More channels mean more paths, and also more things to look after. Choose one at a time.

Think of Henrique, 36, who has had a digital product running for two months. One week he decided to open a shop on a marketplace, start a book, take on content work for brands and offer design services. At the end of a month he hadn't done any of them properly. He was tired, with four tabs open and none of them moving forward.

The temptation is strong. It seems sensible to spread the risk. In practice, spreading your attention across many channels tends to divide your time without multiplying it. In this chapter you'll see four options, the pros and cons of each, and a way to decide where to start. There are no promises here, only honest questions.

## Before you diversify

Diversifying makes sense once you have a foundation. That foundation can be a product validated by real people (chapter 04), a content routine you've kept up for a few weeks (chapter 06), or a way of talking to the people following you without feeling overwhelmed (chapter 08). If you don't have any of these yet, a new channel will take away the time you need to consolidate what you already have.

## The four options

**Marketplaces for digital products and crafts.** Platforms where people look for ready-made products, such as templates, prints or kits. They usually charge fees per listing and per sale, which change over time. Always check the terms on the official page before you decide.

**E-books and print on demand.** You publish a book with no stock, because printing happens as orders come in. Royalties depend on the price, the format and the market, and a title's success depends heavily on the niche, the title and the cover.

**UGC (content created for brands).** Making videos and photos that brands use on their own channels. It's paid per project and requires a portfolio, meeting deadlines and good communication. It isn't passive income. It's creative work on commission.

**Digital services.** Offering work such as social media management, design, copywriting or document organisation to individuals or businesses. It's the option that looks most like traditional service provision, with contracts, invoicing and a direct relationship with the client.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Option | Strengths | Watch out for | Best suited to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketplace | People already searching for that type of product | Fees, competition and little contact with buyers | Anyone with simple, repeatable products |
| E-book or print on demand | A product that stays available for a long time | A lot of writing and cover work before the first sale | Anyone with a niche topic who enjoys writing |
| UGC | Hands-on experience with brands and professional content | Depends on orders and a portfolio | Anyone already comfortable filming and editing |
| Digital services | Direct income and a close relationship with the client | Working time is directly tied to income | Anyone with a well-established skill |

This is how to read the table without fooling yourself. The strengths are real, but none of them takes the work off your plate.

:::alerta
There's no such thing as a passive channel. All of them demand creation time, attention to the page, replies to clients and reviewing rules that change. Be wary of anyone who sells you a channel as if it weren't work.
:::

## Tax and legal framework

Selling products or providing services, even online, usually brings tax and invoicing obligations in Portugal. This includes setting up your activity, issuing invoices and declaring your income on the IRS (Portugal's annual income tax return). The rules change, and the right setup depends on your specific situation. Don't make these decisions off the top of your head.

Speak to a certified accountant (contabilista certificado) before you start invoicing. An initial consultation is a small investment compared with the cost of sorting out a badly handled situation.

:::cartao Before you diversify
- Does your current channel already work with some regularity?
- Do you already have a product that someone has paid for?
- Do you know how many hours a week you can give to the new channel?
- Can you keep going for three months before you judge it?
:::

If the answer to the first question is no, the new channel won't solve the problem. It will only split your time further.

## How to decide

Answer these questions honestly, one option at a time:

1. Do I already have skills someone pays for, without having to learn them from scratch?
2. How many hours a week can I give this channel without taking time from the routine I already have?
3. Can I keep going for at least three months before I know whether it makes sense?
4. Does this channel depend entirely on me, or does it depend on third parties, such as platforms or brands?
5. Do I know where this channel's tax obligations sit, and who can help me organise them?

Results depend on each person's effort. No channel guarantees any amount, and the table is there to help you think, not to make promises.

## In summary

Diversify when you already have a validated base, not to escape a problem you haven't solved yet. Each channel needs time for creation, maintenance and attention. Tax and invoicing are part of the business. And choose one channel at a time, with your criteria defined before you start.
