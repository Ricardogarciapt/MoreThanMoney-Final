# Why the face isn't the product
> A face catches the eye. Hands show that there's work behind it.

Anyone who has worked in marketing for a while notices something odd. The people who buy the most rarely buy the face of the person selling. They buy the solution to a problem they have that week: the spreadsheet that won't add up, the training plan they never manage to stick to, the list of content ideas stuck in their head. The face was, almost always, a detail.

This chapter takes apart the idea that, to sell online, you have to appear with your face uncovered every day. You don't. But it isn't an easy shortcut either, so let's take it step by step.

## The myth of the compulsory face

There's a very common idea: to sell on social media, you have to be a "face", an influencer, someone who shows themselves completely. This idea holds some truth for certain businesses, where personality is the product. For most info products, it doesn't apply. A home organisation guide, an invoice template or a study planner are bought for what they do for the person buying them.

A face helps create connection. Connection is also built through your voice, your handwriting, the way you explain things, your consistency and the care you put into what you deliver.

:::alerta
Faceless doesn't mean without identity. Someone with no name, no story, no tone of voice and no particular way of seeing things doesn't build trust. You may not show your face, but you have to show that there's a real person on the other side.
:::

## The three myths that hold you back most

When I run workshops, I hear three sentences almost every time. They're worth looking at calmly.

| Myth | What happens in practice | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| "I need to look good in photos to sell." | People buy from whoever explains well, not from whoever looks good in a photo. | Focus on clarity: short sentences, concrete examples, one problem solved per post. |
| "Nobody trusts you without a face." | Trust is built through consistency, transparency and delivery. | Show the process, reply to messages and do what you promised. |
| "I have to post every day to exist." | Posting without a strategy wears you out and doesn't sell any more. | Choose a rhythm you can keep up for months and stick to it. |

None of these myths is about you. They describe a pattern other people have imposed on you, often without saying so.

:::cartao Sentences for the fridge
- I don't need to look good in a photo to be useful.
- Trust grows with what I deliver.
- I post at a pace I can keep up for months.

## The real advantages of faceless

I'm not selling faceless to you as the perfect solution. It has concrete advantages, which are worth knowing before you decide:

- **Privacy.** You don't expose your family, your home, your workplace or your children's routine. This matters a lot if your job doesn't yet know you have a side project.
- **Simpler production.** Content made with hands can be filmed in an afternoon and cut into several formats. It doesn't depend on you being in a good mood, on your hair or on studio lighting.
- **Focus on value.** The attention of the viewer goes to the problem and the product, not to your appearance or to comparisons.
- **Less comparison.** You don't have to compete with attractive faces or with lives that look perfect. You compete on the quality of what you deliver.

Inês is a fictional character who helps me explain this. Situations like hers are common.

:::historia
**Illustrative case: Inês.** Inês made recipe guides for friends and family. At first she posted photos of herself next to the kitchen table, and the comments were mostly about how she looked. When she started filming only her hands chopping vegetables and writing the step-by-step on screen, the questions changed. She stopped hearing "is that really you?" and started getting questions about the ingredients. The content became more useful, and attention went back to what she was teaching.
:::

## What actually sells

If you take the face out of the equation, what's left? Three things, which will come up throughout the book:

1. **The problem, properly understood.** The buyer feels that someone has grasped exactly what bothers them.
2. **Proof that you can solve it.** It might be the visible process, a real example of your work or a demonstration.
3. **The transformation.** The person can see the path between the before and the after, without exaggerated promises.

A face is one way of showing these three things. It isn't the only one, and for many products it isn't even the most effective.

:::destaque
You're not selling your face. You're selling the difference your product makes in the life of the person who buys it, and your hands are the proof that the difference is real.
:::

## In summary

- A face isn't compulsory for selling info products. Clarity, consistency and delivery matter more.
- Faceless has real advantages: privacy, simpler production and a focus on value.
- Faceless isn't anonymity. The buyer needs to feel there's a real person behind it.
- What sells is the problem solved, the proof of the process and the transformation of the buyer.

## To do today

1. Pick up your phone and look at the last five pieces of content you saved or watched to the end. Check which ones show hands, objects or process.
2. Write on paper the three words you would use to describe the way you explain things.
3. Choose a provisional name for the brand. You don't need to register it today.
