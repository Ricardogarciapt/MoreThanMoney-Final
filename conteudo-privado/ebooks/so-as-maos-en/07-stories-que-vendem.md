# Stories that sell
> A story doesn't sell on its own. It sells once the person already feels at home with you.

Think of Duarte, 34, who sells hand-made recipe notebooks in Braga. He was posting beautiful stories, with photos of the pages and the right music, and nobody replied. One afternoon he noticed that the stories with the most replies were the ones that ended with a simple question. He changed only the ending of each sequence, and conversations started to appear on their own. It wasn't a technical trick. It was putting the question in the right place.

Stories disappear after a few hours, so people look at them with a different kind of attention. It's a more intimate space than the feed. Anyone watching a story is looking into your day, your table and your hands in a real moment. That's an opportunity, and also a responsibility.

## Why the sequence matters

A post reaches a lot of people at once and then sinks among the others. A story reaches people who already follow you, usually appears at the top of the list and allows a direct reply. But a single story can be lovely and say nothing. Five stories with a clear logic build a small path, from interest to action.

## The five-story sequence

The sequence has five steps: hook, problem, proof, offer and action. These are Duarte's examples. Adapt them to your product, your number of pages and the way you deliver.

Story 1, the hook. Hands placing a pen on the table. It's there to catch the people who already follow you.

:::story
Still putting this off?
sticker: Yes | Not yet
:::

Story 2, the problem. Hands flicking through a notebook full of lists. It shows that you understand what the person is feeling. If you want to add a voiceover, something like "when we try to do everything at once, tiredness arrives fast."

:::story
Most people try to do everything at once and stop on day three.
sticker: What's your biggest doubt?
:::

Story 3, the proof. Hands putting together the first version of the guide. It shows process, never results you can't show.

:::story
This is version 1. I've already changed three things after listening to ten people.
:::

Story 4, the offer. Hands showing the guide open on the screen. It presents the product clearly.

:::story
12-page guide. Instant access.
:::

Story 5, the action. Hands typing a message on the phone. It asks for one specific reply, and only one.

:::story
Reply "I want it" and I'll send you access.
sticker: I want it | Later
:::

Story 3 doesn't say "I sold so much" or "I earned so much". It shows a real process. If you don't yet have a process to show, show the path you're on, without making anything up.

:::alerta
Never use fake countdowns, or "last spots" when it isn't true. Invented urgency wears out the people following you and can count as an unfair commercial practice.
:::

:::curso The five-story sequence
Hook, problem, proof, solution, invitation. Five stories, in this order. The proof is always true and has no invented numbers.

In the course: you build your first sequence with ready-made templates and publish it the same day.
:::

## Question and poll stickers

A question sticker asks the person to write their biggest doubt about the topic. A two-option poll tells you, in seconds, which pain is most common. Use these answers as raw material. They are the real language I talked about in chapter 02, and they form the basis for your next post or your next product.

:::destaque
Stickers are the simplest way to hear your client. Every reply is a clue for your next piece of content.
:::

## When someone replies

When someone replies to a story, the conversation starts. The rule is simple: thank them, ask a question and only then talk about the product. That's how Duarte built the habit, and the conversation below shows the same movement.

:::conversa Instagram · Lúcia
Lúcia: I haven't managed to start yet. I'm worried I won't have the time.
eu: Thanks for replying. What holds you back more, the time or not knowing where to begin?
Lúcia: I think not knowing where to begin.
eu: Then start with the first page, which is just a list. Would you like me to send you the guide?
Lúcia: I would, yes.
:::

:::historia
**Illustrative case: Teresa.** Teresa, a fictional person, created meal-planning templates. At first she posted beautiful stories, but with no question at the end, and almost nobody replied. She changed two things: she started using a question sticker in each sequence and writing a specific invitation in the last story. Replies became more frequent and more useful, and, in people's own words, she could see which topics should come first in her next template.
:::

## In summary

A story works like a conversation, and the five-step sequence takes the person from interest to action. Show real process in the proof and never invent results or urgency. Stickers are the best way to hear from the people following you. And the first reply should be a question.

## To do today

1. Choose a topic you've already worked on in the earlier chapters and write it in one sentence on your phone.
2. Write the five lines of the sequence: hook, problem, proof, offer and action.
3. Film the five stories with your phone propped against a box or a book, each with five or six seconds of hands.
4. Add a question sticker to the second story and a poll sticker to the last one.
5. Publish all five on the same day and reply to every response within the next two hours.
