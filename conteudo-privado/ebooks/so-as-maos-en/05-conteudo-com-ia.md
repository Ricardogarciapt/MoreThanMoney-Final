# Content with AI
> AI writes drafts. The person who decides what's true, and what sounds like you, is you.

Some people think AI will do everything on its own and that all you need is to press a button. Others are scared of any new tool and refuse to try it. Both positions are not much use to anyone who wants to sell without showing up.

In this chapter I'll show you a sensible way to use AI for content: as an assistant, with a clear workflow and human review at every step. AI helps you think faster. It doesn't think for you.

## AI as an assistant, not an author

The golden rule is simple: AI isn't the author. It's an assistant that gives you options, organises ideas and speeds up repetitive tasks. The authorship, the experience and the responsibility for what you publish are yours.

In practice, this means that:

- You never publish a generated text without reading it, correcting it and adding something of your own.
- You always check the facts, dates, numbers and names the tool gives you.
- You don't use AI to create client testimonials, screenshots of results or quotes from real people.
- When AI suggests something that sounds like a promise, you rewrite the sentence into something true and measured.

:::alerta
Never publish testimonials, results or numbers that AI has made up. If it didn't happen, it doesn't exist. It's the quickest way to lose the trust of the people who follow you, and it can cause legal problems, especially in advertising and commercial practices.
:::

## The four-step workflow

Content almost always goes through four stages: idea, script, design and publishing. At each one, AI can help, but the final decision is yours.

[[grafico:fluxo-conteudo]]

**1. Idea.** Gather twenty real questions your clients have asked. Ask AI to group them by theme and suggest different angles for each group. You choose three.

**2. Script.** Ask for a simple structure: hook, problem, step, close. Then write the text yourself, in your own words. If AI gives you a good sentence, rewrite it the way you would say it.

**3. Design.** Use AI to generate visual variations based on your palette and your typography. She Is Studio is the AI design studio from She Is Faceless Academy: it helps you create visual pieces that line up with your identity, trying out and comparing options. Always choose the one that feels most like you.

**4. Publishing.** Schedule it, read the final version out loud and confirm the time. This stage is human from start to finish.

## A prompt that works

A good prompt gives context, sets the tone and asks for a specific format. This template is an example. Adapt it to your own situation:

```
Act as a copywriter for a faceless brand, writing in British English.
Tone: direct, calm, practical. Address the reader as "you". No income promises.
Audience: [describe the person in two sentences].
Real doubt they have: [paste the exact sentence you heard].
Ask for: three hooks of up to ten words, and a 60-second script with on-screen text,
in five short blocks.
```

In the prompt, you don't ask for results, you don't ask for numbers and you don't ask for urgency. If you do, the tool makes them up, and you spend your time cleaning up.

## Review before you publish

Before you press the button, go through this table. It takes two minutes and avoids most mistakes.

| Step | Review question |
|---|---|
| Facts | Has every number, date and name been confirmed? |
| Tone | Does it sound like me, or like a machine imitating me? |
| Identity | Are the colour, lettering and visual signature right? |
| Promises | Is there any sentence that promises money, results or false urgency? |
| People | Has anyone recognisable appeared without permission? |
| Legal | Is there a disclaimer where the topic requires one? |

## In summary

- AI is an assistant. The authorship and the responsibility are yours.
- The workflow has four stages: idea, script, design and publishing.
- A good prompt gives context, tone and format, and doesn't ask for results or numbers.
- Never publish testimonials, numbers or results that AI has made up.
