# Before you start
> A book about hands does not need a face. It needs someone who decides to begin.

Hi, I'm Maria Costa, and I'll be your companion over the next few pages. I've been working in marketing since 2013 and, over the last few years, I've focused mostly on info products: guides, templates, planners and small courses that people buy to solve a specific problem. This book comes from a question people ask me all the time: "Maria, can you sell on social media without showing yourself?" You can. But the short answer hides a lot of work, and that's what you're about to read.

## What this book is

This is a practical book. It isn't loose theory or a manifesto about freedom. Each chapter has one central idea, an image that helps you remember it, and at least one exercise you have to do with your hands, with a pen or on your phone. Reading alone isn't enough.

By the end, I want you to have three things: a faceless brand that makes sense for you, a first product designed around the person who is going to buy it, and a routine of content and conversations you can keep up without burning out. Nobody can keep posting every day for months while pretending to enjoy it. We're going to do something smaller and more sustainable.

I call it *Just the Hands* because that's what you'll see in your posts: hands writing, packing, moving across a screen, holding a cup of coffee at seven in the morning. A face isn't compulsory. What sells is what those hands build and show.

## What this book is not

I want to be clear from the start:

- **It is not a promise of income.** I won't tell you how much you'll earn each month or how long it will take. That depends on your time, your product, your audience and work that only you can do. Results depend on each person's effort.
- **It is not financial, tax or legal advice.** When I talk about IRS, invoicing or contracts, I'm giving you general guidance. For specific decisions, speak to a certified accountant or a lawyer.
- **It is not a magic formula.** There's no trick that replaces consistency.

I use examples with numbers so we can do the maths, but I always label them "illustrative example". They are not anyone's results. The people in the stories are also fictional, inspired by situations you see every day, not real biographies.

## How to use each chapter

The chapters are numbered from 00 to 11, and the order matters, because each one builds on the one before. I suggest one chapter a week, but go at your own pace. Some people take a month and some take three. Both are fine.

You'll find some special blocks. The highlight holds the key idea of the chapter:

:::destaque
A key idea, in two or three sentences. If you only keep one thing from a chapter, keep this one.
:::

The exercises have numbered steps and space to write. The alerts warn you about common mistakes. There are also posts, phone screens and conversations, designed to read as if they came from your own phone. When one of these appears, read it slowly, as if it were meant for you.

## Before you move on: the notebook

Choose a notebook (or a document on your computer, if you prefer) and call it "Hands Diary". This is where you'll write your answers to the exercises. In four weeks you'll want to read what you wrote today, and you'll see the path you've taken.

## A note on the word "faceless"

*Faceless* means, literally, "without a face". In the way I use it, it's a brand where the person behind it exists, works and can be seen, but where the focus is on the process, the product, the hands and the value being delivered. It isn't about hiding yourself. It's about choosing where to shine the light.

Some people say faceless is a trick to avoid exposure. I think it's an honest way of deciding what you share. You decide how much of your personal life goes on the screen, and that is your right, not a flaw.

There are weeks when you miss showing your face and others when you're glad you don't have to. Both feelings are normal.

Rita, 38, a hairdresser in Leiria, is a fictional character. This is the conversation you might have with someone like her at the start:

## In summary

- This book is practical: each chapter has an idea, an image and an exercise.
- There are no income promises. The examples with numbers are illustrative.
- The cases are fictional, and results always depend on each person's effort.
- Use a notebook to keep your answers. You'll need them.
