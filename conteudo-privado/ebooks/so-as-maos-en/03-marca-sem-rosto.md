# A brand without a face
> A brand without a face needs a face made of habits: the colour, the lettering, the voice and the gesture that repeat.

When no face appears, identity starts living somewhere else. It moves into the background colour, the font of the headline, the way you write a "thank you" and the object that always sits on the same table. If none of this is defined, every post seems to come from a different person, and anyone following you feels lost.

This chapter is about making that identity exist on purpose. You don't need an expensive designer or an agency. You need simple decisions, made once, and the discipline to keep repeating them.

## What faceless identity is

Identity is, at heart, whatever repeats. In a faceless brand it usually rests on four pillars:

1. **Colour palette**, the set of colours you always use.
2. **Typography**, the typefaces you choose for headlines and body text.
3. **Voice**, the way you speak, your vocabulary and your tone.
4. **Visual signature**, the recurring elements that identify you: the hands, the objects, the camera angle and the style of text on screen.

None of these pillars is a work of art. They are choices made for consistency.

## Palette

Choose three colours: one for the background, one for the highlight and one neutral for secondary text. Before you decide, check that the text reads well against the background, especially on a phone in the sun.

An illustrative example: a cream background, dark brown text and a moss green for buttons and call-to-action arrows. These three colours feel calm and work well with photos of hands and natural materials. You don't need to copy this example. Choose what fits the way you are.

:::ecra Palette · Brand (example)
Background: #F4EDE1 (cream)
Text: #3B2A1E (dark brown)
Highlight: #4F6B4A (moss green)
Use: background, text and buttons. Nothing else.
:::

:::alerta
Don't choose colours just because they're trendy this month. A palette that changes from week to week is one nobody recognises. Change it only when you have a clear reason and the decision is written down.
:::

## Typography

Use two fonts at most: one for headlines, with more personality, and another for body text, cleaner and easier to read. There are good free options. What matters is size: on a phone screen, small text is tiring and people leave.

A practical rule: if you have to bring the screen closer to read it, make the letters bigger. If the text fills more than half the screen in a short video, cut words.

## Voice

Voice is the hardest part to define and the one that carries the most weight for trust. Choose three adjectives and, for each one, write an example sentence and a sentence you would never say.

| Adjective | I would say | I would not say |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | "This guide answers the overtime question in three steps." | "Maybe this guide could, in some way, help." |
| Calm | "No rush. One step at a time." | "Buy now or you'll lose everything!" |
| Practical | "Here's the template. Copy it and adapt it." | "Imagine every possibility in the universe." |

Also pay attention to the words you use to greet, thank and say goodbye. These little phrases repeat a lot, and they say more about you than a logo does.

:::destaque
Voice isn't what you say in a single post. It's what people hear from you after ten posts in a row.
:::

## Visual signature: the hands

In a faceless brand, the hands are your signature. But hands only work as a signature when they appear repeatedly. Choose:

- **The table.** Always the same one, or the same work surface. It can be a wooden tabletop or a smooth tablecloth.
- **The light.** Always the same source, ideally natural light from a window early in the morning or late in the afternoon.
- **The recurring object.** A particular pen, a notebook, a mug. It's the element people start to recognise.
- **The point of view.** Film from the angle of your eyes when you're working. Viewers feel they're sitting beside you.
- **The on-screen text.** Always the same typeface, the same position and the same size for key phrases.

This list might seem excessive, but it isn't. When someone sees three seconds of hands on a green notebook and knows it's your brand, the identity is already working.

- A reusable on-screen text template.

## In summary

- A brand without a face lives on repetition: colour, lettering, voice and visual signature.
- Choose three colours and two fonts at most, and check they read well on a phone.
- Voice is defined with adjectives, examples and clear limits.
- Hands only work as a signature when they always appear in the same setting.
