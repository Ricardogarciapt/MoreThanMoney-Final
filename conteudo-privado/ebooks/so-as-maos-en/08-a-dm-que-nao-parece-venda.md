# The DM that doesn't feel like a sales pitch
> A private message is a door. Knock, and wait to be invited in.

Think of Marta, 38, who sells a mini sewing course for beginners. One day she received a message from a stranger with a link, three exclamation marks and the word "TODAY" in capitals. She deleted it without reading to the end. The offer might well have been good. It was the form that put her off.

The direct message is where a sale looks most like a conversation, and where most people get it wrong. Either they write coldly, with a copy-and-paste message everyone recognises, or they're so afraid of bothering anyone that they never say what they're offering. Both drive away people who could have been clients.

This chapter teaches you to sell by DM in an ethical way: no spam, no pressure, and respect for each person's decision.

## Ask before you send

The conversation starts with permission. There are two ways this can happen. The person has interacted with you first, with a comment, a reply to a story or a request for information. Or you asked permission before sending the offer: "Can I send you the guide by message?"

If neither of these situations exists, don't send anything. You can post, reply to public comments, or create a way for people to come to you. The cold, mass DM is the shortest route to being blocked and to a bad reputation.

:::cartao DM rules
- No spam: never the same message to dozens of people with no connection to you.
- Reply first to the people who spoke to you. They come before any list.
- Always give a way out, and a graceful goodbye line is enough.
- Respect the time of day: between nine in the morning and eight in the evening is usually reasonable.
- Only keep someone's contact if they agreed to it. If they ask to leave, you leave without arguing.
:::

:::alerta
Sending the same message to two hundred people with no connection is spam. It can lead to reports, account restrictions and a loss of trust from the people following you. It can also breach data protection rules.
:::

## The DM funnel

A sales conversation has stages, and rushing usually costs you. Each stage has its own timing.

[[grafico:funil-dm]]

The five stages are: interaction, permission, conversation, offer and decision. The person buys, thinks it over or says no, and any of these answers is valid.

## Conversations that feel like conversations

These conversations are illustrative examples. Read them out loud before you use them, and swap the words for your own.

The first one starts with a comment on a video. Marta asks permission before sending the guide.

:::conversa Instagram · Joana
Joana: I saw your video of the hands with the needle. Is this for someone who has never picked up a machine?
eu: Thanks for commenting! Yes, I was mainly thinking of people starting from scratch. Would you like me to send the guide I mentioned in the video?
Joana: I really would.
eu: Here it is. If you get stuck while applying it, message me and we'll work it out together.
:::

The second shows a price objection. The reply doesn't argue about money. It offers a smaller first step.

:::conversa Instagram · Rui
Rui: It sounds good, but I don't have the money for it right now.
eu: I understand, and thanks for being honest. Would you like to start with the free guide? That way you can see whether my way of teaching suits you before you decide.
Rui: Maybe. I'll message you later.
eu: Sounds good. No rush at all.
:::

The third is a follow-up three days later, followed by a graceful exit. The person didn't reply, and the final message closes the door politely.

:::conversa Instagram · Sara
eu: Hi, just checking whether anything's unclear. If now isn't the right time, just tell me, no problem.
Sara: Not yet, I'm sorting out my week. Thanks.
eu: Thanks for your time. I'll leave it there. If you need me in future, you know where to find me.
:::

:::nota
When someone says no, thank them and finish. Don't come back with another way of saying the same thing.
:::

And now what not to do. This is an example of a cold message, sent to someone who has never spoken to you.

:::conversa Example of what not to do
eu: Hi!!! I saw you follow this topic. I have something AMAZING for you, today only, buy now!
Stranger: Who are you?
eu: Reply YES to get the link now!!!
:::

The message has no question, no context and no way out. The person receiving it feels they were picked from a list. So don't copy this model, not even to "test" it.

## Follow-up without being annoying

Follow-up is the part most people are afraid to do, and the part most people do badly. Simple rules: no more than two follow-ups per person, spaced a few days apart. Each one brings something new, such as a detail, an answer to a doubt or a different example. And if the person says no, you thank them and finish.

| Moment | Message | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Gentle offer | Open the conversation about the offer |
| Day 3 | Follow-up with a question | Remind them without pressure |
| Day 7 | Gentle exit | Close with respect |

:::historia
**Illustrative case: Afonso.** Afonso, a fictional person, used to send long messages full of explanations to anyone who asked him for the guide. The conversations were good, but hardly anyone moved forward. When he started asking just one discovery question before talking about the product, he realised people had doubts very different from the ones he'd imagined. He adjusted the offer to those doubts, and the conversations became shorter, clearer and more honest.
:::

## Consent and GDPR in a few lines

If you collect emails or contacts, you have to be transparent about what you'll do with them. Say who you are, why you want the contact and how often you'll write. Ask for clear agreement, for example with a tick box that isn't pre-ticked. Always offer an easy way out, and don't keep more data than you need.

For an assessment of your specific case, speak to a lawyer. Here you'll only find the general logic.

## To do today

1. Open your recent interactions (comments and replies to stories) and write a list of the people who have already spoken to you.
2. Reply to each one with a question, without mentioning the product yet.
3. Write a reply script for people who commented, in your own words, and save it in your phone's notes.
4. Write your exit message. If you can write it calmly, your DM rule is ready.
5. Don't send anything to someone who hasn't spoken to you. If you're unsure, ask first whether they'd like to receive it.
