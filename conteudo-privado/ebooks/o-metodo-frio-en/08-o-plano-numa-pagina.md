# The plan on one page

> If your plan does not fit on one page, you will not read it when you need it most.

A pilot with thousands of flight hours still reads the checklist before take-off. Not because they have forgotten it, but because they know that under pressure the mind skips steps. A trading plan is your take-off checklist. It is not a nice document to file away in a folder. It is a sheet you read before every session, until you know it by heart, and then you keep on reading it.

## The five parts

| Part | The question it answers | Illustrative example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Market and hour** | What do I trade, and when? | EUR/USD, 7:00am to 7:45am, Monday to Friday |
| **2. Setup** | What situation do I enter in? | Uptrend on the H4 + pullback to a support zone on the H1 |
| **3. Entry, stop and target** | Where do I enter, where do I get out if it goes wrong, where do I get out if it goes right? | Buy Limit at the zone; stop below the zone; target at the next resistance, minimum 1:1.5 |
| **4. Risk** | How much do I risk, and when do I stop? | 1% per trade; 2 losses in a row, I stop for the day; −4% in the week, I stop for the week |
| **5. Review** | When and how do I assess? | Sunday, 20 minutes, with the journal |

Notice what is not here: "feeling the market", "seeing how it goes", "it depends". If a rule needs interpreting in the moment, it is not a rule. It is an open door for emotion to walk through.

:::alerta Classic mistake · Having the plan in your head
"I know what I'm doing, I don't need to write it down." Right up until the day you lose two in a row and the plan in your head changes by itself: the stop widens, the lot goes up, the pair changes. A plan that is not written down does not protect you from yourself, because you are the one who rewrites it halfway through the storm.
:::

## The right goals

The goal of "making €300 a month" sounds objective and is the worst possible goal for a beginner. It does not depend on you, it depends on the market, and it pushes you to force trades when the month is going badly.

Swap outcome goals for **process** goals, which depend only on you:

- Follow the checklist in 100% of trades.
- Zero trades without a stop.
- Journal filled in on the same day, every time.
- Sunday review done four weeks in a row.

:::destaque
You do not control how much you make. You control whether you follow the plan. Measure what you control.
:::

## The plan as a card

Copy it, adapt it and print it. Keep it beside the screen.

:::cartao My cold plan
- Pair: ________ · Window: ________
- Setup: ____________________
- I enter with: ________ order at the zone
- Stop: where the idea fails · Target: minimum 1:1.5
- Risk: 1% · Lot from the formula
- I stop: 2 losses in a row / −4% in the week
- Review: Sunday, with the journal
:::

## When to change the plan

The plan can change, but never in the middle of a session and never because of a single trade. It changes on Sunday, during the review, with data:

1. You have at least 20 trades taken with the plan exactly as it is.
2. You look in the journal to see where the losses cluster (what time? what type of entry? what emotional state?).
3. You change **one** thing at a time and gather another 20 trades.

Changing everything at once is like changing your diet, your gym and your sleep schedule in the same week and then trying to work out what worked.

This chart helps explain why the minimum target of 1:1.5 is in the plan. With 1:1, you need to be right half the time just to break even, before costs. With 1:2, a third of the time is enough. This is not a promise that you will be right a third of the time. It is just the break-even arithmetic.

## The cold rule for this chapter

:::cartao Cold rule no. 8
The plan is written down, it fits on one page, and I read it before every session. I only change it on Sunday, with 20 trades of data, one thing at a time.
:::

## In short

- A plan has five parts: market and hour, setup, entry/stop/target, risk and review.
- Rules that need interpreting in the moment are not rules.
- Process goals, not outcome goals.
- The plan changes on Sunday, with data, one thing at a time.
