# The plan on one page

> If your plan does not fit on one page, you will not read it when you need it most.

A pilot with thousands of hours in the air still reads the checklist before take-off. It is not because they forgot. It is because they know that, under pressure, the mind skips steps. A trading plan is your take-off checklist. It is not a nice document to keep in a folder. It is a sheet you read before every session, until you know it by heart, and then you keep reading it.

## The five parts

| Part | The question it answers | Illustrative example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Market and time** | What do I trade and when? | EUR/USD, 7:00 to 7:45 AM, Monday to Friday |
| **2. Setup** | In what situation do I enter? | Uptrend on H4 + pullback to a support zone on H1 |
| **3. Entry, stop and target** | Where do I enter, where do I get out if it goes wrong, where do I get out if it goes right? | Buy Limit in the zone; stop below the zone; target at the next resistance, minimum 1:1.5 |
| **4. Risk** | How much do I risk and when do I stop? | 1% per trade; 2 losses in a row, I stop for the day; −4% in the week, I stop for the week |
| **5. Review** | When and how do I assess? | Sunday, 20 minutes, with the journal |

Notice what is not here: "feel the market", "see how it goes", "it depends". If a rule needs interpretation in the moment, it is not a rule. It is an open door for emotion to walk in.

:::alerta Classic mistake · Having the plan in your head
"I know what I'm doing, I don't need to write it down." Until the day you lose two in a row and the plan in your head changes on its own: the stop widens, the lot goes up, the pair changes. A plan that is not written does not protect you from yourself, because you are the one who rewrites it in the middle of the storm.
:::

## The right goals

The goal "earn €300 a month" sounds objective and is the worst possible goal for someone starting out. It does not depend on you, it depends on the market, and it pushes you to force trades when the month is going badly.

Swap outcome goals for **process** goals, which depend only on you:

- Follow the checklist on 100% of trades.
- Zero trades without a stop.
- Journal filled in on the same day, always.
- Sunday review done four weeks in a row.

:::destaque
You do not control how much you earn. You control whether you follow the plan. Measure what you control.
:::

## The plan in card form

Copy, adapt and print. Keep it next to the screen.

:::cartao My cold plan
- Pair: ________ · Window: ________
- Setup: ____________________
- I enter with: ________ order at the zone
- Stop: where the idea fails · Target: minimum 1:1.5
- Risk: 1% · Lot from the formula
- I stop: 2 losses in a row / −4% in the week
- Review: Sunday, with the journal
:::

:::historia Marta, day 13
On a day off, Marta took her notebook and wrote the plan on one page. Pair: EURUSD. Window: 7:15 to 7:45 AM, only on days without a morning shift. Setup: trend on H4, pullback to a zone drawn on Sunday. Entry with a Buy Limit or Sell Limit, never at market. Risk 1%, lot from the formula, volume read out loud. Two losses, the day is closed. She printed it, stuck it on the fridge door and took a photo to use as her phone wallpaper. That way, whenever she opened MetaTrader, the plan would be the first thing she saw.
:::

## When to change the plan

The plan can change, but never in the middle of a session and never because of a single trade. It changes on Sunday, at the review, with data:

1. Collect at least 20 trades made with the plan exactly as it is.
2. Look in the journal at where the losses cluster (what time? what kind of entry? what emotional state?).
3. Change **one** thing at a time, and collect 20 more trades.

Changing everything at once is like changing your diet, your gym and your sleep schedule in the same week and then trying to work out what worked.

## From my life: three phrases that held me

For fourteen years in the GNR (Portuguese National Guard) I was guided by three sayings. I never wrote them on paper, but I knew them by heart:

:::cartao The three mottos
- There is always someone worse off than us.
- Where one eats, two can eat.
- Time does not stop.
:::

The first one took away my self-pity on bad days. The second reminded me that what I have is shared. The third pushed me when I wanted to put things off. A trading plan works the same way: a few sentences, always said the same way, that you decide on when you are calm, so they can hold you when you are not.

## The cold rule of this chapter

:::cartao Cold rule no. 8
The plan is written, fits on one page and I read it before every session. I only change it on Sunday, with 20 trades of data, one thing at a time.
:::

## In short

- A plan has five parts: market and time, setup, entry/stop/target, risk and review.
- Rules that need interpretation in the moment are not rules.
- Process goals, not outcome goals.
- The plan changes on Sunday, with data, one thing at a time.
