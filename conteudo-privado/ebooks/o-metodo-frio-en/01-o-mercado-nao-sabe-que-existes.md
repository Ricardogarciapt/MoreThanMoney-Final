# The market does not know you exist

> When you buy euros against dollars, there is someone on the other side who is convinced of the opposite. At least one of you is wrong.

Say you go to London on holiday and exchange €500 at the airport. You get pounds, pay a fee you do not really understand and carry on with your trip. Without noticing, you have just taken part in the biggest market in the world. Forex is exactly this: swapping one currency for another. The difference is that the tourist swaps because they need pounds, and the trader swaps because they think the price is going to move.

According to the Bank for International Settlements' 2022 survey, the currency market turns over about $7.5 trillion a day. Central banks, commercial banks, companies paying overseas suppliers, funds and, at the very end of the queue, us: small traders with an app on our phones.

This number is for one thing only. To understand that your order of 0.01 lots moves nothing. The market does not know you exist. It is not going to "hunt your stop" because it is you, and it is not going to turn against you because you are watching. This sounds cold, and it is. It is also liberating: you stop taking the market personally.

## The pairs

In Forex you always trade in pairs. EUR/USD at 1.0850 means that 1 euro is worth 1.0850 dollars.

- The first currency is the **base** (EUR).
- The second is the **quote** (USD).
- **Buying** EUR/USD is betting that the euro will strengthen against the dollar.
- **Selling** EUR/USD is betting on the opposite. And yes, you can sell without owning euros: you are trading a contract for difference (CFD), not buying banknotes.

[[foto: mt5-cotacoes-telemovel.jpg | MetaTrader 5 on a phone: quotes on the left, with the sell price and the buy price side by side. Image: MetaQuotes (metatrader5.com). | larga]]

In the Market Watch window, each pair shows two numbers: the sell price (bid) and the buy price (ask). For example, EUR/USD at 1.08502 / 1.08510 (illustrative values). The difference between them is the **spread**, and it is the first thing you pay on every trade: here, 0.8 pips.

| Group | Examples | What you should know |
|---|---|---|
| Majors | EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY | Lowest spreads, most liquidity, the best ones to start with |
| Crosses | EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY | No dollar; some move a lot |
| Exotics | USD/TRY, EUR/ZAR | Wide spreads and sudden jumps: stay away at the beginning |
| Gold | XAU/USD | Not a currency, but traded alongside them; volatile |

:::alerta Classic mistake · Starting with the most "exciting" pair
Someone starts by watching a video of gold or GBP/JPY making huge moves and immediately wants that one. It is like passing your driving test and going straight onto the motorway at rush hour. Pairs that move a lot punish size and stop mistakes much harder. Start with a major, with a low spread, and learn the rule before the adrenaline.
:::

## When the market is awake

Forex runs 24 hours a day, five days a week, because the day rolls across the financial centres: Sydney, Tokyo, London, New York. But not all hours are the same.

[[grafico:sessoes-forex]]

For anyone living in Portugal, there is good news: the busiest window for the euro and dollar pairs, when London and New York are open at the same time, falls roughly between 1pm and 5pm (Portuguese time). The bad news is that it is mid-afternoon, when most of us are at work. That is why, in the chapter on the plan, you will choose a short, fixed window, instead of checking your phone all day long.

:::nota
A market open 24 hours does not mean "I have to be watching for 24 hours". It means I choose my hour and ignore the others.
:::

## Who is on the other side

When you open a trade with a retail broker, the broker is either your counterparty or passes your order on to liquidity providers. For you, in practice, this means three things:

1. **Choose a regulated broker** and check on the regulator's website that the entity you open your account with is really the one it claims to be. In Portugal, the CMVM (the securities regulator) publishes public warnings about unauthorised entities.
2. **Read the risk information document** the broker gives you. The percentage of retail accounts that lose money is in there.
3. **Your account is yours.** At MoreThanMoney, the broker route we use is PU Prime, and the account always stays in your name, with the broker. Never hand your money to a person to "manage it for you".

## In short

- Forex is swapping one currency for another; the trader swaps because they think the price is going to move.
- Every price has two sides, buy and sell, and the difference is the spread.
- Start with a major pair. Exotics and gold can wait.
- For us in Portugal, the most active window is roughly 1pm to 5pm.
- A regulated broker, an account in your own name, and never money handed to third parties.
