# A cool head

> You do not need to stop feeling fear. You need to stop letting it press the button.

Reading a book does not make anyone immune to emotion. Fear still shows up when the price gets close to the stop, greed still shows up when a trade is winning, and anger still shows up after three losses in a row. The cold method does not remove the emotions. It takes away their access to the button.

## The five emotions that press the button for you

| Emotion | How it shows up | What it makes you do | The rule that stops it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fear** | "What if it hits the stop?" | Closing too early, not entering the right setup | The stop is already set; the maximum loss has already been accepted |
| **Greed** | "This is going to go much further" | Taking the target off, increasing the lot | The target belongs to the plan, not to the moment |
| **FOMO** | "I'm missing the move" | Entering late, outside the plan | If it was not in the plan, it does not exist |
| **Revenge** | "The market owes me this one" | Increasing the lot to win it back | After 2 losses, I close the screen |
| **Euphoria** | "I'm unstoppable" | Trading more, risking more | After 3 wins, the next trade is treated like all the others |

:::alerta Classic mistake · Trading tired or with a hot head
You get home after a hard day, open MT5 "just to have a look" and half an hour later you have two open trades that were not in the plan. Tiredness does not show up on the chart, but it shows up in the journal. If your emotional state is 1 or 2 out of 5, MT5 does not open. This is not weakness. It is risk management applied to your nervous system.
:::

## Think in series, not in trades

Kahneman, in "Thinking, Fast and Slow", shows how the brain gives far more weight to a loss than to a gain of the same size. In trading, that means every stop hurts more than it should, and you tend to do silly things to avoid the next pain.

The way to counter this is to change the unit of measurement. Stop judging each trade. Judge series of 20. One lost trade says nothing. Twenty trades with the plan followed say a lot.

[[grafico:sequencia-perdas]]

Flip a coin a hundred times and the probability of at least five "losses" in a row is about 81%. Bad sequences are not a sign that the plan has failed. They are what you should expect. What matters is whether your account can survive that sequence without you breaking the rules.

:::destaque
A loss with the plan followed is the cost of being in the business. A loss outside the plan is a lesson you have not yet learnt.
:::

## The stopping rules

These rules are dull and worth their weight in gold:

1. **Two losses in a row on the same day: the day is over.** You close MT5 and go and do something else.
2. **−4% in the week: the week is over.** You use the rest of the week to review your journal.
3. **Emotional state below 3: you do not open MT5.**
4. **After a very good trade: a 15-minute break** before the next one. Euphoria lies too.

:::conversa WhatsApp · MTM study group
Rui: Guys, two losses first thing this morning. I feel like jumping back in with 0.10 to win it back.
eu: What does your plan say after 2 losses?
Rui: Close the day 😩
eu: Then close it. The market will still be there tomorrow. Your account needs to be there too.
Rui: Done. Off to the gym.
:::

Rui is an example character, but this conversation is one of the most common there is. Having someone you can say out loud "I'm about to break the rule" stops half the stupid mistakes.

## Routines that cool you down

- **Before the session**: read the plan out loud, take three deep breaths, write your emotional state in the journal.
- **During**: one trade at a time; after clicking, leave the chart. Staring at every tick does not change the result, it only changes your heart rate.
- **After**: journal filled in, MT5 closed, the end-of-window alarm respected.

There is a book I recommend to anyone who wants to go deeper on this: "The Daily Trading Coach" by Brett Steenbarger, a psychologist who works with traders. Each chapter is a short lesson to apply the next day.

## The cold rule for this chapter

:::cartao Cold rule no. 10
Two losses: I close the day. −4% in the week: I close the week. State below 3: I do not open MT5. I judge in series of 20, never a single trade on its own.
:::

## In short

- Emotions do not disappear; the method takes away their access to the button.
- Fear, greed, FOMO, revenge and euphoria each have their own rule.
- The brain weighs losses more heavily; judge series, not trades.
- Daily and weekly stopping rules, in writing.
- A short routine before, during and after the session.
