# The cool head

> You do not need to stop feeling fear. You need to stop letting it press the button.

Nobody becomes immune to emotions by reading a book. Fear still shows up when the price nears your stop, greed still shows up when the trade is winning, and anger still shows up after three losses in a row. The cold method does not remove emotions. It takes away their access to the button.

## The five emotions that press the button for you

| Emotion | How it shows up | What it makes you do | The rule that stops it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fear** | "What if it hits the stop?" | Closing too early, not entering the right setup | The stop is already set; the maximum loss has already been accepted |
| **Greed** | "This is going to rise much further" | Taking the target off, increasing the lot | The target belongs to the plan, not to the moment |
| **FOMO** | "I'm missing the move" | Entering late, outside the plan | If it was not in the plan, it does not exist |
| **Revenge** | "The market owes me this one" | Increasing the lot to win it back | After 2 losses, I close the screen |
| **Euphoria** | "I'm unstoppable" | Trading more, risking more | After 3 wins, the next one is the same as the others |

:::alerta Classic mistake · Trading tired or with a hot head
You get home after a hard day, open MT5 "just to have a look" and, half an hour later, you have two open trades that were not in the plan. Tiredness does not show on the chart, but it shows in the journal. If your emotional state is 1 or 2 out of 5, MT5 does not open. This is not weakness. It is risk management applied to your nervous system.
:::

## Think in series, not in trades

Kahneman, in "Thinking, Fast and Slow", shows how the brain gives far more weight to a loss than to a gain of the same size. In trading, that means every stop hurts more than it should, and you tend to do stupid things to avoid the next pain.

The way to counter this is to change the unit of measurement. Stop evaluating each trade. Evaluate series of 20. One lost trade says nothing. Twenty trades with the plan followed say a lot.

[[grafico:sequencia-perdas]]

Flip a coin a hundred times and the probability of at least five "losses" in a row is about 81%. Bad streaks are not a sign that the plan has failed. They are what you should expect. What matters is whether your account can survive that streak without you breaking the rules.

:::destaque
A loss with the plan followed is the cost of being in the business. A loss outside the plan is a lesson you have not learned yet.
:::

## The stopping rules

These rules are boring and worth their weight in gold:

1. **Two losses in a row on the same day: the day is over.** You close MT5 and go and do something else.
2. **−4% in the week: the week is over.** You use the rest of the week to review the journal.
3. **Emotional state below 3: you do not open MT5.**
4. **After a very good trade: a 15-minute break** before the next one. Euphoria lies too.

:::conversa WhatsApp · MTM study group
Rui: Guys, two losses first thing this morning. I feel like going in again with 0.10 to win it back.
eu: What does your plan say to do after 2 losses?
Rui: Close the day 😩
eu: Then close it. The market will still be there tomorrow. Your account needs to be there too.
Rui: Done. Going to the gym.
:::

Rui is an example character, but this conversation is one of the most common there is. Having someone you can say "I'm about to break the rule" out loud to stops half the mistakes.

:::historia Marta, day 24
Two losses before 8:00 AM. Both with the plan, both with a stop, €5 each. Marta felt the familiar urge: "the third one will win it all back". She glanced at the phone's wallpaper, where the plan was, and read the line "Two losses, close the day". Instead of just closing the app, she went to Settings and logged out of the account. To log back in, she would have to find the password in her notebook, and those thirty seconds of friction were enough. She wrote in the journal: emotional state 2, rule followed. It was the first time she felt proud of a negative day.
:::

## Routines that cool you down

- **Before the session**: read the plan out loud, take three deep breaths, write the emotional state in the journal.
- **During**: one trade at a time; after clicking, leave the chart and let the chapter 7 alerts call you. Staring at every tick does not change the result, it only changes your heartbeat.
- **After**: journal filled in, MT5 closed, end-of-window alarm respected.

There is a book I recommend to anyone who wants to go deeper: *The Daily Trading Coach* by Brett Steenbarger, a psychologist who works with traders. Each chapter is a short lesson to apply the next day.

## From my life: the year everything came together

[[foto: rg-pb.jpg | Cool doesn't mean not feeling. It means feeling and deciding at the same time. | lado]]

On my daughter's birthday, Liliana's cancer was already suspected. That same day my nephew had a car accident. The girl who was with him died. He was in a coma and today he uses a wheelchair. Constança was three years old. In November the diagnosis was confirmed and the treatments began.

By then I no longer had any living adoptive family. My grandmother died of old age while I was on the Marines (Fuzileiros) course. My sister Carla died of lung cancer in 2019, in the same month as my mother. I only met my biological family after my mother's funeral, and we didn't stay in touch. We moved to Santarém looking for support, we sold the house we loved, and the money and the time didn't stretch to anything.

I was also carrying an assault I suffered while off duty, because of my job. It left marks that took me years to treat. I won't go into it here.

I took all of my parental leave and I was there. And it was in that year, the worst of all, that I learned the most important rule in this book: emotions don't switch off, you manage them. Whoever decides with a boiling head loses, in the market and in life. The stopping rules in this chapter are the ones I needed when everything came together.

## The cold rule of this chapter

:::cartao Cold rule no. 10
Two losses: I close the day. −4% in the week: I close the week. Emotional state below 3: I do not open MT5. I judge series of 20, never a single trade.
:::

## In short

- Emotions do not disappear; the method takes away their access to the button.
- Fear, greed, FOMO, revenge and euphoria each have their own rule.
- The brain weighs losses more heavily; judge series, not trades.
- Daily and weekly stopping rules, written down.
- A short routine before, during and after the session.
