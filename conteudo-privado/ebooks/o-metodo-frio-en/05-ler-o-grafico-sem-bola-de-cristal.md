# Reading the chart without a crystal ball

> The chart does not tell you what is going to happen. It tells you where other people have already changed their minds.

Some people look at a chart the way others read their horoscope. They look for a pattern, a shape, some secret signal that says "now it goes up". There is no such thing. What the chart shows you is a record: where the price has been, where it stopped, where it turned back. From there, your job is to mark the zones where it makes sense to act and to decide in advance what you will do if the price gets there.

Reading a chart, in the cold method, means drawing a map. It does not mean guessing the destination.

## The candle

Each candle shows one period (an hour, on the H1):

- **Body**: between the open and the close. Gold (or green) if it closed above the open; dark (or red) if it closed below.
- **Wicks**: the highest and lowest points the price reached in that period.

A long wick at the top means the price rose and was pushed back down before the candle closed. It is information, not prophecy.

## Trend: highs and lows

The simplest way to read direction is to look at the highs and the lows:

| Trend | Highs | Lows | What you look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Up** | higher and higher | higher and higher | buys on pullbacks |
| **Down** | lower and lower | lower and lower | sells on rallies |
| **Sideways** | roughly the same level | roughly the same level | stay out, or trade the edges carefully |

Always start with the bigger chart (H4 or daily) to find the direction, and only then drop down to the H1 to choose the moment. Trading against the direction of the bigger chart is rowing against the tide with a plastic oar.

:::nota
First the map (H4 or D1). Then the street (H1). Never the other way round.
:::

## Support and resistance are zones, not lines

**Support** is a zone where the price has stopped falling. **Resistance** is a zone where it has stopped rising. Draw them as bands, not as thin lines: the price rarely respects an exact number to the pip.

1. On the H4 chart, look for two or three places where the price has reversed more than once.
2. Draw a rectangle that covers the wicks and bodies in that zone.
3. Repeat above and below the current price. Two zones on each side are enough.

When a resistance is broken with force, it often starts working as support, and the other way round. But "often" is not "always", and that is why the stop exists.

## Fibonacci, without the mysticism

The Fibonacci retracement tool marks percentage levels of a pullback: 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% are the most used. The maths comes from the Fibonacci sequence, but the reason it sometimes works is simpler: a lot of people look at the same levels.

1. In an uptrend, connect the low to the high of the last move.
2. MT5 draws the levels. Check whether any of them coincides with a support zone you have already drawn.
3. A level that lines up with a zone is an interesting place to plan an entry. A level on its own, no.

:::alerta Classic mistake · Filling the chart with indicators
RSI, MACD, three moving averages, bands, stochastic. The screen becomes a Christmas tree and the decision gets harder, not easier, because there is always one indicator saying yes and another saying no. You pick whichever one proves you right. That is confirmation bias with flashing lights. In the cold method, the chart has candles, zones and, at most, one tool.
:::

## In short

- The chart is a record of where the price changed direction, not a crystal ball.
- Highs and lows tell you the trend; always start with the bigger chart.
- Support and resistance are drawn as zones.
- Fibonacci is worth something when it lines up with a zone; on its own, no.
- Fewer indicators, fewer excuses.
