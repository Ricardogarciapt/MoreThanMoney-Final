# The journal, the mirror that does not lie

> Memory is the defence lawyer. The journal is the judge.

Ask a trader how their month went and they will remember the two good trades. The five bad ones, the ones they entered outside the plan, the ones where they moved the stop, memory files away in a drawer it rarely opens. This is not dishonesty. It is the brain protecting you. The problem is that in trading, that protection is expensive: if you cannot see the mistake, you will repeat it.

The journal is the only tool that shows you who you are when you are trading, and not who you think you are.

## What to record

You do not need expensive software. A spreadsheet, a notebook or a note on your phone will do. What matters is that you always record it, on the same day.

| Field | Illustrative example |
|---|---|
| Date and time | 12/03, 07:22 |
| Pair and direction | EUR/USD, buy |
| Setup from the plan? | Yes, pullback to H1 support with an uptrend on the H4 |
| Entry, stop, target | 1.0820 / 1.0795 / 1.0870 |
| Lot and risk | 0.02 / $5 |
| Result | −25 pips / −$5 |
| Did I follow the plan? | Yes |
| Emotional state (1 to 5) | 4, calm |
| Screenshot before and after | yes |
| One line of learning | "The zone broke on news I did not see on the calendar." |

Look at the column "Did I follow the plan?". It is the most important one in the sheet. A loss with the plan followed is a normal cost of the business. A win with the plan broken is a problem waiting to happen.

:::alerta Classic mistake · Only recording the good trades
The journal fills up with wins and the account keeps going down. When you only record what goes well, the journal becomes a photo album of your holidays. Nice to look at, no use at all. Record everything, especially the trades you are ashamed of. Those are the ones that will teach you the most.
:::

## The screenshot

Always take two screenshots: one before you enter (with the zones, stop and target drawn in) and one after the trade closes. Save them in a folder for each month. In three months' time you will leaf through that folder and recognise patterns in your own behaviour that no book could show you.

:::ecra MetaTrader 5 · History (illustrative example)
12/03 EUR/USD buy 0.02: −$5.00
13/03 EUR/USD sell 0.02: +$9.80
14/03 EUR/USD buy 0.02: −$5.00
15/03 EUR/USD buy 0.02: +$10.20
Week: 4 trades · 2 / 2
Result: +$10.00
:::

Two wins, two losses, and the week closed positive because the gains were double the losses. An illustrative example to show the logic, not a forecast.

## The Sunday review

Twenty minutes, on Sunday, with a coffee:

1. Count the trades of the week and how many followed the plan.
2. Split the losses into two piles: "with plan" and "without plan".
3. Read the emotional state column. Is there any link between the low numbers and the losses outside the plan?
4. Write down **one** thing you will do differently next week.

:::nota
This week: 6 trades, 5 with the plan. The one that did not follow it was on Friday at 4pm, tired, after work. Next week: on Friday afternoons I do not open MT5.
:::

## The numbers that matter

At the end of a month, work out three simple numbers:

- **Plan adherence rate**: trades taken with the plan ÷ total trades. Target: 100%.
- **Win rate**: winning trades ÷ total trades.
- **Average win ÷ average loss**: if it is greater than 1, when you win you win more than you lose when you lose.

With these three numbers, the conversation with yourself stops being "I think I'm getting better" and becomes "I am following the plan 85% of the time, and all the losses outside the plan happen on Fridays".

## In short

- Memory protects the ego; the journal protects the account.
- Record everything, including your emotional state and whether you followed the plan.
- Two screenshots per trade: before and after.
- A 20-minute Sunday review, with one single change.
- Three numbers a month: adherence, win rate and average win ÷ average loss.
