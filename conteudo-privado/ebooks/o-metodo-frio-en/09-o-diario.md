# The journal, the mirror that does not lie

> Memory is the defence lawyer. The journal is the judge.

Ask a trader how the month went and they will remember the two good trades. The five bad ones, the ones entered outside the plan, the ones where the stop was moved, memory files away in a drawer it rarely opens. This is not dishonesty. It is the brain protecting you. The problem is that in trading that protection is expensive: if you cannot see the mistake, you repeat it.

The journal is the only tool that shows you who you are when you are trading, and not who you think you are.

## What to record

You do not need expensive software. A spreadsheet, a notebook or a note on your phone will do. What matters is recording every time, on the same day.

| Field | Illustrative example |
|---|---|
| Date and time | 12 Mar, 7:22 AM |
| Pair and direction | EUR/USD, buy |
| Plan setup? | Yes, pullback to H1 support with an H4 uptrend |
| Entry, stop, target | 1.0820 / 1.0795 / 1.0870 |
| Lot and risk | 0.02 / $5 |
| Result | −25 pips / −5 $ |
| Did I follow the plan? | Yes |
| Emotional state (1 to 5) | 4, calm |
| Screenshot before and after | yes |
| One line of learning | "The zone was broken by news I did not see on the calendar." |

Look at the "Did I follow the plan?" column. It is the most important one in the sheet. A loss with the plan followed is a normal cost of the business. A gain with the plan broken is a problem waiting to happen.

:::alerta Classic mistake · Only recording the good trades
The journal fills up with wins and the account keeps falling. When you only record what goes well, the journal becomes a photo album of your holidays. Pretty, useless. Record everything, especially the trades you are ashamed of. Those are the ones that will teach you the most.
:::

## The screenshot

Always take two screenshots: one before entering (with the zones, the stop and the target drawn) and one after the trade closes. Save them in a folder per month. In three months, you will flick through that folder and recognise patterns in yourself that no book could show you.

:::ecra MetaTrader 5 · History (illustrative example)
12 Mar EUR/USD buy 0.02: −5.00 $
13 Mar EUR/USD sell 0.02: +9.80 $
14 Mar EUR/USD buy 0.02: −5.00 $
15 Mar EUR/USD buy 0.02: +10.20 $
Week: 4 trades · 2 / 2
Result: +10.00 $
:::

Two wins, two losses, and the week closed positive because the gains were double the losses. Illustrative example, to show the logic, not a forecast.

## On the screen: History, the raw material of the journal

The fourth button in the bottom bar, **History**, keeps everything that has closed. You do not need to remember anything: the app remembers for you.

1. Tap **History** and choose the period at the top: today, last week, last month or a range of your choice.
2. At the top you see the summary for the period: profit, deposits, balance. Below, each trade with pair, direction, volume, prices and result.
3. Tap a trade to see the opening and closing time, the stop and the target.
4. Copy that data into the journal on the same day. The "Did I follow the plan?" column and the emotional state, those only you can write.

On the PC it is even quicker: in the toolbox, **History** tab, right-click › **Report**. MT5 generates a file with all the trades for the period, which you can open in a spreadsheet.

For screenshots: on the phone, the normal screen capture works. On the PC, right-click the chart › **Save As Picture**, and the image keeps your zones, stop and target drawn on it.

:::historia Marta, day 21
Three weeks of journal, and Marta did what the chapter asked: she opened the month's History and split the losses into two piles. The losses with the plan were scattered across the week. The losses without the plan were all in the same place: Fridays, after night shifts, between 4:00 and 6:00 PM. No new strategy would ever have shown her this. She wrote the one change for the following week: "Friday after a night shift, MetaTrader does not open."
:::

## The Sunday review

Twenty minutes, on Sunday, with a coffee:

1. Count the week's trades and how many followed the plan.
2. Split the losses into two piles: "with plan" and "without plan".
3. Read the emotional state column. Is there any link between the low numbers and the losses without a plan?
4. Write **one** thing you will do differently next week.

:::nota
This week: 6 trades, 5 with the plan. The one that did not follow it was on Friday at 4:00 PM, tired, after work. Next week: on Friday afternoons I do not open MT5.
:::

## The numbers that matter

At the end of a month, calculate three simple numbers:

- **Plan adherence rate**: trades with the plan ÷ total trades. Target: 100%.
- **Win rate**: winning trades ÷ total trades.
- **Average win ÷ average loss**: if it is above 1, when you win you win more than you lose when you lose.

With these three numbers, the conversation with yourself stops being "I think I'm getting better" and becomes "I follow the plan 85% of the time and all my losses outside the plan are on Fridays".

## In short

- Memory protects the ego; the journal protects the account.
- Record everything, including your emotional state and whether you followed the plan.
- Two screenshots per trade: before and after.
- A 20-minute Sunday review, with one single change.
- Three numbers a month: adherence, win rate and average win ÷ average loss.
