# From demo to real

> The real account does not teach you anything new. It only shows you, with money, what you have not yet learned on the demo.

Almost everyone feels the same rush after two weeks on a demo: "I've got it, now I want to do it for real". Two weeks of demo is about ten trades. Ten trades say nothing about you, the plan or the market. They only say that you made ten trades.

Moving to a real account is not a promotion for good behaviour. It is a test with criteria.

:::alerta Classic mistake · Moving to a real account too early
The demo went well for a week and the urge to "win for real" takes over. The real account opens with a bigger deposit than planned, the lot goes up "because now it's for real", and the emotions that did not exist on the demo all show up on the same day. The demo is for making mistakes cheaply. Leave it when your numbers show you are ready, not when the urge demands it.
:::

## The passing criteria

You only move to a real account when you meet **all** of these:

1. **At least 40 trades** on the demo with the same written plan.
2. **Plan adherence of 90% or more** over the last 20.
3. **Zero trades without a stop** across the whole period.
4. **A complete journal**, with a screenshot before and after, for every trade.
5. **You respected the stopping rules** every time they were triggered.
6. **Average win greater than average loss** over the last 20 (before you give weight to the win rate).

Notice that no criterion is "I made X €". A demo with profit and 60% adherence is a worse sign than a slightly negative demo with 95% adherence. The first had luck. The second has a process.

:::cartao Passing criteria
- 40 trades with the same plan
- Adherence ≥ 90% over the last 20
- Zero trades without a stop
- Complete journal
- Stopping rules respected
- Average win > average loss
:::

:::historia Marta, day 30
At the end of thirty days Marta had 19 trades, not 40. Adherence of 95% over the last ten, zero trades without a stop, complete journal. The demo was slightly negative. For the first time, she read those numbers without getting angry: trades were missing, not discipline. She decided to stay on the demo for another month with the same plan. She did not change the pair, the window or the lot. The book ended there. Her method was only just beginning.
:::

## How to start on a real account

1. **Deposit only what you are willing to lose** without touching your income, your bills or your emergency fund. If you do not yet have an emergency fund, *"I Destroyed It All. And I'm Glad."* has a whole chapter on it, and it comes before trading.
2. **Start with the minimum lot** for the first 20 real trades, even if the 1% formula allows more. The first phase of a real account is for getting your nervous system used to it, not for your wallet.
3. **Keep exactly the same plan** as on the demo. Changing the plan when you change the account means throwing away everything you trained.
4. **Compare adherence week by week** between the demo and the real account. If it drops, go back to the demo. It is not a step backwards. It is maintenance.

:::destaque
The first real account is not for making money. It is for proving to yourself that you follow the plan when the money is yours.
:::
