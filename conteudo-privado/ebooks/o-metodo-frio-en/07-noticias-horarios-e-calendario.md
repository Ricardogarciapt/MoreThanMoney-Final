# News, hours and the calendar

> There are moments when the market stops being a chart and becomes a coin in the air. The job is knowing when.

First Friday of the month, 1:29 PM. EUR/USD is calm, the candles are small, everything looks tidy. At 1:30 PM the US jobs report comes out and, within two minutes, the price swings 60 pips one way, comes back 40 the other, and the spread, which was 1 pip, opens up to 5. Anyone with a tight stop got caught by the jump. Anyone who entered "to catch the move" entered at the worst price of the day.

None of these people was doing analysis. They were placing bets.

## The economic calendar

[[foto: mt5-calendario-noticias.jpg | MetaTrader 5 includes news and an economic calendar inside the platform: the "News" and "Calendar" tabs in the toolbox. Image: MetaQuotes (metatrader5.com). | larga]]

The economic calendar is the market's agenda: it lists data releases and central bank decisions, with the time and the expected impact (usually marked with one, two or three stars, or with colours). There are free calendars on many financial websites, and MetaTrader 5 has one inside the app: on the phone, in **Settings › Tradays** (the same screen where you opened the demo account in chapter 1); on the PC, in the **Calendar** tab of the toolbox. Filter by the currencies of your pair and by high importance, and the list for the week fits on one screen.

The events that move the main pairs the most:

| Event | When (Lisbon time, approximate) | Mainly moves |
|---|---|---|
| US jobs report (NFP) | First Friday of the month, 1:30 PM | USD, gold |
| US inflation (CPI) | Once a month, 1:30 PM | USD, gold |
| Fed interest rate decision | 8 times a year, 7:00 PM | the whole market |
| ECB interest rate decision | 8 times a year, 1:15 PM + press conference | EUR |
| Bank of England interest rate decision | 8 times a year, 12:00 PM | GBP |

The times change with summer time in the US and in Europe, which do not change on the same day. Always confirm on the calendar for the week.

:::alerta Classic mistake · Trading into major news without checking
You open a trade at 1:25 PM without looking at the calendar. Five minutes later the price jumps, the spread widens and your stop is filled several pips below the price you had chosen (this is called slippage). It was not bad luck. It was written on the calendar since Monday.
:::

## The 30-minute rule

The simplest rule, and the one I propose for anyone starting out:

1. On Sunday, open the calendar and mark the three-star events of the week for the currencies in your pair.
2. Do not open new trades **30 minutes before** or **30 minutes after** those events.
3. If you have an open position and a strong event is coming, decide **in advance** what you will do: close it, reduce it or keep it with the stop in place. Decide with a cool head, on Sunday, not at 1:29 PM on Friday.

:::cartao Sunday, 20 minutes
- Open the calendar for the week.
- Mark the 3-star events for my currencies.
- Write the forbidden hours in my diary.
- Draw the zones on H4.
- Write the plans for the week.
:::

## Choosing your window

In chapter 2 you saw that the hour with the most movement in the euro and dollar pairs is between 1:00 PM and 5:00 PM (Lisbon time). But the best time is not the market's, it is yours. If you work from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM, you are not going to trade at 3:00 PM secretly in the bathroom, heart pounding, with your boss at the door. You will choose something else:

- **Before work** (7:00 to 8:00 AM): London opening, prices coming to life. Good for planning and leaving pending orders.
- **End of the day** (6:30 to 7:30 PM): New York still open. Good for reviewing positions and writing the journal.
- **At the weekend**: market closed, fresh head. The best time to draw the map and write the plan.

## Alerts: let the price call you

A short window only works if you do not spend the rest of the day checking your phone. That is what price alerts are for. You drew the zones in chapter 4; now you put an alert on the edge of each one and forget about it until it rings.

1. In the PC terminal, open the chart for your pair and the toolbox (**Ctrl+T**).
2. On the **Alerts** tab, right-click › **Create**.
3. Choose the symbol, the condition (for example, Bid greater than 1.08500) and, as the action, **Notification**.
4. Confirm. Because you linked the phone to the PC with the MetaQuotes ID in chapter 1, the alert reaches the phone as a push notification, even when the computer is far away.

An alert is not an order. It is a tap on the shoulder saying "the price has reached your zone, go and look at the plan". The decision stays cold: you open the chart, do the chapter 6 checklist and only then decide.

:::nota Two alerts per zone, at most
One at the entry of the zone, another at the stop. More than that and you are back to living glued to your phone, only now with bells.
:::

:::historia Marta, first Friday of the month
On Thursday night, Marta opened Tradays and saw three stars on Friday at 1:30 PM: the US jobs report. She was on shift. Instead of carrying her phone in her pocket, vibrating, she did three things on the PC before leaving home: she cancelled the pending order that was near the price, set an alert on the upper zone and another on the lower one, and wrote in her diary: "1:00 PM to 2:00 PM: no new orders." At 1:32 PM EURUSD jumped 50 pips. She only found out at 7:00 PM, when she saw the notification. The lower zone had been touched and the price had already come back. She lost nothing because she was not there. It was the best trade she didn't make.
:::

## From my life: the window I had

[[foto: rg-comboio.jpg | Three hours of train a day. That's where I studied a large part of what is in this book. | lado]]

When I started learning trading I had no time. I had shifts, a three-year-old daughter and three hours of train a day. My window was the train, the hour before everyone else woke up and half an hour after Constança fell asleep. There was no room for staring at the screen, waiting for the price to move.

That's why I learned early to use the calendar, pending orders and alerts. Not because I was lazy, but because there was no other way. If you have little time, that's not a flaw to hide. It's the reason to choose your window well and leave the rest of your day for your life.

## The cold rule of this chapter

:::cartao Cold rule no. 7
Calendar on Sunday. No new trades 30 minutes before and after three-star events. I trade in my window, not in the market's.
:::

## In short

- The economic calendar tells you when the market can jump.
- The NFP, US inflation and interest rate decisions are the events that move the most.
- During strong news, the spread widens and the stop can be filled worse than expected.
- The 30-minute rule, before and after.
- Your window is the time when you can stay calm and uninterrupted.
- Price alerts created on the PC reach the phone: the price calls you, you do not wait for it.
