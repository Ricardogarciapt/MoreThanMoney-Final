# News, timing and the calendar

> There are moments when the market stops being a chart and becomes a coin in the air. Your job is to know when.

First Friday of the month, 1:29pm. EUR/USD is quiet, the candles are small, everything looks tidy. At 1:30pm the US jobs report comes out and, within two minutes, the price swings 60 pips one way, comes back 40 the other, and the spread, which was 1 pip, blows out to 5. Anyone with a tight stop got caught by the jump. Anyone who entered "to catch the move" got in at the worst price of the day.

None of these people was doing analysis. They were placing bets.

## The economic calendar

[[foto: mt5-calendario-noticias.jpg | MetaTrader 5 includes news and an economic calendar inside the platform: the "News" and "Calendar" tabs, in the toolbox. Image: MetaQuotes (metatrader5.com). | larga]]


The economic calendar is the market's diary: it lists data releases and central bank decisions, with the time and the expected impact (usually marked with one, two or three stars, or with colours). There are free calendars on many financial websites, and some brokers have one built into the platform.

The events that move the major pairs the most:

| Event | When (Lisbon time, approximately) | Mainly moves |
|---|---|---|
| US jobs report (NFP) | first Friday of the month, 1:30pm | USD, gold |
| US inflation (CPI) | once a month, 1:30pm | USD, gold |
| Fed interest rate decision | 8 times a year, 7:00pm | the whole market |
| ECB interest rate decision | 8 times a year, 1:15pm + press conference | EUR |
| Bank of England interest rate decision | 8 times a year, 12:00pm | GBP |

Times shift with summer time in the US and in Europe, and those changes do not happen on the same day. Always check the calendar for the week.

:::alerta Classic mistake · Trading into big news without knowing it
You open a trade at 1:25pm without looking at the calendar. Five minutes later the price jumps, the spread widens and your stop is filled several pips below the price you had chosen (this is called slippage). It was not bad luck. It had been written on the calendar since Monday.
:::

## The 30-minute rule

The simplest rule, and the one I suggest for anyone starting out:

1. On Sunday, open the calendar and mark the week's three-star events for the currencies in your pair.
2. Do not open new trades **30 minutes before** or **30 minutes after** those events.
3. If you have a position open and a strong event is coming, decide **in advance** what you will do: close it, reduce it or hold it with the stop in place. Decide with a cool head, on Sunday, not at 1:29pm on a Friday.

:::cartao Sunday, 20 minutes
- Open the week's calendar.
- Mark the three-star events for my currencies.
- Write the forbidden hours into my diary.
- Draw zones on the H4.
- Write the plans for the week.
:::

## Choosing your window

In chapter 1 you saw that the busiest hours for the euro and dollar pairs fall between 1pm and 5pm Lisbon time. But the best time is not the market's, it is yours. If you work from 9am to 6pm, you are not going to trade at 3pm on the sly from the toilet, heart pounding, with your boss at the door. You will choose something else:

- **Before work** (7am to 8am): London opening, prices coming to life. Good for planning and leaving pending orders.
- **End of the day** (6:30pm to 7:30pm): New York still open. Good for reviewing positions and writing the journal.
- **At the weekend**: market closed, fresh head. The best moment to draw the map and write the plan.

## In short

- The economic calendar tells you when the market might jump.
- NFP, US inflation and interest rate decisions are the events that move prices the most.
- During big news, the spread widens and your stop can be filled worse than expected.
- The 30-minute rule, before and after.
- Your window is the time when you can stay calm and uninterrupted.
