# Módulo 6 — DCA, cycles and halving (≈17 min)
**Objetivo:** understand the difference between DCA and a single lump-sum investment, read the halving and the cycles without guessing, and build a regular buying plan. **No fim, o aluno consegue:** 1) explain DCA with an illustrative example; 2) explain the halving and what changes on each date; 3) separate volatility, dominance and noise.

## Aula 6.1 — DCA, the boring method that takes the guesswork out (≈4–5 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** There is a way of investing that almost nobody wants to hear about, because it is boring. That is exactly why it takes the guesswork out of the equation.

**[CENA 1]** Animated calendar with one marked day in each month and a coin falling onto every marked day.
**NARRAÇÃO:** DCA stands for regular buying, or dollar cost averaging. The idea is simple. Instead of investing everything at once, you buy a fixed amount at regular intervals. It can be one hundred euros a month, or twenty euros a week. You buy when the price is high and you buy when it is low, without trying to pick the right moment. The goal is not to hit the top or the bottom of the market. It is to stay disciplined for a long time, without relying on a hunch that makes you stop or speed up. This is the part that sounds unexciting, and it is also the part most people give up on. Think of it as the monthly savings you put aside, except that instead of leaving it sitting still, you put it into an asset.

**[CENA 2]** Animated table with the illustrative example: three purchases of one hundred euros, at prices of fifty, twenty-five and fifty. Caption: “illustrative example, made-up figures”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let's look at an illustrative example, with made-up, round prices, just to understand the mechanics. Imagine you buy one hundred euros a month, for three months, in a hypothetical asset. In the first month it costs fifty euros, so you buy two units. In the second month the price drops to twenty-five, and with the same one hundred euros you buy four units. In the third month it goes back up to fifty, and you buy two more. In total you have eight units and you have spent three hundred euros. Your average price per unit is thirty-seven euros and fifty cents. Notice that this figure is below the simple average of the three prices, because you bought more when it was cheap.

**[CENA 3]** Split screen. On the left, the lump sum: three hundred euros at fifty, six units. On the right, DCA: eight units. The totals change across three milestones.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Now the comparison. If you had invested the full three hundred euros in the first month, at fifty euros, you would have six units. At the end of the example, with the price back at fifty, the eight DCA units are worth four hundred euros, and the six lump-sum units are worth three hundred. But look at the middle of the path. After the second month, with the price at twenty-five, DCA has six units, worth one hundred and fifty euros on two hundred invested. The lump sum also has six units and is worth one hundred and fifty, but on three hundred invested. In percentage terms, the lump sum is down fifty percent and DCA is down twenty-five percent.

**[CENA 4]** Red alert on screen: “Not a guarantee of profit”. Below it, a chart of a price that rises right at the start and never comes back down.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Now notice what this example does not say. It does not say that DCA is always better. If the price had risen in the first month and never come back down, the lump sum would have earned much more. DCA trades part of that opportunity for less stress and less risk of buying everything at the top. It is not a guarantee of profit, and an asset can fall for years without recovering. So the amount you use in each purchase has to be an amount you can afford to lose, without it affecting your income, your bills or your sleep.

**[CENA 5]** Calendar with a tick mark in each month, and beside it a sheet with the rules written down.
**NARRAÇÃO:** DCA works like a habit. The biggest enemy is not the market. It is your hand pressing the button when the price falls and fear kicks in, or when it rises and greed kicks in. So write the rules down before you start. How much you buy, on which day, on which platform, and what you do if one month you are short of money. Then stick to them. A rule written on a sheet of paper is worth more than ten decisions made in a hurry, in the middle of the night, with your phone in your hand. Once the rule is written, the emotion of the day no longer runs the show.

**[CENA 6]** Arrow pointing to lesson 6.2. Text beside it: “What actually changes?”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** In the next lesson we will look at one of the most talked-about topics in the crypto world, the halving, and why so many people get so excited about it. You will understand what actually changes on the network, and what is just noise around the date. Keep the central idea of this lesson: DCA does not predict anything. It organises your purchases and lets the market do whatever it wants. Work through the sum yourself, with other made-up prices, and you will understand the logic in ten minutes.

## Aula 6.2 — Halving: the supply clock (≈4–5 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** Roughly every four years, the creation of new bitcoins is cut in half. Do you know why, and do you know what that really means?

**[CENA 1]** Animation of a reward bar shrinking in five steps, with the dates underneath: fifty, twenty-five, twelve point five, six point two five, three point one two five. Chart ID: halving.
**NARRAÇÃO:** New bitcoins are created by miners, as a reward for validating blocks of transactions. That reward is not fixed. It is set in the protocol itself: every 210,000 blocks, which takes about four years on average, the reward per block is cut in half. This is called the halving. When the network started, in 2009, the reward was fifty bitcoins per block. In 2012 it dropped to twenty-five. In 2016 it went to twelve point five, in 2020 to six point two five, and in 2024 to three point one two five. Each step cuts the new supply in half. Notice that the rule does not depend on any one person: it is written in the code and applies in the same way to everyone.

**[CENA 2]** Screen with the phrase “21 million” and a bar filling up, almost to the top.
**NARRAÇÃO:** With this rule, the total supply converges towards the limit of twenty-one million bitcoins. Most of it has already been issued, and what remains comes out in smaller and smaller slices, almost unnoticed. This is a fact about new supply, and that is what makes the topic interesting. Fewer new bitcoins per day means, in theory, less supply entering the market. Miners also pay for energy and equipment, so the drop in reward has a big effect on their accounts. But theory and price are different things, and this is where many people get it wrong. Supply is one piece of the puzzle. Demand, context and the mood of all the participants are the other pieces.

**[CENA 3]** Line chart with the price and vertical markers at the halving years. Above it, a box saying “what happened before”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** It is tempting to look at past halvings and conclude that the price always rises after each one. That conclusion is dangerous. There are too few cases to draw a rule from, the context changed a lot from one cycle to the next, and the market may have anticipated the event months earlier. Each halving is a fact about supply, not a date marked for a price rise. Use it as information to understand the market, never as a certainty about what will happen.

**[CENA 4]** Illustrative case. A bedroom in Aveiro, Marta, 38, a teacher, with her laptop open at night. Caption: “illustrative case”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** I want to tell you an illustrative case. Marta, 38, a teacher in Aveiro, watched a video about the halving and became convinced that a rise was certain. She bought everything at once, in one night, with money she had set aside for the car. In the following weeks the price fell, and she was left staring at the screen, not knowing what to do. She did not sell in a panic, but she did not have a plan either. The mistake was not the halving, which is a real fact. The mistake was treating a date as a guarantee, and using money that should not have been used. If the purchase had been made in instalments, with a written plan, she would have had time to review her decisions at every step.

**[CENA 5]** Split screen: on the left, the logo of a proof-of-work network; on the right, that of a proof-of-stake network. Text: “Not every network has a halving”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** A technical note, because it is easy to get confused. Not every network works this way. Bitcoin uses proof of work, with miners competing with energy and equipment. Ethereum moved to proof of stake in September 2022, in the event known as The Merge, and it no longer has mining or halvings. Each network has its own issuance rule. Before you say that something will rise because it has a halving, first ask yourself whether that network has a halving at all, and what kind.

**[CENA 6]** Screen with a big question: “Fact, news or promise?”. Three boxes underneath.
**NARRAÇÃO:** So what does the halving teach you that is useful? It teaches you to think in long cycles and in supply, not in the news of the day. It also teaches you to be wary of anyone who sells the halving as a money machine. There is a lot of content that turns a date into a show, with countdown clocks and dramatic music. You do not need any of that. You need to understand the rule, keep the fact in mind, and decide what to do with your money based on a plan, not on a countdown. That brings us to the topic of the next lesson: how to read the market without guessing.

## Aula 6.3 — Cycles, volatility and dominance: reading without guessing (≈4–5 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** The crypto market goes up and down with a force that frightens anyone who is not prepared. The question is not how to get it right. It is how to read it without guessing.

**[CENA 1]** Chart with a volatility line and the caption: “drops of more than 70% in different years”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let's start with volatility, which is the size of price movements. In crypto it is very high. Bitcoin has fallen more than seventy percent from its peak, on more than one occasion, and many other coins have fallen even further. Before you buy, ask yourself an honest question: if my balance fell by half, could I carry on without selling in a panic? If the answer is no, your exposure is too big, whatever the videos say. This question is worth more than any forecast.

**[CENA 2]** Cycle curve drawn on screen, with four phases marked: accumulation, uptrend, euphoria, downtrend.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Many people talk about cycles: a phase of accumulation, a strong rise, a euphoria in which everyone seems rich, then a long decline and a period of exhaustion. Cycles are a useful way of reading the past, because they help you recognise patterns of human behaviour. But they are not a calendar. In the euphoria it seems the whole world has got rich, and in the decline it seems it will never recover. Nobody knows exactly when the phase changes. There is a dangerous phrase you hear in every cycle: this time is different. Sometimes there are real changes, but the wish for it to be different is always the same old emotion. Anyone who swears they know is almost always trying to sell you something. You do not need to memorise any of this.

**[CENA 3]** Pie chart with the bitcoin slice standing out, and an arrow going up and down.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Another indicator is bitcoin dominance, which is the percentage of the total market value that belongs to bitcoin. When dominance rises, money tends to concentrate in bitcoin, which is seen as the most established asset. When it falls, there can be more appetite for altcoins, which are riskier and more volatile coins. It is not a magic rule, and it can mislead you if you read it on its own. But it is a way of seeing where the money is going, and that is already useful information for your plan. Notice that dominance also rises when the market falls back, because smaller coins usually drop harder.

**[CENA 4]** Screen with three headlines in a row: “It's going to explode!”, “It's all over!”, “Official news: new rule published”. The last one is highlighted.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Learn to separate news from noise. News is a verifiable fact: a law passed, a confirmed technical failure, a concrete change in supply or regulation. Noise is an opinion repeated a thousand times, the alarmist headline, and the video with epic music and red arrows. Before you react, ask: has this changed anything in the project, or has it only changed people's mood? If it is only mood, you usually do not need to act today. A good habit is to save the news in a list and review that list a week later. If nobody is talking about it any more, it was probably noise.

**[CENA 5]** Screen with a weekly calendar and the phrase “Plan before the guess”. A clock with its hand stopped.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Reading the market without guessing means accepting that you do not know what comes tomorrow, and organising yourself for that. Define your plan before things happen. Write down what would make you change your mind, and trust that piece of paper more than the noise of the moment. Check your balance once a week, not every minute, because watching nonstop increases emotion and reduces clarity. Someone without a plan ends up reacting to everything, and reacting to everything is usually expensive, because decisions get made out of fear or euphoria. The market owes you nothing, and that is not a problem. The problem is being left without a plan.

**[CENA 6]** Final screen with the phrase: “Reading without guessing = written plan + verified information”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** To sum up this lesson: volatility shows you how much you could suffer, cycles show you patterns that are not a calendar, dominance shows you where the money is going, and verifiable news is separated from noise. None of these pieces gives you the right answer for tomorrow. Together, they give you something better, which is a criterion for deciding calmly. If any of these ideas seem complicated, that is fine. Just start with one of them and write down what you observed. In the last lesson of this module we will turn all of this into a concrete buying plan.

**Texto no ecrã:** volatility = size of the moves · cycles ≠ calendar · dominance ≠ magic rule · verifiable news vs noise · plan before the guess

## Aula 6.4 — Module mission: your DCA plan (≈4–5 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** Moving from theory to practice is the step most people skip. Today we are not going to skip it. We are going to do it with paper and pen.

**[CENA 1]** Sheet with five fields to fill in: monthly amount, fixed day, platform, exception rule, review date.
**NARRAÇÃO:** We are going to build a DCA plan, step by step. First, choose a monthly amount that you could lose without it ruining your life. It is not whatever happens to be left at the end of the month by chance. It is what remains after your fixed bills, an emergency fund and the taxes you already have to pay. Second, choose a fixed day, for example the day after payday. Third, choose the regulated platform where you will buy, and confirm that verification and authentication are in order. Fourth, write the exception rule: what you do in a month when it is not possible. Fifth, mark when you will review the plan, for example every three months. Remember: the plan is not a contract with the market. It is a contract with yourself.

**[CENA 2]** Large warning on screen: “Never money for rent, your transport pass or water”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** One rule that is not negotiable: never use money you need for rent, your transport pass, water, electricity or the IRS, the Portuguese income tax. If a month goes wrong, you do not want to be forced to sell in order to pay for your home, and that is exactly when decisions turn bad. Start small. Ten or twenty euros a week is enough to learn the process, understand the fees and see how you feel when the price falls. That feeling is the most valuable part of the training. If you have doubts about the platform, ask before you buy, never after.

**NARRAÇÃO:** Once you have written the plan, sign it with the date and keep it somewhere safe, separate from your seed phrase. Before the first purchase, confirm that the platform is verified, that two-factor authentication is active, and that you have the addresses saved in your bookmarks. And let 24 hours pass before you make the first purchase. That wait is the final test: if, the next day, you still want to, the plan is yours. If you no longer want to, good thing you waited. Do not rush just because the market seems to be in a good mood that day. Also write down what you will do if the price falls by half, so the rule is clear before the scare, not during it.

Keep this paper and reread it before every review.
