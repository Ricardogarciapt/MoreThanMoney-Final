# Módulo 9 — Reading the market as an investor (≈14 min)
**Objetivo:** learn to read the market in three layers, the weekly chart, the daily chart with DEFY and the emotional context, and use those readings to decide the size of the top-up, without ever letting them stop the DCA.  **No fim, o aluno consegue:** 1) read the Momentum on the weekly chart and know what changes, and what does not change, in the plan; 2) use DEFY as a second opinion, without treating it as a buy order; 3) place market sentiment and Bitcoin dominance as context, without turning them into a button; 4) put it all together into a four-step method and record the Friday readings.

**Production note:** the numbers in this unit are illustrative examples and are said out loud and shown on screen. The charts are real screen recordings, with the data of the day of recording, and no balances or client names are shown.

## Aula 9.1 — Weekly Momentum (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "A daily chart shows the week that has passed. The weekly chart shows the year. If you only look at one of them, you are seeing half the film."

**[CENA 1]** Ruben in a medium shot, almost black background, details in gold. TradingView screen recording: the weekly Bitcoin chart with Momentum. Text on screen: “Educational content. The risk of loss is real.”
**NARRAÇÃO:** Today we are going to read Momentum on the weekly chart. You already know the five confirmations: the cloud present and in the right colour, the cloud on the right side of the candles, the exponential moving average in agreement, the volume in the colour of the direction, and the RSI crossing in the right zone. On the weekly chart, each candle is a whole week. That changes the rhythm of the reading. A weekly reading does not change from Friday to Monday. So it is a background frame, not a button. Anyone who reads the weekly chart every morning is looking for noise under a magnifying glass. And the risk of loss is real, on any chart, and it always stays with you.

**[CENA 2]** Animation: five squares on a weekly chart light up one by one. Below, three bands: “five out of five”, “four out of five”, “three or fewer”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Read the confirmations the way you read them on the daily chart, but with more patience. Five out of five, in green: the long-term trend is in favour. Four out of five is still a useful reading. Three or fewer: the weekly chart does not have a strong opinion, and that is also information. The question you ask is simple: does the weekly chart confirm what the daily chart is saying? If the daily is green and the weekly is red, that is not a contradiction to resolve in a hurry. It is a sign that the fall may not be over, and that the pot should be used with more calm, not more haste.

**[CENA 3]** Three weekly charts side by side, with captions: “turned to buy after a long fall”, “prolonged strong red”, “sideways with lilac boxes”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let us go through three typical scenarios. First: the weekly chart has turned green after a long fall. It is a good moment to confirm that the pot is ready, because Strong Buy weeks may come next. Second: strong red for several weeks. Here the rule is the same as in module seven: base always, and the pot kept still until the weekly chart starts to ease. Third: lilac boxes on the weekly chart, a sideways market. For the DCA, it is a period to accumulate with patience, with no hurry to use the pot. In none of these three cases does the weekly chart stop the DCA. It only helps you decide the size.

**[CENA 4]** Ruben showing a calendar with two markings: “weekly: once a month” and “daily: every Friday”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Now the rhythm, which is the part most people get wrong. You read the weekly chart once a month, on a quiet weekend, to see the whole picture and review your plan. You read the daily chart every Friday, within your ritual. The weekly reading does not replace the daily one: the weekly sets the context, and the daily decides the day. If you ever catch yourself opening the weekly chart every morning, stop, breathe and go back to the notebook. That is anxiety dressed up as analysis, and it costs a lot in hasty decisions.

**[CENA 5]** Ruben, close-up, talking to camera. Line on screen: “The weekly gives you the map, not the timetable.”
**NARRAÇÃO:** A common mistake is looking on the weekly chart for the exact day to buy. That day does not exist. The weekly chart gives you the map: the direction of the background and the weight of the trend. It does not tell you whether tomorrow is the best day for anything. And there is another trap: changing your plan just because the weekly chart changed colour once. Wait for the change to be confirmed over another one or two candles. If your plan is long term, the weekly chart serves to adjust the size of the pot, and never to stop the DCA. Never.

**[CENA 6]** Exercise sheet with four columns: “month”, “weekly”, “confirmations”, “did the plan change?”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Three-minute exercise. Open the weekly chart of an asset in your plan, count the five confirmations and write the result in the notebook, with the date. Then write, in one sentence, whether the plan changes or not. If it does not change, write “no change”. It may look like a formality, but it is discipline. Six months from now, you will have a history of readings, not a memory of when you happened to remember. And memory, as you know, tends to side with the story that suits us. If the weekly chart is unreadable to you, that is fine. Write “cannot read it” and ask yourself why. Very often it is a lack of habit, and habit comes with repetition.

**Texto no ecrã:** weekly = background map · five confirmations · read once a month · never to stop the DCA
**Check question:** The daily is green and the weekly is strong red. Do I stop the DCA? Answer: no. The base is always bought; the pot calls for more calm.
**Fecho / CTA da aula:** "Next lesson: DEFY, as a second opinion."

## Aula 9.2 — DEFY as a second opinion (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "A second opinion is only worth something if it is not a copy of the first. So let us see what DEFY asks that Momentum does not."

**[CENA 1]** Daily chart with DEFY: exhaustion bands, inner, middle and outer, around a smooth average, with the dashed projection.
**NARRAÇÃO:** You already know DEFY from the earlier lessons. It draws exhaustion bands around a smooth average: an inner one, a middle one and an outer one. The question DEFY asks is a different one. While Momentum asks whether the direction is in favour, DEFY asks whether the move has already gone too far. These are different questions, which is why they can disagree. When they disagree, nothing is broken. There is information. And that information is worth more than a forced agreement. We are not going to repeat everything you saw in module seven. We are going to look at DEFY as an extra reading, which comes after Momentum, not before it.

**[CENA 2]** Close-up of a candle that touches the outer band and turns with a strong candle. Above it, the stars of the idea.
**NARRAÇÃO:** When the price reaches the outer band and turns with a strong candle, DEFY may mark an idea. The stars count the confirmations: has the level already reacted before? Is the trend cloud on the same side? Is there a volume break? Three confirmations give one star, more give two or three, and when everything lines up the candle turns gold. The stars measure how many confirmations there are. They do not measure the certainty of the result, because that does not exist. A gold candle is not a guarantee of a rise. It is a stretched price with many pieces fitting together, and that is all it is.

**[CENA 3]** Animated table with four rows: “Momentum green with 4 or 5 · DEFY with stars”, “Momentum green · DEFY without stars”, “Momentum red or lilac · DEFY with stars”, “Momentum red or lilac · DEFY without stars”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let us go through the combinations. Momentum green and DEFY with stars: both readings in favour. If the MTM Smart DCA label also helps, it is a week to use the pot with judgement. Momentum green and DEFY without stars: one reading in favour. Momentum red or lilac with stars on DEFY: also one reading in favour, because a buy idea exists, but the trend does not yet agree. And Momentum red or lilac without stars: no reading in favour, and the base is enough. Notice that there is no row that says “buy everything”. That row does not exist, and if you find it in some group or other, be suspicious of it.

**[CENA 4]** Ruben drawing a three-line decision table in the notebook: “two in favour: base + pot”, “one in favour: base + at most half the pot”, “none: base”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let us put this into a simple frame, to stick next to the screen. Two readings in favour: base plus pot, within the ceiling. One reading in favour: base plus, at most, half the pot. None: only the base. Above everything sits the monthly ceiling, which is never breached, and the MTM Smart DCA label, which gives the starting point. DEFY is a second opinion. It is not the first criterion, and it has no power to cancel the label. It helps you choose how much of the pot to use, and that is all. Print it or copy it by hand, and always keep it in front of you on Friday, so that the decision does not depend on who is most tired that day.

**[CENA 5]** Warning on screen, in large letters: “DEFY signals are for trading. In DCA, they only affect the size.”
**NARRAÇÃO:** Repeat after me: DEFY signals were designed for trading. They carry an entry, a stop and targets, because that is what a trader needs. In DCA you do not use those levels. You do not buy at the “entry price”, you do not place a stop and you do not take profit at targets. Your DCA is long term, and DEFY only affects the size of the top-up. If one day you want to trade with rules, that is a different path, with different training, a different account and a different risk. It is not this one. And having DEFY on the screen does not give your DCA a stop.

**[CENA 6]** Exercise sheet with three charts and three boxes to fill in: “stars”, “Momentum”, “decision”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Exercise: open the daily chart of an asset in your plan with DEFY. Count the stars, if there are any. Then look at the Momentum on the same chart. Write down the two readings and the decision: base, base plus half the pot, or base plus the pot, always within the ceiling. Do this for a week, without buying anything extra. You will see that the two tools disagree more often than you imagined, and that the disagreement teaches more than agreement does. If there are no stars in any week, that is also a valid reading. Record that there was no idea and follow the plan, without looking for a star that is not there.

**Texto no ecrã:** DEFY: exhaustion bands · stars = confirmations · two in favour: base + pot · one: base + half pot · none: base · DEFY only changes the size
**Check question:** DEFY has three stars and Momentum is strong red. What do you do with the pot? Answer: one reading in favour, so base and, at most, half the pot.

## Aula 9.3 — Sentiment, fear and euphoria, and BTC dominance (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "You have heard someone say “there is fear in the market” or “this is euphoric”. It sounds like information. But what actually changes in your plan? Let us find out."

**[CENA 1]** Screen with a sentiment gauge, a scale from zero to one hundred in gold tones. Text on screen: “public sentiment indices · context, not a signal”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Sentiment is the collective mood. There are public indices that try to measure whether people are frightened or greedy. They usually combine several signals, for example volatility and public attention to the topic, and present the result on a scale from zero to one hundred. Zero is extreme fear; one hundred is extreme euphoria. Notice the words: these indices are constructions. They are not the market. They are a snapshot of how people feel about the market. Before you use any of them, read on its own page how it is built. Useful for context, but never a command.

**[CENA 2]** Animation: a scale with three zones, “extreme fear”, “neutral” and “euphoria”, and an arrow pointing to the plan: “the number does not change the top-up”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Why do fear and euphoria matter? Because people's behaviour tends to get worse at the extremes: they buy more at the top and sell more at the bottom. DCA was designed precisely to escape that trap. So sentiment does not tell you how much to buy for the base. It tells you where you stand in the collective emotion. Extreme fear is a good moment to remember the written plan. Extreme euphoria is a good moment to remember the ceiling. In neither case does the DCA stop, and in neither case does the index number by itself change the top-up.

**[CENA 3]** Line chart of Bitcoin dominance, with the caption: “BTC dominance = Bitcoin's share of the total crypto market value”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Now Bitcoin dominance. It is the share Bitcoin occupies in the total value of the crypto market. Illustrative example: if the whole market were worth one hundred, and Bitcoin were worth fifty, dominance would be fifty per cent. When dominance rises, it usually means that money is concentrating in Bitcoin and that the smaller coins suffer more. When it falls, the opposite can happen, with the smaller coins gaining ground. These are patterns that are observed, not laws. You never know how long they last, and you never know whether they repeat in the same form.

**[CENA 4]** Split screen: on the left, the plan's split written in the notebook; on the right, the same split with the satellites worth less. Caption: “illustrative example”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let us see how this falls on your plan. Illustrative example: dominance goes from fifty to fifty-six per cent. If your satellites are small coins, it is quite possible they have fallen more than the core. This is context for your notebook, and the question is a different one: has my split changed? If it has, it is time to look at the rebalancing rule you wrote in module eight. Do not buy more satellite just because dominance has risen. First confirm that the satellite has a written thesis and that the core still fits the plan.

**[CENA 5]** Ruben, close-up, with the line on screen: “Sentiment is context. The label is the starting point.”
**NARRAÇÃO:** The golden rule of this lesson: sentiment and dominance are context. They do not replace the MTM Smart DCA label, they do not replace Momentum or DEFY, and they do not change your ceiling. They can go into a line of the notebook, something like “context for the week: high fear, dominance rising”. That is useful for you to look back at in three months. But they do not change the amount of the purchase. If you catch yourself saying “with this euphoria, I am not buying”, come back to this lesson. Euphoria is a reason to look at the pot calmly. It is not a reason to stop the plan.

**[CENA 6]** Exercise sheet with four columns: “date”, “sentiment”, “dominance”, “did the plan change?”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Two-minute exercise: write in the notebook the current value of the sentiment index you use and Bitcoin's dominance, with the date. Then answer one question: did my plan change because of this? In most weeks, the answer is no, and that is fine. This line is not there for you to change your purchase. It is there so that, over time, you can see the moments when the market's mood pushed you to decide badly. Once that pattern is written down, it has less power over you. If you use more than one index, write them all down, and note whether they point the same way. When they do not, that is useful information too.

**Texto no ecrã:** sentiment = context · dominance = BTC's share · fear and euphoria do not stop the DCA · context in the notebook, not on the button
**Check question:** Bitcoin dominance has risen and your satellites have fallen. Do you buy more satellite? Answer: not because of that. First I check the thesis and the ceiling; the rise in dominance is context.

## Aula 9.4 — Putting it together to choose the size of the top-up (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "You already have several tools in your head. But Friday's question is only one: how much do I buy? Let us answer that question in order."

**[CENA 1]** Chalkboard drawing with four numbered steps. Text on screen: “1 label · 2 readings · 3 context · 4 ceiling and record”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let us put everything together into one method, with four steps, always in this order. First, the MTM Smart DCA label, which gives the starting point. Second, the daily readings: Momentum and DEFY, counting how many are in favour. Third, the context: sentiment and dominance, which are written down but do not change the number. Fourth, the monthly ceiling and the record. The order matters. If you start with the context, you begin building your story before you have looked at the chart, and that is exactly where decisions become emotional.

**[CENA 2]** Animated table with the four labels: “Strong Buy”, “Buy”, “Wait”, “Do Not Add”. Beside them, the starting point for each.
**NARRAÇÃO:** The label gives the maximum for the day. Strong Buy can go up to twice the base. Buy, one and a half times. Wait, half the base, and the rest stays in the pot. Do Not Add, zero for that asset that week, and the whole base goes into the pot. Do Not Add never uses the pot. Then the readings decide how much of the pot comes in. Two or more in favour: base plus pot, within what the label allows. One: base plus, at most, half the pot. None: only the base. In no row is the monthly ceiling exceeded.

**[CENA 3]** Screen with an example over four weeks, with columns: “week”, “label”, “readings”, “purchase”, “pot”. Caption at the top: “illustrative example”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let us look at an illustrative example, with a base of twenty-five euros a week and a pot that starts with forty euros. Week one: Strong Buy, two readings in favour. You buy fifty: twenty-five from the base and twenty-five from the pot, and the pot falls to fifteen. Week two: Buy, one reading in favour. You buy twenty-five from the base and, at most, half the pot, seven euros and fifty cents. The pot stays at seven euros and fifty cents. Week three: Do Not Add. Zero for the asset, and the twenty-five from the base go into the pot, which rises to thirty-two euros and fifty cents. Week four: no reading in favour, so only the base is bought. The new money for the month was one hundred euros. What came out of the pot was money that was already saved.

**[CENA 4]** Ruben, close-up. Line on screen: “The DCA does not stop. It only changes size.”
**NARRAÇÃO:** This sentence is the most important one in the programme so far. In the example we have just seen, there was not a single week in which the plan stopped. There were weeks with a bigger purchase, one week with only the base, and one week in which the asset received no purchase, but the money went into the pot and remains part of the plan. There is one thing you never do: stop the plan because the chart looks ugly, or because everyone is euphoric. If your DCA stops, it is no longer a DCA. It has become a guess with a calendar.

**[CENA 5]** Friday ritual sheet with the columns: “date”, “asset”, “label”, “readings”, “sentiment and dominance”, “purchase”, “pot”.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Where is everything recorded? On the Friday ritual sheet you have used since module seven. Date, asset, label, Momentum and DEFY readings, one line of context with sentiment and dominance, how much you bought, how much went into the pot and how much came out of it. Without this record, the decision stays in memory, and memory tends to embellish what we did. With it, months from now you can see your own pattern: whether you buy more when you are euphoric, whether the pot always stays still, whether the label gives you more signals than the readings do. That is real learning.

**[CENA 6]** Ruben with a calendar showing four marked Fridays. Final text on screen: “Module 9 mission: four Friday readings, recorded.” Underneath, the educational warning.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Module mission: do the four Friday readings and record each one. On each Friday, follow the four steps: label, readings, context, ceiling and record. If you do not have money to buy, do the reading anyway and record what you would have done. The mission is the record, not the purchase. Four consecutive Fridays give you the first history of your own method. At the end, set aside five minutes and compare the first reading with the last. And remember: none of this guarantees results. It is education. The risk of loss is real, and it always stays with you. See you in module ten.

**Texto no ecrã:** 1 label · 2 readings · 3 context · 4 ceiling and record · the DCA never stops · mission: 4 recorded Friday readings
