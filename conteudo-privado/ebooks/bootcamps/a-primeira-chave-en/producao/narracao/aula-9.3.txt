You have heard someone say “there is fear in the market” or “this is euphoric”. It sounds like information. But what actually changes in your plan? Let us find out.

Sentiment is the collective mood. There are public indices that try to measure whether people are frightened or greedy. They usually combine several signals, for example volatility and public attention to the topic, and present the result on a scale from zero to one hundred. Zero is extreme fear; one hundred is extreme euphoria. Notice the words: these indices are constructions. They are not the market. They are a snapshot of how people feel about the market. Before you use any of them, read on its own page how it is built. Useful for context, but never a command.

Why do fear and euphoria matter? Because people's behaviour tends to get worse at the extremes: they buy more at the top and sell more at the bottom. DCA was designed precisely to escape that trap. So sentiment does not tell you how much to buy for the base. It tells you where you stand in the collective emotion. Extreme fear is a good moment to remember the written plan. Extreme euphoria is a good moment to remember the ceiling. In neither case does the DCA stop, and in neither case does the index number by itself change the top-up.

Now Bitcoin dominance. It is the share Bitcoin occupies in the total value of the crypto market. Illustrative example: if the whole market were worth one hundred, and Bitcoin were worth fifty, dominance would be fifty per cent. When dominance rises, it usually means that money is concentrating in Bitcoin and that the smaller coins suffer more. When it falls, the opposite can happen, with the smaller coins gaining ground. These are patterns that are observed, not laws. You never know how long they last, and you never know whether they repeat in the same form.

Let us see how this falls on your plan. Illustrative example: dominance goes from fifty to fifty-six per cent. If your satellites are small coins, it is quite possible they have fallen more than the core. This is context for your notebook, and the question is a different one: has my split changed? If it has, it is time to look at the rebalancing rule you wrote in module eight. Do not buy more satellite just because dominance has risen. First confirm that the satellite has a written thesis and that the core still fits the plan.

The golden rule of this lesson: sentiment and dominance are context. They do not replace the MTM Smart DCA label, they do not replace Momentum or DEFY, and they do not change your ceiling. They can go into a line of the notebook, something like “context for the week: high fear, dominance rising”. That is useful for you to look back at in three months. But they do not change the amount of the purchase. If you catch yourself saying “with this euphoria, I am not buying”, come back to this lesson. Euphoria is a reason to look at the pot calmly. It is not a reason to stop the plan.

Two-minute exercise: write in the notebook the current value of the sentiment index you use and Bitcoin's dominance, with the date. Then answer one question: did my plan change because of this? In most weeks, the answer is no, and that is fine. This line is not there for you to change your purchase. It is there so that, over time, you can see the moments when the market's mood pushed you to decide badly. Once that pattern is written down, it has less power over you. If you use more than one index, write them all down, and note whether they point the same way. When they do not, that is useful information too.
