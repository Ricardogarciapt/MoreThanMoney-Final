With crypto, tax is not paid on the day you buy. It can show up, however, on the day you sell, if you are not prepared.

Let's get to taxes, with a preliminary note: I speak in general terms, because the rules change, and whoever decides your situation is a certified accountant. That said, there is a basic principle you should know. In Portugal, crypto capital gains on assets held for less than 365 days are taxed at 28 per cent. If you hold them for 365 days or more, they are, as a rule, exempt. A capital gain is the positive difference between the sale value and the purchase value. If you sell for less than you paid, there is no gain to tax. The principle is simple. The details are what change the sum, and they are what catch those who are not prepared. The purchase date and the sale date count days, and a single day's difference can change everything.

Let's look at an illustrative example, with round figures and no fees. You buy one thousand euros of crypto and, three months later, you sell for one thousand three hundred. The gain is three hundred euros. On those three hundred you apply the twenty-eight per cent, which gives eighty-four euros. Notice this carefully: the tax does not fall on the total of one thousand three hundred, it falls only on the gain. If the same investment had been held for 365 days or more, the general rule would be different, and the sum would, as a rule, be exempt. All of this can change, so always confirm the current version before drawing conclusions.

So, record everything. Every purchase, every sale and every swap, with the date, the asset, the quantity, the value in euros at the time and the fees paid. Add a column for the type of operation. Don't rely on memory or on loose screenshots, because in a year you won't remember everything. A simple spreadsheet is enough to start. If you swap one coin for another, record both ends of the operation, because both can count. And keep this sheet alongside the trading journal you learned about in module 14. They are two different records, and both pay you back for the time they take.

Now the most important warning of the lesson. Crypto-to-crypto swaps, staking income, airdrops and other cases may be treated differently, and I don't want to guess that in a video. There is also the option of aggregation, in which the gain goes into your annual income. It may be more or less advantageous, depending on your tax bracket, and that comparison needs someone who sees your complete figures. Don't make tax decisions based on a video, nor on what someone said in a message group. Take your record sheet and the questions you wrote beforehand to the meeting with the accountant.

An important note for anyone who does not live in Portugal, for example in France or another country. This lesson explains the Portuguese rules, and the rules of other countries are different. They can differ in the rate, the deadline, the way you declare and the way they treat crypto. Don't apply anything I have said to your situation without confirming it. What you should do is contact the tax authority of your country of residence and an accountant qualified in that country. Ask for the answer in writing and bring your records. If you live in France, for example, the conversation is with the French authorities and a French professional, not with this video.

Illustrative case: Pedro made several purchases and sales over the year, without recording anything. When the time came to declare, he did not know the exact dates or the amount he had paid in each operation. He spent weeks searching through statements and old conversations. The tax itself was not the worst part. The worst was the stress of rebuilding a whole year from memory. A ten-minute record each month would have avoided all of that. In short: under 365 days can mean 28 per cent on the gain, 365 days or more is, as a rule, exemption, and the record is your best ally.
