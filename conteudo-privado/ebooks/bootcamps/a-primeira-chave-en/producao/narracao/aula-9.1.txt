A daily chart shows the week that has passed. The weekly chart shows the year. If you only look at one of them, you are seeing half the film.

Today we are going to read Momentum on the weekly chart. You already know the five confirmations: the cloud present and in the right colour, the cloud on the right side of the candles, the exponential moving average in agreement, the volume in the colour of the direction, and the RSI crossing in the right zone. On the weekly chart, each candle is a whole week. That changes the rhythm of the reading. A weekly reading does not change from Friday to Monday. So it is a background frame, not a button. Anyone who reads the weekly chart every morning is looking for noise under a magnifying glass. And the risk of loss is real, on any chart, and it always stays with you.

Read the confirmations the way you read them on the daily chart, but with more patience. Five out of five, in green: the long-term trend is in favour. Four out of five is still a useful reading. Three or fewer: the weekly chart does not have a strong opinion, and that is also information. The question you ask is simple: does the weekly chart confirm what the daily chart is saying? If the daily is green and the weekly is red, that is not a contradiction to resolve in a hurry. It is a sign that the fall may not be over, and that the pot should be used with more calm, not more haste.

Let us go through three typical scenarios. First: the weekly chart has turned green after a long fall. It is a good moment to confirm that the pot is ready, because Strong Buy weeks may come next. Second: strong red for several weeks. Here the rule is the same as in module seven: base always, and the pot kept still until the weekly chart starts to ease. Third: lilac boxes on the weekly chart, a sideways market. For the DCA, it is a period to accumulate with patience, with no hurry to use the pot. In none of these three cases does the weekly chart stop the DCA. It only helps you decide the size.

Now the rhythm, which is the part most people get wrong. You read the weekly chart once a month, on a quiet weekend, to see the whole picture and review your plan. You read the daily chart every Friday, within your ritual. The weekly reading does not replace the daily one: the weekly sets the context, and the daily decides the day. If you ever catch yourself opening the weekly chart every morning, stop, breathe and go back to the notebook. That is anxiety dressed up as analysis, and it costs a lot in hasty decisions.

A common mistake is looking on the weekly chart for the exact day to buy. That day does not exist. The weekly chart gives you the map: the direction of the background and the weight of the trend. It does not tell you whether tomorrow is the best day for anything. And there is another trap: changing your plan just because the weekly chart changed colour once. Wait for the change to be confirmed over another one or two candles. If your plan is long term, the weekly chart serves to adjust the size of the pot, and never to stop the DCA. Never.

Three-minute exercise. Open the weekly chart of an asset in your plan, count the five confirmations and write the result in the notebook, with the date. Then write, in one sentence, whether the plan changes or not. If it does not change, write “no change”. It may look like a formality, but it is discipline. Six months from now, you will have a history of readings, not a memory of when you happened to remember. And memory, as you know, tends to side with the story that suits us. If the weekly chart is unreadable to you, that is fine. Write “cannot read it” and ask yourself why. Very often it is a lack of habit, and habit comes with repetition.
