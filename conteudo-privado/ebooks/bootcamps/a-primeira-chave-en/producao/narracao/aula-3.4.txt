Your first purchase should be the smallest of all. It isn't timidity. It's method.

We've reached the moment of buying. Before that, I want you to set a limit. Not a profit target, a loss limit. An amount that, if it disappears, won't change your life. Choose it calmly, without rushing. Ask yourself: how much money can I lose without hurting my income, my food, the household bills, my emergency fund? That amount, or a small fraction of it, is your first euro. If you can't find an amount like that, don't buy. There's no hurry, and no coin will vanish because you waited a week. A loss limit isn't a forecast. It's a rule you write for yourself, before the market starts talking.

Let's go through the practical steps, so you don't improvise. One: turn on two-factor authentication on the platform. It's the code that goes to your phone when you log in. Without it, anyone who finds out your password can get into your account. Two: confirm the address of everything you're going to use. The site, the app, the platform's name. Type the address by hand or save it in your bookmarks, and never click on links that reach you by message.

Three: do a test run first. A small deposit, a small purchase and, if relevant, a small transfer to check the route works. Only then do you increase. The first operation is there to learn the process, not to make money. If something doesn't add up, you'll find out with little at stake. And a golden rule: don't test with the amount you intend to use for the big purchase. The test is for learning the route, not for testing your luck.

Four: buy only after you've made the decision, not during a price move. When the screen is flashing green, the temptation is to jump in now. That urge is the first sign to stop. Decisions made under the pressure of a chart are rarely the best, because the chart knows nothing about your life. A useful trick: write the decision down in a note before you open the app, with the amount and the reason. If the urge to buy passes, the note is still there, and you decide with a cool head.

Five: record everything. Date, amount, price, fee, order type, reason. It sounds like paperwork, but that's where you learn. In three months' time, you'll look back at this record and understand better than you do today what worked and what didn't. People who don't keep records tend to remember only what went well, and that fools anyone. If, when you review the record, you notice you buy whenever the price goes up and make mistakes when it goes down, you already have precious information about yourself. That's worth more than any technical analysis.

Illustrative case: Inês did exactly this. She chose an amount she didn't need, turned on authentication, tested with a small deposit and wrote each step in a notebook. She didn't get rich or poor in the first month, and that wasn't the goal anyway. She ended up with a process, and that's what we want you to have. The process is what protects you when emotions get in the way. And if one day the checklist tells you no, that isn't a failure. It's the process working.

Exercise: create a five-line checklist with the steps I've just gone through, and tick each one off as you do it. Don't move on to buying until you have all five. If you think one line doesn't apply, write down why, and talk to someone you trust before you cross it off. Let me repeat the essentials of this module: educational content, not financial advice, and the risk of loss is real. If the amount you've chosen doesn't let you sleep soundly, it's because it's too high.
