The crypto market goes up and down with a force that frightens anyone who is not prepared. The question is not how to get it right. It is how to read it without guessing.

Let's start with volatility, which is the size of price movements. In crypto it is very high. Bitcoin has fallen more than seventy percent from its peak, on more than one occasion, and many other coins have fallen even further. Before you buy, ask yourself an honest question: if my balance fell by half, could I carry on without selling in a panic? If the answer is no, your exposure is too big, whatever the videos say. This question is worth more than any forecast.

Many people talk about cycles: a phase of accumulation, a strong rise, a euphoria in which everyone seems rich, then a long decline and a period of exhaustion. Cycles are a useful way of reading the past, because they help you recognise patterns of human behaviour. But they are not a calendar. In the euphoria it seems the whole world has got rich, and in the decline it seems it will never recover. Nobody knows exactly when the phase changes. There is a dangerous phrase you hear in every cycle: this time is different. Sometimes there are real changes, but the wish for it to be different is always the same old emotion. Anyone who swears they know is almost always trying to sell you something. You do not need to memorise any of this.

Another indicator is bitcoin dominance, which is the percentage of the total market value that belongs to bitcoin. When dominance rises, money tends to concentrate in bitcoin, which is seen as the most established asset. When it falls, there can be more appetite for altcoins, which are riskier and more volatile coins. It is not a magic rule, and it can mislead you if you read it on its own. But it is a way of seeing where the money is going, and that is already useful information for your plan. Notice that dominance also rises when the market falls back, because smaller coins usually drop harder.

Learn to separate news from noise. News is a verifiable fact: a law passed, a confirmed technical failure, a concrete change in supply or regulation. Noise is an opinion repeated a thousand times, the alarmist headline, and the video with epic music and red arrows. Before you react, ask: has this changed anything in the project, or has it only changed people's mood? If it is only mood, you usually do not need to act today. A good habit is to save the news in a list and review that list a week later. If nobody is talking about it any more, it was probably noise.

Reading the market without guessing means accepting that you do not know what comes tomorrow, and organising yourself for that. Define your plan before things happen. Write down what would make you change your mind, and trust that piece of paper more than the noise of the moment. Check your balance once a week, not every minute, because watching nonstop increases emotion and reduces clarity. Someone without a plan ends up reacting to everything, and reacting to everything is usually expensive, because decisions get made out of fear or euphoria. The market owes you nothing, and that is not a problem. The problem is being left without a plan.

To sum up this lesson: volatility shows you how much you could suffer, cycles show you patterns that are not a calendar, dominance shows you where the money is going, and verifiable news is separated from noise. None of these pieces gives you the right answer for tomorrow. Together, they give you something better, which is a criterion for deciding calmly. If any of these ideas seem complicated, that is fine. Just start with one of them and write down what you observed. In the last lesson of this module we will turn all of this into a concrete buying plan.
