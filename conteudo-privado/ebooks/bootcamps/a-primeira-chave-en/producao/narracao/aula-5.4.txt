The scam that's hardest to stop doesn't start with a website. It starts with a friendly conversation that gets closer and closer.

There's a scam that begins slowly. Someone meets you on a dating app, or in a group chat, and the relationship grows closer over several weeks. You share routines, photos, jokes and confidences. When the subject of money comes up, the person talks about a crypto investment that's going really well for them, and offers to let you in too. They show you screenshots of profits and a platform with a beautiful dashboard. The closer the relationship gets, the harder it is to be suspicious. It comes disguised as friendship, and that's what makes it so dangerous. Look at the dashboard. Those numbers are designed to look real, and whoever shows them to you controls everything you see. It proves nothing. A platform that only exists inside a screen someone else shows you doesn't prove a profit, it only proves that somebody knows how to draw a chart. If you can't withdraw the money without paying more fees, or without waiting weeks for a new verification, the problem is there, not in your bad luck.

So let's go through the ten-point checklist, which you can print and keep. One: nobody asks you for your seed phrase, your password or a code, ever. Two: you type the address by hand or use a verified bookmark, and you never click a link that reached you by message. Three: the platform is authorized under MiCA, the European regulation for crypto-asset service providers, and you can confirm that on the register of the authority in the country where it operates. Four: you turn on two-factor authentication with an app, not with SMS. Five: you use a unique password, stored in a password manager. Six: you're suspicious of any guaranteed return or any doubling. Seven: time pressure is an alarm, not an incentive.

Eight: you never approve a contract you don't understand. Nine: you never install remote-access software at the request of a stranger, because whoever controls your screen controls everything you do. Ten: before you move any money, you wait twenty-four hours and talk to someone you trust. That last rule protects more people than any software does. Haste is the fuel of every scam, and waiting is the cheapest antidote there is.

No single defense is enough, so think in layers. The first layer is your email account, with a strong, unique password. The second is app-based authentication on the platforms you use. The third is a verified address, always the same one, saved in your bookmarks. The fourth is withdrawal limits and the allowed-address list, when the platform offers that option. And the fifth, the most important one for large amounts, is keeping most of it in a cold wallet, away from any screen connected to the internet. If one layer fails, the next one still protects you.

When someone pressures you to decide today, you're not talking to a friend who wants to help you. You're talking to someone who needs you not to think. A serious investment can withstand a week of reflection, and an honest project doesn't get angry when you ask for time. If the person gets irritated, disappears or makes you feel guilty for hesitating, you already have your answer. Your calm is the best defense you have, and it costs nothing.

The module's mission is simple and concrete. This week, print the ten-point checklist and place it next to your computer. Check whether your platforms have app-based authentication. Review the active approvals in the wallets you use, and revoke any you don't recognize. And write your seed phrase on paper, by hand, in a safe place, without photographing it or sending it to anyone. When you've done all of this, tick the word done. This is the first key to your shield, and it's the one that matters most.
