Leverage is the button most people confuse with position size. And confusing the two is the shortest route to losing more than you wanted.

Let's separate three things that look like one. Leverage is the multiplier the platform gives you to open a position bigger than the money you have deposited. Size, or notional, is the total value of the position. And risk is how much you lose if the stop is hit. Leverage does not create risk out of nothing. It changes how much money you have to set aside as margin, and it makes it easy to open large positions without noticing. If the size and the stop have not changed, the risk has not changed either, whatever leverage you chose.

The Aurum Flow account panel shows four things for the size you chose. The useful leverage: the leverage the position needs for the size you chose, not the maximum the platform offers. The notional, which is the total value of the position. The cost in fees, on entry and exit, which eats part of the result. And the risk as a percentage per trade. The values on screen are blurred, because they do not matter for the lesson. What matters is learning to read all four, not memorizing numbers.

Apply this to the example from a moment ago: a thousand-euro account, a five-hundred-euro position. The useful leverage is low, about one times. That means the position fits in the account without stretching anything. Now imagine the panel showed you ten times for the same position. What would change? The size would still be five hundred, and the stop would still be at two per cent. The only thing that would change is the margin the platform asks to hold the position. Whoever chooses the leverage is not choosing the risk. The risk was already written into the stop and the formula.

The rule you write in your notebook is this, without exceptions: the leverage I use is the one the calculated size requires, never the maximum offered, even if the platform presents it as the normal option. If the account fits without leverage, I don't use leverage. If the position needs some leverage, the panel shows it to you. And you always check the notional before you press confirm. It is not a rule of fear, it is a rule of precision. The platform shows the maximum because that is what it shows. Your interest is different: it is the size you calculated in the previous lesson, and nothing else.

Finally, fees. In an illustrative example, with a fee of zero point one per cent per side, a five-hundred-euro position pays fifty cents on entry and fifty cents on exit. It seems little. But with a two per cent stop and a risk of ten euros, one euro of fees is ten per cent of the risk you defined. It is not a detail. That is why the panel shows the cost, and why you look at it before you confirm, not after. The real fee is your platform's, and it is in its terms. Check it. Don't use mine as if it were yours.

Summary. Leverage is how the platform asks for margin. Size is the value of the position. Risk is the loss at the stop. Don't confuse the three. The Aurum Flow panel shows the useful leverage, the notional, the fees and the risk per trade, and your rule is to look at all four before pressing any button.
