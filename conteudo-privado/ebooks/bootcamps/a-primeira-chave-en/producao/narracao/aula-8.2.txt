Everyone asks how much to put into each coin. The honest answer starts with a different question: what is each one for?

Let us look at the three most common pieces of a crypto portfolio. Bitcoin is the oldest and best-known coin, which is why many people use it as the base of the core. Ethereum is a platform on which applications run, and its role is different: it is a bet on how that platform is used, with more variables along the way. Stablecoins are tokens, generally tied to a currency such as the dollar. They try to keep a stable value, but they are not money in your bank account, and they carry risks of their own. Each one answers a different question. Do not throw them all in the same bag.

Let us do some sums with an illustrative example. Imagine a plan of one hundred euros a month and a rule you wrote yourself: the core is Bitcoin, the satellites are Ethereum, and a small part stays in stablecoins. One possible design, just so you can see the sum, would be seventy, twenty, ten. Seventy euros in Bitcoin, twenty in Ethereum, ten in stablecoins. Notice that this is not a recommendation. It is an example, to show how a percentage turns into euros, and to make clear that your design has to fit your plan, not what you saw in someone else's video.

Now, what happens when the market moves. Imagine, illustrative example, that the Ethereum part falls twenty per cent. The twenty euros you put in there are now worth sixteen. That is not a tragedy, it is arithmetic. What matters is that the split of your plan does not change because of it. The following month is still one hundred euros, and the split is still seventy, twenty and ten. If you change the split in the middle of a drop, you are deciding out of fear. If you still need to change it, record the change in the notebook and write down why, with the date.

Let us go through the role of each piece, which is what should guide your choice. Bitcoin has the role of base, with a long history, a lot of liquidity and a lot of discussion around it. Ethereum has the role of a bet on the platform, with more complexity and more factors that can change. Stablecoins have the role of parking and liquidity, never of growth, and they bring issuer risk, risk linked to the reference currency, and regulatory risk. Notice that what can go wrong is written next to each one. Anyone who only writes the good side has not yet done the work.

A practical rule: set a cap per coin before you start. Illustrative example: no satellite goes above ten per cent of the plan, and stablecoins do not go above twenty per cent, even if they seem safe. These numbers are only there so you can do your own sums. What matters is that the cap exists, that it is written down and that you keep it. If your plan is small, do the sums with five or ten euros and see whether the design still makes sense. Often it does not, and it is much better to find that out on paper than with the money already on the platform.

Exercise: write your monthly plan, say one hundred and fifty euros, and split it into three lines: BTC, ETH and stablecoins. Use a design that seems reasonable to you. Then do the sum for one month in which the smallest part falls twenty per cent, illustrative example, and for another in which it rises. It is not about getting it right. It is about seeing whether you could keep the plan without touching it. If the answer is “no”, the design is too big for your wallet or for your stomach. Adjust it until you can stick to it.
