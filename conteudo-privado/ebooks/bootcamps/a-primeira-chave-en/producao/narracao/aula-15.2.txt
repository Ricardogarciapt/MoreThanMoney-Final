Ninety days sounds like a long time. But what you are going to see is a calendar with three phases and one rule: don't skip any.

The ninety-day plan has three phases, and each one has its mission. Days one to thirty: fundamentals and first purchase. Days thirty-one to sixty: the DCA ritual, the Friday one, done every week and recorded. Days sixty-one to ninety: level three, which is optional, and which only happens in a simulated account. Notice the order. First you understand your key and your money, then you buy with rules, and only then do you practise whatever you want to practise, with no real risk. There are no shortcuts between the phases. If one goes wrong, repeat it before you move on.

Phase one, days one to thirty. Week one: security. Authenticator app, password manager, and the seed phrase written by hand, kept in two separate places. Week two: you choose an authorised platform, complete identity verification, and understand the fees and the difference between a market order and a limit order. Week three: the first purchase, with a small amount, for example ten euros, followed by a test transfer to your wallet. Illustrative example, of course. Week four: you review the anti-scam checklist and sign the level one mission. Without the signed checklist, level one is not closed.

Phase two, days thirty-one to sixty. This is where the Friday ritual comes in, which you learned in module 7 with the MTM Smart DCA, and everything you put together in modules 8, 9 and 10: your Portfolio with the reserve pot, the reading of the market, and the rules for taking profits. The level two mission is simple to state and hard to fulfil: a written DCA plan, with the monthly cap and the pot, the DCA alerts switched on in the app, and the ritual done four weeks in a row, recorded in the notebook. If one Friday is missed, you don't start over. You record what happened and come back the following Friday. What counts is consistency, not perfection.

Phase three, days sixty-one to ninety, and here it is optional. If you want to move on to level three, with the Aurum Flow Cripto and the perpetuals, the rule is the same one we saw in module 14: simulated account, written stopping rules, and thirty trades with a complete journal before you think about a real account. If you don't want to, that's fine. An investor who does DCA with discipline and reviews their plan does not need derivatives to be doing good work. Derivatives are a choice, never an obligation, and never with money you need.

Illustrative case: Sara made the plan with the notebook beside her. In phase one, she reached week three without buying anything, because she didn't trust the platform. Instead of jumping to phase two, she repeated week two, did the verification and only then bought. In phase two, she missed a Friday because of a trip. She noted the gap in the notebook and carried on the following week. When she reached ninety days, she had not done everything perfectly. She had done everything in the right order. And that is what the plan asks for.

One last rule for the plan. Write it on a sheet, with the real start and end dates of each phase, and sign it. Put the review of each phase in the calendar, as if it were a booked appointment. And don't move on to the next phase just because you feel bored. Boredom is normal in DCA, and that is exactly where most people give up. If the phase is closed, you move on. If it isn't, you go back to the missing week. This is not a race. It is a calendar, and the calendar is yours. Keep a copy of the plan in the course notebook, so you always have it to hand.
