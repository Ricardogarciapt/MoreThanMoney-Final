A second opinion is only worth something if it is not a copy of the first. So let us see what DEFY asks that Momentum does not.

You already know DEFY from the earlier lessons. It draws exhaustion bands around a smooth average: an inner one, a middle one and an outer one. The question DEFY asks is a different one. While Momentum asks whether the direction is in favour, DEFY asks whether the move has already gone too far. These are different questions, which is why they can disagree. When they disagree, nothing is broken. There is information. And that information is worth more than a forced agreement. We are not going to repeat everything you saw in module seven. We are going to look at DEFY as an extra reading, which comes after Momentum, not before it.

When the price reaches the outer band and turns with a strong candle, DEFY may mark an idea. The stars count the confirmations: has the level already reacted before? Is the trend cloud on the same side? Is there a volume break? Three confirmations give one star, more give two or three, and when everything lines up the candle turns gold. The stars measure how many confirmations there are. They do not measure the certainty of the result, because that does not exist. A gold candle is not a guarantee of a rise. It is a stretched price with many pieces fitting together, and that is all it is.

Let us go through the combinations. Momentum green and DEFY with stars: both readings in favour. If the MTM Smart DCA label also helps, it is a week to use the pot with judgement. Momentum green and DEFY without stars: one reading in favour. Momentum red or lilac with stars on DEFY: also one reading in favour, because a buy idea exists, but the trend does not yet agree. And Momentum red or lilac without stars: no reading in favour, and the base is enough. Notice that there is no row that says “buy everything”. That row does not exist, and if you find it in some group or other, be suspicious of it.

Let us put this into a simple frame, to stick next to the screen. Two readings in favour: base plus pot, within the ceiling. One reading in favour: base plus, at most, half the pot. None: only the base. Above everything sits the monthly ceiling, which is never breached, and the MTM Smart DCA label, which gives the starting point. DEFY is a second opinion. It is not the first criterion, and it has no power to cancel the label. It helps you choose how much of the pot to use, and that is all. Print it or copy it by hand, and always keep it in front of you on Friday, so that the decision does not depend on who is most tired that day.

Repeat after me: DEFY signals were designed for trading. They carry an entry, a stop and targets, because that is what a trader needs. In DCA you do not use those levels. You do not buy at the “entry price”, you do not place a stop and you do not take profit at targets. Your DCA is long term, and DEFY only affects the size of the top-up. If one day you want to trade with rules, that is a different path, with different training, a different account and a different risk. It is not this one. And having DEFY on the screen does not give your DCA a stop.

Exercise: open the daily chart of an asset in your plan with DEFY. Count the stars, if there are any. Then look at the Momentum on the same chart. Write down the two readings and the decision: base, base plus half the pot, or base plus the pot, always within the ceiling. Do this for a week, without buying anything extra. You will see that the two tools disagree more often than you imagined, and that the disagreement teaches more than agreement does. If there are no stars in any week, that is also a valid reading. Record that there was no idea and follow the plan, without looking for a star that is not there.
