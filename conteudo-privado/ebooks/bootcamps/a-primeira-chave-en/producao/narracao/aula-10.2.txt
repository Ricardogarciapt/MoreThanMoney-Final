A day of heavy falls does not look like a crisis. It looks like a notification, a friend calling and a finger that wants to press sell. That is why the plan has to be written beforehand.

Large falls happen in crypto. I am not going to tell you when or how big, because I do not know, and nobody knows. What I do know is that they happen often enough to deserve a plan. And the moment when decisions are most often made badly is precisely the moment of the fall, when the screen is red, the conversations are in panic and the brain wants to do something, anything, right now. The solution is not to have more courage in the moment. It is to have fewer decisions to make in that moment. That is why we write the plan today, when we are calm, and not tomorrow, when it is already on fire.

The plan for a crash has three parts. Before: what you already have set up. Your base amount, your monthly ceiling, your reserve pot and the alerts switched on in the app. During: what you do in each band of decline, written with the same logic as the MTM Smart DCA, in which the Strong Buy label changes the size of the top-up and does not tell you to stop. After: when and how you review the strategy. One page is enough. If it does not fit on one page, it is still being thought out too much, and on the day of the fall nobody reads twenty.

Now the during, which is the heart of the plan. For each band of decline, you write the action. For example, illustrative example: if the asset falls more than thirty per cent against your average price, the DCA continues on the base. If the pot has money, you use part of it, within the ceiling, as module seven teaches. You do not sell anything in that band. Notice this carefully: the DCA never stops, it only changes size. A fall does not give you permission to stop buying, and it does not oblige you to buy more than the ceiling allows. If your strategy has a position you genuinely want to sell in a fall, that is a separate written rule, with its own condition.

There is one rule that should be in every crash plan: the pause. When something makes you want to sell outside the plan, you wait twenty-four hours. It is not a punishment, it is a filter. A great deal of panic decisions disappear within twenty-four hours. If after the pause you still think the sale makes sense, write down why, with the condition that justifies it, and check whether it matches the rule you wrote for the crash. If it does not match, keep to the plan. And switch off price notifications during the day of the fall. Looking at the chart every five minutes does not help you think. It only gives you the feeling that you are doing something.

I will tell you this plainly: in a fall, the people around you will say things. Some will tell you to sell everything, others to buy everything, and others will show charts that look like certainties. None of them has your table. That is why the plan stays written and signed by you, kept where you can see it. If you only have it in your head, in a fall your head will tell you whatever it likes. Also write down the contact of one trusted person who can remind you of the plan. Not to decide for you. To remind you of what you decided when you were calm.

Mission for this lesson, ten minutes: write your crash plan on half a page. Before, during and after. Include at least three bands of decline, with the action for each one, the twenty-four-hour pause rule and the name of the trusted person. At the end, sign and date it.
