Saying 'there is a trend' is easy. Proving it with numbers is another story. Three averages and a strength indicator are there for that.

To measure the trend, Aurum Flow uses three DEMA averages, of 15, 50 and 238 periods. A DEMA lags less than a simple average and reacts faster. The 15 shows the recent move, the 50 the medium term, the 238 the background. When all three are ordered in the same direction and the price is on the same side, the filter says there is a trend. When they are tangled together, the filter says there isn't. And a filter that says no is as useful as one that says yes, because it saves you a bad entry.

Notice the difference. On the left, the three averages are ordered, with the fastest on top and the slowest at the bottom, and the price is trading above them. That is an uptrend. On the right, the lines cross each other and the price keeps crossing them from one side to the other. That is sideways. Both are illustrative examples. If you look at ten charts like these, you will see that sideways shows up more often than you would like. With the filter, it is a no without drama, not a signal you have to force.

The trend filter tells you which way to look, but not when. That is where the momentum confirmation comes in, done with the RSI. The RSI measures the recent strength of moves on a scale from zero to one hundred. On a buy entry, we want the RSI to confirm strength in the same direction as the signal, not turn against it. On a sell entry, it is the opposite. There is no magic number here for you to memorize. The rule is the direction: momentum has to be on the side of the trend.

Put the two parts together and you have the entry gate. First, the three DEMA say there is a trend and say which way it goes. Second, the RSI confirms that the strength is going in the same direction. Only with both, the signal is eligible. If one fails, there is no signal. That is not a preference, it is a closed door. And notice that this does not oblige you to pick the top or the bottom. It obliges you to wait for the market to show its hand before you show yours. Don't jump to conclusions from what you have just heard.

Do the exercise you will repeat in every lesson of this module. Choose a one-hour chart. First question: are the three averages ordered? Second: does the RSI confirm the same direction? Third: do both answers point to the same side? If the answer to any of them is no, write no signal and move on to the next chart. It is not a failure. It is the filter doing its job, and doing it without asking you for an opinion. There is no shortcut here, and that is why it is worth practising. Write this in your notebook before you move on.

Summary. The three DEMA answer the trend question. The RSI answers the momentum question. The signal only exists when both answers point the same way. There is no prediction here. There is a question asked the same way every time, so that you don't decide based on your mood of the day.
