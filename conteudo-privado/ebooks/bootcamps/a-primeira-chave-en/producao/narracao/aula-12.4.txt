When the price goes your way, the problem changes. It is no longer what you do if you are wrong. It is what you do when you are right.

The three targets sit at one and a half, three and six times the risk. They are not three predictions. They are three decision points. The first is the nearest, so it is the one reached first. The last is the most ambitious, and it may never be reached. A signal that reaches the second target did not fail at the first. Each target is a different moment, with its own decision, and that is why the plan separates them instead of lumping them into a single number. Don't jump to conclusions from what you have just heard.

Now the part that changes how you trade: partial exits. The idea is simple. You don't have to close the whole position in the same place. You can close one part at the first target, another at the second, and leave a part running for the third. What you have already collected stays collected, and what remains has room to run. But the percentage of each part is not a number you pick on top of the chart. It is a rule you write before entering. Decided during the trade, it is already emotion, and emotion tends to choose the wrong exit.

Break-even is the step most people skip and later regret. When the price hits the first target, you can move the stop to the entry. If the market comes back, the part still open closes near zero, instead of giving back the full risk. Careful: this is your rule. Don't count on any button doing it for you, and the decision to apply it is entirely yours. Write it in the words you will use. For example: when the first target is hit, I close one part and move the stop to the entry.

Let's look at this with an illustrative example, with the entry at one hundred and the stop at ninety-eight. The price rises to one hundred and three. Say you close one part and move the stop to one hundred. Then the market turns back and reaches one hundred. The part still open closes at break-even, and the part you collected at the first target stays as profit. If the price had dropped right after the entry, the loss would be the original stop, and nothing more. What the rule changes is what happens after the plan has proven it was right.

Now the notebook exercise. Write four lines: the percentage you close at the first target, the one you close at the second, the part you leave running for the third, and the stop rule. You don't need to copy anyone's percentages, not even the ones that appear in this course. You need to have your own. And also write down what you will do if the price reaches the second target and turns back. Whoever doesn't have that line written always decides at the last minute, and at the last minute nobody decides well. Write this in your notebook before you move on.

Summary. Three targets, three decisions. Partial exits that you define before entering. Break-even as a written rule, which protects the trade once the plan has been right. None of this guarantees a profit. It is a way of not letting emotion decide what you do with an open trade. In the next lesson, the part that protects the most: when not to enter. Most of a beginner's mistakes do not happen inside the trade. They happen before it, when they enter without a reason. Pay close attention to this, because it is the basis of everything that follows. There is no shortcut here, and that is why it is worth practising.
