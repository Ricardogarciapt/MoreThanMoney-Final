The worst decision in an account is rarely the first loss. It is the fourth, after a bad morning, when you decide that 'now is the time'.

Last lesson of the module, and perhaps the one that saves you the most. Everything we have seen so far is about single trades. But whoever loses money almost always loses in sequence. One loss, then another, then a third, and the head starts asking for an immediate recovery. That urge has a name: chasing losses. And the antidote is not willpower, which runs out quickly. It is a rule written before the day starts. Whoever decides what to do after three losses at ten in the morning is already deciding with a head in revenge mode. That is why the decision has to be made beforehand.

Look at this series, illustrative example. Three losses of one per cent each. On the thousand-euro account, that is around thirty euros. It hurts, but it is not the end. The problem starts when the fourth trade comes out bigger, because the person wants to recover quickly. At that moment, the risk stops being the one per cent of the rule and becomes a desperate bet. Nobody decides that on purpose. It happens gradually, with the justification that this time it pays off. The stopping rule exists to prevent that shift, and so you don't have to argue about it with yourself in the worst moment.

Write three stopping rules, with your own numbers. The first is the daily loss limit. For example, and it is only an illustrative example, two per cent of the balance in one day: once you hit it, you close the platform. The second is the losing streak: three losses in a row on the same day, and you stop. The third is the most important: after a stop, you don't come back to the screen that day, not even to see if the signal has improved. This is the rule that saves the most accounts, because the market owes you nothing and it will always be back tomorrow. The numbers you write are yours. The ones I use here are small on purpose.

Before each session, run a two-minute routine. Is the daily limit written down? Is the losing streak written down? Do you know the maximum number of trades you will take? Have you checked the news in the window? If the answer to any of these is no, you don't start. Notice that this routine says nothing about the market. It says everything about you. It is the part of trading that most resembles the Friday ritual of the DCA: rules before the money, not during it. Someone who has this routine done does not need to be smarter than the market. They need to be more predictable than their own emotions.

Daily limit and losing streak written down before the first signal. None of these rules guarantees a profit. What they do is limit what a bad streak can take from you, and that is already a lot.
