Liquidation is the moment when the platform closes your position because there is no longer enough collateral. Nobody asks you. It just happens.

Let us work through the liquidation sum step by step, with an illustrative sum. It is not anyone's actual account. These are round numbers, so you can see the mechanics. Imagine a simulated account with five hundred euros of margin on a long position, with ten times leverage. Ten times means the position has a notional value of five thousand euros. The five hundred is your collateral, the five thousand is the size of the bet. This difference is the centre of this lesson. And notice: the example is illustrative, and the platform you use may have different numbers and different rules.

When the price moves against your position, the loss comes out of the margin. With five thousand of notional, every one per cent move against you is worth fifty euros. If the price falls ten per cent, you lose five hundred, and the margin drops to zero. In a simplified account, that is where liquidation happens: when the loss consumes the margin. But this is a simplification, and the difference matters. In practice, the platform requires a minimum maintenance margin, and it liquidates before the margin reaches zero. So the real liquidation price sits a little closer to the entry than the simple sum says.

Now the price. Imagine the position is long with an entry at one hundred. With this simplified sum, the liquidation sits near ninety, because a ten per cent fall is what the five hundred of margin can absorb against the five thousand of notional. Notice the logic behind it: roughly one divided by the leverage. Ten times gives ten per cent. Twenty times gives five per cent. The higher the leverage, the shorter the distance to liquidation. In other words, leverage does not buy you more time. It shortens your margin for error. This figure is an illustrative example, and it is not a rule for any position.

And now the point I want you to keep. Liquidation does not warn you. There is no moment when the platform tells you it is about to close, and there is no guarantee that the price stops exactly at the value you calculated. In a fast market, with one long candle and suddenly, the price can pass the liquidation level in seconds, and the close happens at whatever price there is at that moment, which can be worse than the number you wrote down. So the liquidation price you see on screen is a reference limit, not a protection. Protection, if it exists, is somewhere else: it is the stop, which we will look at later in this level.

Mission for this lesson: take an illustrative account, in a simulation, and calculate the simplified liquidation for three leverage levels, five, ten and twenty. Write down the percentage distance to the liquidation price in each case. Then check it on the panel of the platform you use, if it has one. If the numbers do not match, look for the difference, which is usually in the maintenance margin and the fees. Do not open any real position to do this calculation, not even out of curiosity.
