Your seed is your entire account. And the worst thing you can do to it is not to lose it on an unlucky day. It is to leave it where someone can look at it at their leisure.

In lesson four we talked about your seed, the words that give access to your coins. Now we go one level up, because when the value grows, the risk changes shape. There are two threats that weigh the same: losing the seed, and someone else getting hold of it. Any security plan has to deal with both. The rule that is not negotiable is this: the seed never exists in digital form. Not a photo, not a note on your phone, not an email to yourself, not a file in the cloud, not a password manager. Any of these can be copied, synced or stolen, and all of that can happen without you noticing anything.

If it is not digital, is it paper? It can be, but paper is fragile. It burns, it gets wet, it breaks down over time, and a house move can throw away a folded sheet in a drawer. That is why the most solid option is metal: a plate or a holder where the words are engraved or punched, resistant to fire and water. Using paper as the first copy is not a mistake, as long as the metal copy follows. The goal is for the information to survive a fire, a flood or a day when your home is turned upside down.

Now the rule about copies. Two copies, in two different places. Not the same drawer, not the same safe, not the same house. If a fire or a burglary takes one, the other is still there. One option is a safe at home, well hidden and fixed to the structure. The other can be a safe in a trusted place, outside the home. There is no perfect place. There are places where the risk of loss is low and the risk of improper access is also low. Choose with that balance in mind, and write in the plan where each copy is.

A rule that sounds obvious and that many people break: nobody asks you for the seed. No legitimate support service, no authorised platform, no person saying they are from support asks for the words. If anyone asks, even if the request comes with a beautiful logo, an urgent message or a friendly phone call, the answer is no. And do not write it down on any website, in any third-party app, or for a friend who is helping you set something up. The seed is only used at the moment of restoring your wallet, in a place you control. Afterwards it goes back to the paper copy and the metal one.

How do you know the copy works? You do not open it to check, because every time you expose it there is a small exposure. What you do is a restore test, in a new, empty wallet, with none of your main value, following the procedure you already know from module four. If the test works, the copy is good. If it does not, you find out now, and not on the day you need it. Do this test once a year, or whenever you change wallets. And never put the copies together in one photo, one folder or one envelope.

Mission for this lesson, to do this week: check your seed against four questions. Is it free of any digital format? Do you have two copies? Are they in two different places? And have you done the restore test in a new wallet? If the answer to any of these is no, that is where the work is. It is not glamorous, but it is what separates someone who loses a phone from someone who loses access to their coins. In the last lesson of this module we talk about what happens to your seed and your coins when you are no longer around to look after them.
