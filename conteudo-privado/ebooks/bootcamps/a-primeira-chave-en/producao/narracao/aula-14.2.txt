Copying looks like the solution for those who don't have time. But there is one question almost nobody asks before switching it on: who answers for what happens afterwards?

Third piece of this module: MTM Auto. It is MoreThanMoney's tool for copying strategies, and the keyword is this: with rules and limits. In other words, it is not a button that copies everything blindly. Before you switch on a strategy, you have to see which rules it follows and which limits the tool applies. This is very different from copying someone who turned up in a message group, without knowing anything about what that person does. Here there are rules in plain view, and that is already half the way to an informed decision. But only half. The other half is yours.

Before you switch on any strategy, read three things. First, what it does: which markets it works in, with what kind of entries and how often. Second, the limits that apply on your side of the copy. Third, what happens when things go wrong. If you cannot say, in your own words, what the strategy does and what happens in the worst case, don't switch it on. And notice something that seems counterintuitive: a strategy that has been rising for a long time does not, for that reason, carry less risk. It may even carry more, because confidence grows just before a fall.

Now the part that matters most. Copying does not take away your responsibility. When you switch on a copy, you remain the owner of the decision to have switched it on. The tool executes, but whoever chose the strategy, whoever decided how much they are willing to risk, and whoever decides to stop, is you. This holds even when the strategy belongs to someone you trust, and even when the tool is the house's own. The tool executes what is written. It does not know how much of your money is rent money, it does not know you have a bill to pay on Friday, and it does not know that you are tired and eager to win back a loss. Only you know those things, and only you can take them into account.

Illustrative case. Inês, thirty-five, switched on a copy because she did not have time to follow the markets. For a few weeks it seemed perfect. Then a drop came, and she had not defined how much she was willing to lose. She put in more money to recover and, before she knew it, she was no longer copying a strategy. She was trying to win back a loss. The tool did exactly what was written. The mistake was not writing down her own limit. If Inês had defined, before switching it on, how much she could lose and what she would do when she reached that amount, the story would have been different. Illustrative example, of course.

Before copying, four questions, written in your notebook. Do I know what the strategy does? Do I know the limits that apply? Do I know how much I can lose, in euros, without it affecting my life? And do I know when I stop, and what I do that day? If any answer is no, the copy stays off. The practical exercise of this lesson is done in the simulated account: you switch on MTM Auto in a simulated account, observe for a week and write down, each day, what you felt. Copying is not the end of the process. It is just one more piece of it.
