Twelve or twenty-four words, in a precise order. Whoever has them has the money. Nobody else needs you for anything.

When you create a wallet, the app or the device generates a recovery phrase. It's called a seed phrase, or secret recovery phrase. It usually has twelve or twenty-four words, in a specific order. In practice, that phrase is the master key to your wallet. It isn't a password you can change whenever you like. It's the origin of the keys themselves, and whoever has it can recreate the wallet anywhere. Think of it as the combination of a safe, not the door: whoever knows it can open the safe from anywhere, at any time, and leave no trail that anyone can follow in time. Also note that the phrase is generated once, when you create the wallet. That's why it's the moment you need to be most alert.

Why does it matter so much? Because if you lose your phone, if the app fails, if the device breaks, you can recreate the wallet somewhere else with just those words. Without them, there is no recovery. And with them, whoever holds them can do everything you do. They don't need your password, your code or you. This is the difference that most people don't understand.

That's why there are rules that always apply. There are no exceptions. First rule: never enter your seed phrase on a website. Not to verify, not to recover, not to unlock. No serious platform, no legitimate support team, no official app will ever ask you for those words. Second rule: never send it to anyone, by message, email, call or photo. If someone introduces themselves as support and asks for the phrase, it's a scam. Full stop.

Third rule: never photograph it, and never store it on your phone, your computer, your email, a notes app or in the cloud. Any digital copy is a copy that can be stolen, often without you noticing. Fourth rule: write it by hand, on paper, with a pen. And never keep it in the same place as your bank card or your passwords.

Does this sound like overkill? Think about it. Most people who lose crypto don't lose it because of the market. They lose it because they gave the words to someone who phoned them saying they were from support. Illustrative example: Inês received a call from a number that claimed to belong to a platform. The person on the other end was friendly, had a professional tone, and asked her for the words to confirm her identity. She hung up. Only later did she realize she had come within a step of losing everything, and that the pressure was part of the scheme.

Notice the pattern. Urgency, authority, a request for words. It's always the same script, and only the platform's name and the tone of voice change. When you feel the pressure rising, stop. No legitimate person needs you in that much of a hurry. No message, no video and no call that starts with urgency is a good reason to unlock anything. Urgency is the favorite tool of anyone who wants your money before you've had time to think. And if the call pushes you to decide now, the answer is to hang up and call the official number yourself. Let's go through the correct way to store it. Take a clean sheet of paper, write the words one by one, with the number beside each, and check them twice. Then keep the sheet somewhere dry and secure. Don't fold it too often, because paper and damp don't get along. If you ever write a word down wrong, don't try to guess the correction on anyone's screen. Start the sheet again, calmly, at home, with nobody rushing you. And don't show it or photograph it, not even to remind yourself of a word. If you ever need to recover, use only the official app, downloaded from the official stores, and never a link someone sent you.
