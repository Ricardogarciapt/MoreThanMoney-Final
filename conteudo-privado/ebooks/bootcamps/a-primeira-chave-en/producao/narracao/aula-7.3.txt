The best way to learn to cook is not to read recipes. It is to stand in the kitchen next to someone. With DCA it is the same.

In the app there are two house portfolios: one crypto and one ETF. They are topped up every Friday, with fixed amounts per asset, and they are recorded in the app, purchase by purchase. You can see what was bought, when and at what price. It is an open DCA diary.

Notice the dates. Friday, Friday, Friday. Weeks of good markets, weeks of bad markets, and the purchase is there. This is what I want you to see: the hard part of DCA is not the maths. It is carrying on buying when everyone around you says it is nonsense, and not buying too much when everyone says it is now or never.

Also look at how the portfolio is divided. There is a larger weight on a few assets and smaller weights on others. I am not telling you to copy these assets. I am telling you to copy the idea: a portfolio has a structure, written down beforehand, and that structure does not change every week because of a headline.

Now do yours. Write down the assets in your plan, the weight of each one and the amount per week. If you are starting out, less is more. One or two assets are perfectly enough. Remember the seven questions from module two before you put any name on this list.

Use the house portfolios as a mirror, not as a template. When you have doubts, for example “should I buy this week, with the market falling like this?”, go and see what happened in the house portfolios in similar weeks. You will see that the answer, almost always, was to buy the base and move on.

The house portfolios exist for learning. What they did in the past says nothing about what they will do in the future, nor about what yours will do. In the next lesson we will go up a step and look at the chart, with a tool you already know by name: Momentum.
