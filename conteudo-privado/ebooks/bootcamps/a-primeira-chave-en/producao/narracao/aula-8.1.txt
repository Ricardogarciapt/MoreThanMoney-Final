In crypto, tax is not paid on the day you buy. It can, however, appear on the day you sell, if you are not prepared.

I am going to talk about taxes in general terms, because the rules change and the person who decides your situation is a certified accountant. That said, there is a basic principle you should know. Since 2023, in Portugal, crypto capital gains on assets held for less than 365 days are taxed at a rate of 28 percent. If you hold them for 365 days or more, they are, as a rule, exempt. A capital gain, mais-valia in Portuguese, is the positive difference between the sale value and the purchase value. If you sell for less than you paid, there is no gain to tax. If you live outside Portugal, the rules where you live are different, so check with your own tax authority or an accountant. The principle is simple. It is the details that change the sum, and they are what catch you off guard. For example, the purchase date and the sale date are counted in days, and a single day's difference can change everything.

Let's look at an illustrative example, with round figures and no fees. You buy one thousand euros of crypto and, three months later, you sell for one thousand three hundred. The capital gain is three hundred euros. On those three hundred you apply the twenty-eight percent, which gives eighty-four euros. Notice this carefully: the tax does not apply to the full value of one thousand three hundred, only to the gain. If the same investment had been held for 365 days or more, the general rule would be different, and the sum would, as a rule, be exempt. All of this can change, so always confirm the current version before drawing conclusions. Also remember that the fees paid when buying and selling can reduce the gain, which is why they go into the record.

So, record everything. Every purchase, every sale and every swap, with the date, the asset, the quantity, the value in euros at that moment and the fees paid. Add a column for the type of operation, so you can see at a glance what was a purchase, a sale or a swap. Do not rely on memory or loose screenshots, because a year from now you will not remember everything. A simple spreadsheet is enough to start. When the time comes to declare, you will thank yourself, and so will the accountant. If you swap one coin for another, record both sides of the operation, because both count.

Now the most important warning in this lesson. Crypto-to-crypto swaps, staking income, airdrops and other cases can be treated differently, and I don't want to guess that in a video. There is also the option of joint taxation with your income, which is called englobamento in Portuguese, where the gain is added to your annual income. It can be more advantageous or less, depending on your tax bracket, and that comparison needs someone who sees your full numbers. Do not make tax decisions based on a two-minute video, or on what someone said in a group chat. Take your record sheet and the questions you wrote down beforehand to the meeting with the accountant.

Illustrative case: Pedro, 31, a designer in Lisbon, made several purchases and sales over the year without recording anything. When it came time to declare, he did not know the exact dates, nor the amount he had paid in each transaction. He spent weeks searching through statements and old conversations. The tax itself was not even the worst part. The worst part was the stress of rebuilding a whole year from memory. A ten-minute record each month would have prevented all of that, and would have saved hours of doubt.

To sum up: less than 365 days can mean 28 percent on the gain, 365 days or more is, as a rule, exemption, and your record is your best ally.
