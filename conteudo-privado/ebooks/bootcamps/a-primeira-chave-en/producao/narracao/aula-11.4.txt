A trade can look as though it went well, and once you do the sum on the fees, nothing is left. This lesson is about the sums nobody does in front of the screen.

Let us talk about money that leaves without being seen. With perpetuals there are at least three kinds of cost. The opening fee, the closing fee and the funding we have already seen. There is also a hidden cost, which is the spread, the difference between the buy price and the sell price at that moment. Each one is small, but added up and multiplied by the leverage and by how often you trade, they can eat a large part of what you thought you had. This invoice is illustrative, with no values from any particular platform. Your authorised platform has its own fee tables, and you have to read them before you open anything.

The fee depends on how you enter. When you place an order that waits in the order book, and is not executed straight away, you usually pay a lower fee, called maker. When the order executes immediately and takes liquidity from the market, you pay a higher fee, the taker fee. Platforms vary, so check the fee menu of your authorised platform before you open, for each type of order. Also ask yourself whether the order type you are using is the one you want, and not simply the fastest one to click.

Illustrative example, with round numbers. A notional of five thousand euros, and a fee of zero point zero five per cent on each side of the trade, opening and closing. Opening costs two euros and fifty cents. Closing costs another two euros and fifty cents. Total, five euros, on a trade of five thousand. That seems little. Now look at the margin: five hundred euros. Five euros on five hundred is one per cent of the collateral, in fees alone, before the market has moved. If you repeat this trade ten times with the same margin, that is fifty euros, ten per cent of the collateral, going to the platform. None of those fifty euros appears on the chart.

This is where the account panel of Aurum Flow Cripto helps. It shows the notional, the useful leverage and the fee cost for the size of your account, before you open anything. The question you should always ask is this: if the trade does exactly what the plan says, how much is left after the fees? If the answer is almost nothing, the trade may not be worth the effort, however good it looks on the chart. This is not a guarantee of anything. It is a sum done in your head, and it protects you from an illusion.

Five-minute exercise, on paper. A margin of one thousand euros, leverage of five times, and a fee of zero point zero five per cent per side. What is the notional? Five thousand. How much does opening cost? Two euros and fifty cents. How much does closing cost? Another two euros and fifty cents. What is the total cost, as a percentage of the margin? Half a per cent. Now repeat it with leverage of twenty times, keeping the same margin. You do not need to get your platform's fee exactly right. You need to understand that the cost rises with the notional, not with the margin you put in.
