A portfolio moves on its own. One coin rises, another falls, and the split you wrote three months ago no longer exists. What do you do about it?

It is called drift. Illustrative example: you started with seventy per cent in Bitcoin, twenty in Ethereum and ten in stablecoins. A year later, Bitcoin has risen a lot and you have eighty per cent. Ethereum and the stablecoins have fallen to twelve and eight. Nobody gave you a signal. The portfolio simply changed shape. Rebalancing is the act of going back to the split you wrote down, using a rule you defined beforehand, and not the mood of the day. It is not a guess about what is going to happen. It is keeping the risk you chose.

There are two simple ways to rebalance. The first is by calendar: once a quarter, you review the split and correct whatever has drifted. The second is by band: you only act when a part drifts further than a certain number of points from its target. Illustrative example: a five-point band. If the core is written as seventy, you act only if it leaves sixty-five or seventy-five. Each method has its advantages. The calendar is simpler to follow. The band only reacts when the drift is real. Choose one, write it in the notebook and keep to it, with no exceptions invented on the spot.

Now the reserve pot, which you already know from module seven. It is the part of the plan's money that was not spent in a given week. It does not disappear and it is not lost: it is kept aside, and it has one use only. Topping up in weeks when the MTM Smart DCA label is Strong Buy, within the monthly ceiling. The ceiling comes first. The pot is not a second account to spend freely. That is why it has to be in the notebook, with a balance and an exit rule, so that it is never an improvised decision.

The exit rule is the most important part, and you write it down before you need it. For example: the pot is only used in Strong Buy weeks, and never for more than half of its balance at once. Illustrative example, of course. And there is another rule that is never broken: the pot is never used to buy what has risen sharply. If the market is euphoric, the pot stays still. When you use the pot, record the week, the asset and the reason on the same Friday ritual sheet, so that the saved money always has a history you can account for.

A common confusion: rebalancing is not an attempt to guess the top or the bottom. When you sell a little of what has risen to get back to the split, you are not saying the price is going to fall. You are keeping the risk you chose. And when you buy what has fallen to restore the split, you are not saying it will rise. You are respecting the plan. Rebalancing also has costs: platform fees, possible taxes, which we will look at at the end of the programme, and the work of recording everything. So do not rebalance every week, and always confirm the conditions on your authorised platform.

Five-minute exercise. Write your rebalancing rule in the notebook: calendar or band, and the value. Then create your pot: one line with the starting balance, which can perfectly well be zero. Write the exit rule in one sentence, and keep the notebook. You do not have to rebalance today. You have to know, before you need to, what you would do. That is what separates a plan from a wish. If you do not know which to choose, start with the calendar, because it is the easiest to keep in a busy week. Notice that you are not choosing the best rule in the world. You are choosing one you are going to respect.
