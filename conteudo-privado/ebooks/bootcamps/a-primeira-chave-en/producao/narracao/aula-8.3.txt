In thirty days you will not master crypto. But you can leave with a solid foundation, and that is already far more than most people who start in a rush.

Let's design a thirty-day plan, split into four weeks. Week one: security. You turn on app-based authentication, you create a password manager, you write your seed phrase on paper, and you confirm the platforms you are going to use. Week two: you choose a regulated platform, complete identity verification, understand the fees, and learn the difference between a market order and a limit order. Week three: you start DCA with a small amount and record everything. Week four: you review the numbers, the rules and what you felt. Each week has one main task and one secondary task, so you don't get stuck in the detail. And one week per phase, without skipping any. If a week goes badly, repeat it before you move on. There is no prize for haste.

In the first week, your minimum checklist is this. A single strong password, kept in a password manager. Two-factor authentication through an app, not by SMS. Your seed phrase written by hand, in two separate places, never photographed, never sent, never saved in an email or a note on your phone. Addresses verified and saved in your bookmarks. No links from messages, ever. And turn off the price notifications that make you look at the screen every minute. You don't need to be perfect. You need to be consistent. If you remember this by heart, you are already ahead of many people who start with money and without this foundation.

So that you don't get lost in the words, go to the glossary in the e-book, which has thirty terms explained in plain language. Seed phrase, DCA, halving, liquidation, dominance, airdrop, capital gain, and so on. For example, capital gain, liquidation and custody are terms you will come across in almost every conversation. Write down in your notebook the words you don't understand and look them up before you decide. A term you don't understand is always a signal to stop. What would be dangerous is deciding without understanding what you are doing. If you come across a new term in a lesson, pause the video, look it up, and only then carry on.

At the end of thirty days, do an honest review. How many times did you keep to the plan, and how many times did you drift away? What made you want to change course? How much did you invest in total, and what is worth today what you bought? Do not answer with what you wished had happened. Answer with what actually happened, even if it is not pretty. If you want, share that review with someone you trust, because it helps you stay honest. That record is worth more than any advice I could give you, because it is your own experience, written with true numbers. Read it at the end of every week, not only at the end of the month.

Before you move on, a warning that is not optional. This plan does not guarantee profits or results. It is there so that you can learn safely, with money you can afford to lose. If anyone ever promises you that a plan like this will give you a certain return, be suspicious straight away, and go back to lessons 5.1 and 5.2. A promise of certainty is almost always the first clue of a scam. And if the plan goes badly, it is not a sign that you are incompetent. It is a sign that you learned something expensive, and that also counts.

The first step of this plan is the most boring, and it is the most important: security. Before you buy anything at all, your key has to be stored safely and your account has to be protected. If you do your first week today, you are ahead of those who have not even started. In the last lesson of this bootcamp I will close with a final warning and an invitation. Until then, write your week one. And keep the security checklist in sight, because your memory, under pressure, is not reliable.
