The lesson fewest people want to see is the one that protects the most. In this method, the best decision is often not to enter.

There are three situations in which the rule is not to enter, even when a signal is on the screen. The first: the signal goes against the trend. We have already seen why. If the three DEMA say one thing and the signal says another, there is no signal for you, however good it looks. The second: there is a high-impact news event scheduled for the next few hours. The third: you have already reached your daily loss limit. That is the most important one, and we will look at it calmly in module thirteen, because that is where many accounts are lost.

Look at this illustrative example. A sell signal appears, but the three averages are aligned upwards. There is a pullback candle, and instinct says now. It isn't. The trend is on the other side, and the signal is just a reaction to a pause. When in doubt, ask yourself just one thing: am I entering with the trend? If the answer is no, the answer to the entry is also no. You don't need more arguments than that. Don't jump to conclusions from what you have just heard. Write this in your notebook before you move on.

The second situation is the calendar. Before each session, check whether there is a high-impact news event scheduled for the next few hours. If there is, the rule is simple: you do not open new positions in that window. In those hours, the price can jump for reasons no one-hour chart can read. You can keep studying, marking and recording. What you don't do is enter on top of news you cannot control, just because the chart looked clean five minutes earlier. Pay close attention to this, because it is the basis of everything that follows.

Write these three no-entry rules below your exit rules. Against the trend: I don't enter. Strong news in the window: I don't open a new position. Daily limit reached: I don't enter any more that day, even if the signal looks perfect on paper. Notice that these rules do not depend on how you feel. They depend on facts that either hold or don't. That is what makes them useful, and what makes them easy to follow when you need them most. There is no shortcut here, and that is why it is worth practising. Write this in your notebook before you move on.

Now the mission. You will mark ten past Aurum Flow signals on the one-hour chart, in a simulated account, on the MTM WebTrader or in an MTM Funded account. It is not real money and it is not about being right. It is about training your reading. For each signal, note the entry, the stop, the three targets with their percentage distances, whether the signal passed the trend and momentum filters, and whether the first target was hit, yes or no. Don't draw conclusions from ten signals. Ten signals give you practice, nothing more, and practice is what we are after.
