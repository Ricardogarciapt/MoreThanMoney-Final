If there is one thing the market teaches quickly, it is this: emotion does not wait until you are ready. So before any money, we train in a place where a mistake costs no real money.

Before we start, a warning that applies to this whole module. Educational content. The risk of loss is real. In this lesson you get to know the tools you will practise with, and the first rule is the most important of all: practice is done in a simulated account, never with rent money, never with money you need for the month's bills. I know it is tempting to skip this part and go straight to the real account. Don't skip it. Those who train calmly learn what haste hides. We will look at the three pieces of this module: the MTM WebTrader, the MTM Funded simulated account and, further on, MTM Auto. None of this is a shortcut to making money. It is a place to learn to do things well, with written rules, before any real risk.

First piece: the MTM WebTrader. It is the browser version of the MoreThanMoney execution tool. You open it on the computer, you see the asset's chart and the panel where orders are placed. You don't need to know everything at once. At this stage, what matters is one thing only: understanding where each element of the screen is before you touch any button. Whoever clicks without knowing where they are is not training, they are improvising. And improvising with emotion, on a screen with buy and sell buttons, is the simplest recipe for a decision you will regret.

Second piece: the MTM Funded simulated accounts. Notice the word on the screen: simulated. These are simulation accounts, with money that does not exist. There is no real deposit, no withdrawal, no real balance to lose. What you train here is the method: the entry, the stop, the size, the record. When emotion appears, and it will, the difference is this: the money is simulated, but the discipline you practise is real. Be careful, though. A simulated account does not reproduce the nerves of losing real money. So the test of the simulation is not winning. It is following the rules, trade by trade, even when the account looks small and the temptation looks big.

How you train, in practice. First, you open the simulated account in the WebTrader. Second, you choose a single asset, one you already know from the earlier modules. Third, and this is the step people skip: you write the rule before clicking. Why you enter, where the stop goes, what the size is, and what you will do if the price moves against you. Only then do you place the order. If you can't write the rule in two sentences, you don't enter. This sounds like bureaucracy, and it is. Bureaucracy is what protects you from emotion at the moment it appears with the most force, which is almost always when the price moves fast.

One last note, because it is easy to fool yourself. A simulated account that goes up does not prove the strategy works. A simulated account that goes down does not prove you are bad. What the simulation shows is whether you followed your own process, and that is the only indicator that matters to you at this stage. Don't look at the balance as a trophy. And if someone shows you a screenshot of a simulated account with a high balance, as proof that they make money, be suspicious. A screenshot is not a statistic, and a training account is not a real track record. When you need a number, ask for the full series of trades, not the pretty picture. Illustrative example, of course: a screenshot with a high balance can hide a hundred trades without a single written rule.
