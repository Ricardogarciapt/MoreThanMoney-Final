# Módulo 14 — Executing without emotion (≈18 min)
**Objetivo:** learn to execute in a simulated environment, with written rules, a journal and a short review routine, so that emotion does not decide the trades.  **No fim, o aluno consegue:** 1) use the MTM WebTrader and the MTM Funded simulated account to practise without real money; 2) understand what copying with MTM Auto does and what remains your responsibility; 3) record each trade in a journal and do the weekly audit in thirty minutes; 4) set stopping rules and complete the level 3 mission: thirty simulated trades with a complete journal before you think about a real account.

**Nota de produção:** record the WebTrader and simulated account screens on the day of recording, in a demo account, with no one's data. Blur any balance, name or address. Whenever a number appears on screen, it is an illustrative example and is said out loud.

## Aula 14.1 — MTM WebTrader and the MTM Funded simulated account (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "If there is one thing the market teaches quickly, it is this: emotion does not wait until you are ready. So before any money, we train in a place where a mistake costs no real money."

**[CENA 1]** Dark screen, with the phrase in gold: "Educational content. The risk of loss is real." Then Ruben in a medium shot, at a desk, with a closed notebook in front of him.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Before we start, a warning that applies to this whole module. Educational content. The risk of loss is real. In this lesson you get to know the tools you will practise with, and the first rule is the most important of all: practice is done in a simulated account, never with rent money, never with money you need for the month's bills. I know it is tempting to skip this part and go straight to the real account. Don't skip it. Those who train calmly learn what haste hides. We will look at the three pieces of this module: the MTM WebTrader, the MTM Funded simulated account and, further on, MTM Auto. None of this is a shortcut to making money. It is a place to learn to do things well, with written rules, before any real risk.

**[CENA 2]** MTM WebTrader screen open in the browser, with the chart in the centre and the trading panel beside it. Small caption: "illustrative screen".
**NARRAÇÃO:** First piece: the MTM WebTrader. It is the browser version of the MoreThanMoney execution tool. You open it on the computer, you see the asset's chart and the panel where orders are placed. You don't need to know everything at once. At this stage, what matters is one thing only: understanding where each element of the screen is before you touch any button. Whoever clicks without knowing where they are is not training, they are improvising. And improvising with emotion, on a screen with buy and sell buttons, is the simplest recipe for a decision you will regret.

**[CENA 3]** Screen with the label "MTM Funded simulated account" and, beside it, a large tag: "SIMULATED".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Second piece: the MTM Funded simulated accounts. Notice the word on the screen: simulated. These are simulation accounts, with money that does not exist. There is no real deposit, no withdrawal, no real balance to lose. What you train here is the method: the entry, the stop, the size, the record. When emotion appears, and it will, the difference is this: the money is simulated, but the discipline you practise is real. Be careful, though. A simulated account does not reproduce the nerves of losing real money. So the test of the simulation is not winning. It is following the rules, trade by trade, even when the account looks small and the temptation looks big.

**[CENA 4]** Animated list on screen: "1 · open the simulated account · 2 · choose one asset from your plan · 3 · write the rule before clicking".
**NARRAÇÃO:** How you train, in practice. First, you open the simulated account in the WebTrader. Second, you choose a single asset, one you already know from the earlier modules. Third, and this is the step people skip: you write the rule before clicking. Why you enter, where the stop goes, what the size is, and what you will do if the price moves against you. Only then do you place the order. If you can't write the rule in two sentences, you don't enter. This sounds like bureaucracy, and it is. Bureaucracy is what protects you from emotion at the moment it appears with the most force, which is almost always when the price moves fast.

**[CENA 5]** Ruben with the notebook open. On screen, the phrase: "Simulation trains the method. It proves nothing."
**NARRAÇÃO:** One last note, because it is easy to fool yourself. A simulated account that goes up does not prove the strategy works. A simulated account that goes down does not prove you are bad. What the simulation shows is whether you followed your own process, and that is the only indicator that matters to you at this stage. Don't look at the balance as a trophy. And if someone shows you a screenshot of a simulated account with a high balance, as proof that they make money, be suspicious. A screenshot is not a statistic, and a training account is not a real track record. When you need a number, ask for the full series of trades, not the pretty picture. Illustrative example, of course: a screenshot with a high balance can hide a hundred trades without a single written rule.

## Aula 14.2 — MTM Auto: copying with rules and limits (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "Copying looks like the solution for those who don't have time. But there is one question almost nobody asks before switching it on: who answers for what happens afterwards?"

**[CENA 1]** MTM Auto screen with the list of available strategies. Highlighted: the expression "rules and limits".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Third piece of this module: MTM Auto. It is MoreThanMoney's tool for copying strategies, and the keyword is this: with rules and limits. In other words, it is not a button that copies everything blindly. Before you switch on a strategy, you have to see which rules it follows and which limits the tool applies. This is very different from copying someone who turned up in a message group, without knowing anything about what that person does. Here there are rules in plain view, and that is already half the way to an informed decision. But only half. The other half is yours.

**[CENA 2]** Screen split in two: on the left, "strategy rules"; on the right, "tool limits". Arrows connecting the two columns.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Before you switch on any strategy, read three things. First, what it does: which markets it works in, with what kind of entries and how often. Second, the limits that apply on your side of the copy. Third, what happens when things go wrong. If you cannot say, in your own words, what the strategy does and what happens in the worst case, don't switch it on. And notice something that seems counterintuitive: a strategy that has been rising for a long time does not, for that reason, carry less risk. It may even carry more, because confidence grows just before a fall.

**[CENA 3]** Ruben in a close-up, with the phrase on screen: "Copying is not delegating responsibility."
**NARRAÇÃO:** Now the part that matters most. Copying does not take away your responsibility. When you switch on a copy, you remain the owner of the decision to have switched it on. The tool executes, but whoever chose the strategy, whoever decided how much they are willing to risk, and whoever decides to stop, is you. This holds even when the strategy belongs to someone you trust, and even when the tool is the house's own. The tool executes what is written. It does not know how much of your money is rent money, it does not know you have a bill to pay on Friday, and it does not know that you are tired and eager to win back a loss. Only you know those things, and only you can take them into account.

**[CENA 4]** Calendar with three weeks, an arrow going down. Caption: "illustrative case".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Illustrative case. Inês, thirty-five, switched on a copy because she did not have time to follow the markets. For a few weeks it seemed perfect. Then a drop came, and she had not defined how much she was willing to lose. She put in more money to recover and, before she knew it, she was no longer copying a strategy. She was trying to win back a loss. The tool did exactly what was written. The mistake was not writing down her own limit. If Inês had defined, before switching it on, how much she could lose and what she would do when she reached that amount, the story would have been different. Illustrative example, of course.

**[CENA 5]** Checklist on screen: "1 · I know what the strategy does · 2 · I know the limits · 3 · I know how much I can lose · 4 · I know when I stop".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Before copying, four questions, written in your notebook. Do I know what the strategy does? Do I know the limits that apply? Do I know how much I can lose, in euros, without it affecting my life? And do I know when I stop, and what I do that day? If any answer is no, the copy stays off. The practical exercise of this lesson is done in the simulated account: you switch on MTM Auto in a simulated account, observe for a week and write down, each day, what you felt. Copying is not the end of the process. It is just one more piece of it.

**Texto no ecrã:** MTM Auto · rules and limits · copying does not remove responsibility · simulated first
**Check question:** A copy loses money in one week. Whose decision was it to switch it on? Answer: yours. The tool executes; the decision to switch on, the size and the stop are yours.

## Aula 14.3 — The trading journal (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "If it isn't written down, it didn't happen. That sounds exaggerated, until we notice how many decisions we make from memory, and how often we recount them wrongly."

**[CENA 1]** Notebook open at the journal page, with the fields appearing one by one. Caption: "template in the course notebook".
**NARRAÇÃO:** This lesson is about the trading journal. It is not a pretty dossier. It is a page where you write each trade before, during and after. The full template comes in the student notebook, but the idea is simple: a trade that is not in the journal, for learning purposes, did not exist. Because memory is generous with wins and cruel with mistakes. A week later, you remember having been right. You don't remember having entered outside the rule, or having moved the stop in a hurry. The journal exists to end that automatic selection our brain makes without asking permission.

**[CENA 2]** Two animated columns: on the left, "before": date · asset · reason · rule · size · stop · targets. On the right, "after": result · rule followed? · what I felt.
**NARRAÇÃO:** Let's go through the fields. Before entering, you record the date, the asset and the reason for the entry, written in one sentence, and which rule you are applying. You record the size of the position and how much you lose if the stop is hit, always in euros, never only in percentage. And you record the stop and the targets. Before you touch the button, the journal already has everything. After closing, you add the result, whether the rule was followed, and one line about what you felt. Illustrative example: "I entered because the price returned to the average; I lose twenty euros if the stop is hit; I followed the rule; I felt rushed at ten in the morning."

**[CENA 3]** Ruben pointing at the column "rule followed? yes or no", highlighted in gold.
**NARRAÇÃO:** The most important column is the one almost nobody fills in: was the rule followed, yes or no. Because the goal at this stage is not to win. It is to execute the process you wrote. A losing trade with the rule followed is a good training trade. A winning trade outside the rule is a warning, not a compliment. If you mark "no" and the trade made a profit, write exactly that, without excuses. That "no" will be the most useful piece of information you get in a whole week, because it shows you where emotion got involved, even when it went well. Always write it, even when the answer bothers you.

**[CENA 4]** Journal sheet with three filled-in lines. Caption: "illustrative case".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Illustrative case: Rui, twenty-nine, made three trades in one week. On the first, he followed the rule and lost the expected amount, without drama. On the second, he moved the stop halfway through, because he didn't want to watch the loss grow. On the third, he entered with no written reason, just because the chart seemed to be moving fast. When he did the review, the last two lines had "no" in the rule column. That was when he understood the problem. It was not the market. It was him stepping out of the process, twice, in silence. The journal showed that in two minutes. Memory would have hidden it all.

**[CENA 5]** Ruben with the notebook closed. On-screen text: "Write before. Close after. Never rewrite."
**NARRAÇÃO:** Two rules so you don't spoil the journal. First: write before you enter. A journal written afterwards is a story, and stories adjust themselves. Second: never rewrite an old line. If you get a word wrong, you cross it out and write over it, with the date of the correction. If you erase what you did badly, you are erasing the only thing that can teach you. The journal is not there to judge you. It is there to show yourself what you really did, dated today and without makeup. Take this out of the notebook and keep it in view, so you can reread it before each session.

**Texto no ecrã:** before · during · after · size in euros · rule followed? yes or no · never rewrite
**Check question:** A winning trade outside the rule: how do you record it? Answer: with "no" in the rule column and an honest note, without counting it as a success.
**Fecho / CTA da aula:** "Next lesson: the weekly audit and the thirty-minute routine." On-screen button: "View lesson 14.4".

## Aula 14.4 — Weekly audit and the 30-minute routine (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "Thirty minutes a week, with the journal open in front of you. Without this routine, the journal is just a notebook full of lines."

**[CENA 1]** Animated clock with thirty minutes split into four blocks: "prepare 5 · review trades 10 · audit rules 10 · decide 5".
**NARRAÇÃO:** This is the routine. Thirty minutes, once a week, always on the same day and at the same time. It breaks down like this: five minutes to prepare, ten to review the week's trades, ten to audit the rules, and five to decide on one single thing to change. Notice the time. Thirty minutes is short on purpose. A review that lasts three hours turns into anxiety, and a five-minute review turns into an excuse. Thirty minutes is enough to be honest, and not so long that you want to change everything at once. Choose the day and time in your calendar, as if it were a booked meeting.

**[CENA 2]** Journal screen with the week filled in. Arrows pointing at the column "rule followed".
**NARRAÇÃO:** First, review the trades. You open the week's journal and read each line from top to bottom, without editing anything. You look for three things: how many trades you made, how many followed the rule, and at what moments you stepped out of the process. Don't look at the result in euros as a judge. The result is a data point, and a single data point deceives. A week of profit with three rules broken is a bad week. A week of loss with all rules followed is a well-trained week. Read with a pencil in hand and mark the lines that make you uncomfortable, without correcting them.

**[CENA 3]** List on screen: "audit · size within the plan? · stop moved? · entry with no written reason? · rule by rule, with proof from the journal".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Then, the audit. For each rule written in your plan, you answer yes or no, with evidence taken from the journal. Was the size within what you defined? Was the stop moved? Was there any entry with no written reason? Any "no" is a point of attention, and every point of attention has a cause. The most frequent cause is almost never technical. It is emotional: boredom, haste, the urge to recover, fear of missing the rise. Give it a name, write it beside the line, and see whether it repeats the following week. It is not a punishment. It is a cool-headed reading, done with the same calm you would bring to reviewing any other process.

**[CENA 4]** Ruben writing on a sheet, with the phrase on screen: "One thing to change this week."
**NARRAÇÃO:** Now, the hardest step: deciding one thing. Only one. Ask yourself which mistake repeated the most, and write a concrete rule to avoid it. For example: from now on, no order goes in without a written reason. And here is the golden rule of this lesson: don't change the rules halfway through the week. Change them on review day, after you have seen them written down. Changing everything on the back of the last trade is the quickest way to never know what works and what doesn't. If the change is too big, shrink it until it fits in a single sentence.

**[CENA 5]** Final screen with the routine in five lines: "fixed day and time · journal open · audit · one change · review log".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Last step, and this one is not to be skipped: log the review itself. Date, number of trades, number of rules followed, and the change you chose. That way, three months from now, you can see whether your discipline went up or down, and not just what you feel about it. Thirty minutes a week, without fail, is worth more than a study marathon that never repeats. And if one week you can't, do the short version: ten minutes, just the audit. Something small and done beats something big and postponed. Without a log, the review did not happen.

**Texto no ecrã:** thirty minutes · journal open · rules before the result · one change per week · don't change rules halfway
**Check question:** In a week with a profit, what does the review look at first? Answer: the rules followed, not the result in euros.
**Fecho / CTA da aula:** "Next lesson: stopping rules, psychology and the level 3 mission." On-screen button: "View lesson 14.5".

## Aula 14.5 — Stopping rules, psychology and the level 3 mission (≈3–4 min)
**Gancho (0:00–0:20):** "The worst decision of a market day is almost never the first. It is the fourth, made to win back the third."

**[CENA 1]** Screen with "stopping rules" in large letters and, below, three items: "consecutive losses · maximum daily loss · cut-off time".
**NARRAÇÃO:** We have reached the last lesson of the module, and the part that decides whether the others are worth anything. Stopping rules. These are the rules that say when you stop, before you start arguing with yourself. There can be three: a maximum number of consecutive losses, a maximum loss amount for the day, and a time after which you do not enter. Write them with the numbers you choose. There is no magic number, not even mine. There is your number, written beforehand and kept. And if the number is hit, the day ends there, even if the chart is doing exactly what you wanted.

**[CENA 2]** Three spiral arrows on screen: "I lose" → "I increase the size" → "I lose more".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Now psychology, without beating around the bush. There is a classic spiral. You lose a trade and feel the urge to win it back. You increase the size of the next one, to recover faster. You lose again, because the bigger position amplifies the loss, and you enter a third to plug the hole. Each step seems reasonable at the time. Together they are a disaster. Break this spiral with a simple rule: never increase the size after a loss. The size is the one in the plan, always, the one you wrote before any emotion showed up. Notice that the spiral does not need greed. The urge to plug a hole is enough.

**[CENA 3]** Ruben taking a deep breath, in silence. On screen, in large letters: "24-hour pause".
**NARRAÇÃO:** And when you feel emotion taking over, use the pause. If you have hit a stopping rule, or if you feel you can't think clearly, close the WebTrader and don't come back that day. If you really want to look at a market again, wait twenty-four hours and come back with the journal in front of you. The pause is not weakness. It is the cheapest rule there is, and the one that saves the most money for whoever uses it. If someone tells you that you have to decide now, because the opportunity won't come back, you already know what that is. Close the conversation, breathe, and go back to your plan.

**[CENA 4]** Notebook with a crossed-out page and an underlined sentence: "I want to win it back". Caption: "illustrative case".
**NARRAÇÃO:** Illustrative case: Tiago, forty, had no stopping rule. One morning, he lost two trades in a row and decided he would recover before lunch. It was not the first time he had thought that way, but it was the first time the journal was in front of him. He wrote, without meaning to: "I want to win it back". That sentence gave everything away. He closed the screen. At the Friday review, he realised the losses that morning had the same origin: haste. Illustrative example, of course, because what matters is the pattern, not the value of each trade. Think about this during your next week, before you need it.

**[CENA 5]** Mission screen, with a box to tick and the title "Level 3 mission". Checklist: "stopping rules written · 30 simulated trades · complete journal · weekly review done".
**NARRAÇÃO:** The level three mission, which is optional, and which is the most demanding in this course: thirty trades in a simulated account, with a complete journal, before you think about a real account. Thirty. Not thirty days of luck. Thirty trades with the rules written beforehand, with the size from the plan, and with the weekly review done. Each trade with its journal line filled in, including the rule column. If at the end you have not followed the rules consistently, the mission is not complete, and you start again more calmly. Nobody needs level three to be an investor with DCA. And think about a real account only after this, and only with money you are prepared to lose completely.

**Texto no ecrã:** stopping rules · never increase after a loss · 24-hour pause · mission: 30 simulated trades + complete journal
**Check question:** You lost two trades in a row and feel like increasing the next one. What do you do? Answer: the size stays as in the plan; if you hit a stopping rule, you stop for the day.
